# Latent world models for control and generation — what transfers to a tiny single-frame spatial-memory model

Scope note for the report writer: this file covers the control/generation world-model lineage (PlaNet, Dreamer v1–v3, TD-MPC/TD-MPC2, MuZero/EfficientZero, SPR, Genie/Genie 2, Cosmos, Navigation World Models), the predictive-coding / HTM / Thousand Brains lineage, the hippocampal-model lineage (successor representation, TEM, grid-cell networks), and the latent-map/neural-map lineage. I-JEPA / V-JEPA proper are assumed to be covered by another researcher; seq-JEPA appears here only because it is the one JEPA variant built around action-conditioned prediction. All mechanism claims below are taken from the papers' own text (arXiv HTML, ar5iv, or pdftotext of the PDF). Where a claim rests only on a search-result snippet rather than on a page I read, it is marked "(snippet only)".

Problem being assessed against (from the assignment): a from-scratch CNN of roughly 3 MB (hard cap 4 MiB exported ONNX, ESP32-P4-class target), one function `estimate_position(frame) -> (lat, lon, confidence)`, memorising one city (Berlin) from 1 m/px orthophotos in 128x128 crops; trained on three surveys of the same ground, scored on a fourth survey never seen; previous champion memorised one survey's pixel intensities rather than the ground's structure; deployment is strictly single-frame, but at training time the harness knows the exact position and heading of every crop, so translation/rotation between crops and "change of survey" are available as actions.

## Key question 1 — For each family: what is predicted, in what space, with what loss; how actions are conditioned; what prevents latent collapse; what role reconstruction plays and what the papers say about dropping it

### Takeaway
Two camps. The Dreamer lineage predicts its own stochastic latent with a KL between an observation-conditioned posterior and an action-conditioned prior, and keeps pixel reconstruction because its own ablation shows removing it destroys performance (DreamerV2 gamer-median 1.64 → 0.04). The MuZero / TD-MPC / SPR / EfficientZero camp has no decoder at all: the latent is trained only by action-conditioned latent prediction plus task heads, and collapse is prevented by a combination of (a) a stop-gradient or EMA target, (b) a normalised (cosine or SimNorm) latent, and (c) task heads (value/reward or, for this project, position) that make a constant latent useless. The second camp's evidence on visual distractors (DreamerPro, TPC) is the direct analogue of this project's "memorised one survey's pixels" failure.

### Cited Findings

**PlaNet (Hafner et al. 2019) — the RSSM**
- State is split into a deterministic path and a stochastic path. Rationale, quoted from the figure caption: "(a) Transitions in a recurrent neural network are purely deterministic. This prevents the model from capturing multiple futures and makes it easy for the planner to exploit inaccuracies. (b) Transitions in a state-space model are purely stochastic. This makes it difficult to remember information over multiple time steps. (c) We split the state into stochastic and deterministic parts" — [PlaNet PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04551)
- "the purely stochastic transitions make it difficult for the transition model to reliably remember information for multiple time steps. In theory, this model could learn to set the variance to zero for some state components, but the optimization procedure may not find this solution. This motivates including a deterministic sequence of activation vectors" — [PlaNet PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04551)
- Loss: "reconstruction terms for the observations and KL-divergence regularizers for the approximate posteriors"; a noted limitation is that the prior transition "is only trained via the KL-divergence regularizers for one-step predictions", which motivates latent overshooting (training multi-step latent priors against posteriors). The final agent did not need overshooting: "we used latent overshooting and an additional fixed global prior, but we found this to not be necessary." — [PlaNet PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04551)
- Sizes: "a GRU with 200 units as deterministic path", "Distributions in latent space are 30-dimensional diagonal Gaussians", convolutional encoder/decoder "from Ha & Schmidhuber (2018)". Experiments "show that both stochastic and deterministic paths in the transition model are crucial for successful planning." — [PlaNet PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04551)

**Dreamer v1 (Hafner et al. 2020) — reconstruction vs. contrastive vs. reward-only**
- Same RSSM as PlaNet (30-dim diagonal Gaussian latents; Ha & Schmidhuber conv encoder/decoder); actor-critic trained in imagination with horizon H=15. — [Dreamer ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.01603)
- Representation-learning comparison: "Pixel reconstruction performs best for the majority of tasks"; a contrastive (NCE) variant "solves about half of the tasks"; "predicting rewards alone was not sufficient in our experiments." The paper notes predicting pixels "can require high model capacity" and that the contrastive bound "is limited" in how much information it extracts. — [Dreamer ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.01603)

**DreamerV2 (Hafner et al. 2021) — discrete latents, KL balancing, the reconstruction ablation**
- Latent: "32 categoricals" with "32 classes each", trained with straight-through gradients, replacing DreamerV1's Gaussians. — [DreamerV2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02193)
- KL balancing: the prior is trained faster than the posterior, "α = 0.8" ("KL balancing encourages learning an accurate prior"). — [DreamerV2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02193)
- Ablation Table 2 (Atari, 200M frames, gamer-normalised median): DreamerV2 1.64; No Reward Gradients 1.68; No Discrete Latents 1.08; No KL Balancing 0.84; **No Image Gradients 0.04**. Text: "Stopping the image gradients increases performance on 3 tasks, decreases performance on 51 tasks, and is tied on 1 task. The world model of DreamerV2 thus heavily relies on the learning signal provided by the high-dimensional images." and "In contrast to MuZero, DreamerV2 thus learns general representations of the environment state from image information alone." — [DreamerV2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02193)

**DreamerV3 (Hafner et al. 2023, v2 text) — the current form of the Dreamer loss**
- RSSM: sequence model h_t = f(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}); encoder z_t ~ q(z_t | h_t, x_t); dynamics predictor ẑ_t ~ p(ẑ_t | h_t); reward, continue and decoder heads conditioned on (h_t, z_t). — [DreamerV3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104v2)
- Loss weights β_pred = 1, β_dyn = 1, β_rep = 0.1. L_dyn = max(1, KL[sg(q) ‖ p]) trains the sequence model to predict the next representation; L_rep = max(1, KL[q ‖ sg(p)]) makes representations more predictable; the two "differ in their stop-gradient placement and loss scale." Free bits clip both KL terms below 1 nat "to avoid a degenerate solution where the dynamics are trivial to predict." — [DreamerV3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104v2)
- Latents are "sampled from a vector of softmax distributions" with straight-through gradients; 1% uniform mixing ("unimix") makes it "impossible for them to become deterministic and thus ensuring well-behaved KL losses." — [DreamerV3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104v2)
- symlog(x) = sign(x) ln(|x|+1) for decoder/reward targets; twohot categorical regression over symexp-spaced bins for reward and critic. Six model sizes "ranging from 12M to 400M parameters." — [DreamerV3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104v2)
- On reconstruction: Dreamer "rests predominantly on the unsupervised objective of its world model"; the text reports no experiment that removes reconstruction entirely. — [DreamerV3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104v2)

**DreamerPro (Deng et al. 2022) — Dreamer without reconstruction, under visual distractors**
- Replaces "the reconstruction-based representation learning in Dreamer with reconstruction-free methods": observations are clustered onto K trainable prototypes learned "from the recurrent states of the world model"; the model state predicts the prototype assignment of an augmented view (SwAV-style), plus a temporal term. — [DreamerPro HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14565v1)
- Trivial solutions are avoided by Sinkhorn-Knopp applied "to each augmented batch separately, to encourage uniform cluster assignment within each augmented batch", a momentum (EMA) target encoder with η = 0.05, K = B×T prototypes, prototypes frozen for the first 10K updates; non-contrastive methods are preferred because they "can be applied to small batch sizes." — [DreamerPro HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14565v1)
- Results (final return, 3 seeds). Standard DMC: Cheetah Run Dreamer 840±74 / DreamerPro 897±8; Walker Run 737±26 / 784±28. **Natural-background DMC: Cheetah Run Dreamer 30±2, TPC 444±35, DreamerPro 349±61; Walker Run Dreamer 35±4, TPC 161±6, DreamerPro 394±33.** Dreamer "fails completely" with natural backgrounds. — [DreamerPro HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14565v1)

**TD-MPC (2022) and TD-MPC2 (2024) — decoder-free, consistency-trained latent dynamics**
- TD-MPC: "a learned task-oriented latent dynamics model for local trajectory optimization over a short horizon" plus "a learned terminal value function", learned jointly by TD learning; DMControl and Meta-World. — [TD-MPC abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04955)
- TD-MPC2 components: encoder z = h(s,e); latent dynamics z' = d(z,a,e); reward R(z,a,e); Q ensemble (5 by default); policy prior p(z,e). — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)
- Objective: joint-embedding (consistency) loss, squared L2 between d's predicted latent and sg(h(s'_t)); reward cross-entropy; value cross-entropy against TD target; summed over horizon H with weight λ^t. Table 8 coefficients: 20 (consistency), 0.1 (reward), 0.1 (value), λ = 0.5. Reward and value are "discrete distributions over 101 bins in a log-transformed space." — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)
- SimNorm: latent split into groups of V dims, each passed through softmax(z/τ), defaults V = 8, τ = 1; "biases the representation towards sparsity without enforcing hard constraints", mitigates exploding gradients and keeps the latent norm small; "SimNorm is essential to the training stability of TD-MPC2." — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)
- No EMA target encoder: the consistency target "uses a stop-gradient on the same encoder h"; the only EMA is on the Q ensemble (momentum 0.99). — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)
- Explicitly "an implicit, control-centric world model" that learns "without decoding observations"; reconstruction-based models are called tempting but hard to get right over long horizons, and "predicting raw observations does not necessarily lead to effective control" (citing Lambert et al. 2020). — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)
- Size table (Table 9): 1M (encoder dim 256, latent 128, 2 Q-functions); 5M default (latent 512); 19M; 48M; 317M (latent 1376). Pixel encoder "a shallow (4 layers) convolutional encoder" at 64×64 with random-shift augmentation. — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)

**MuZero (Schrittwieser et al. 2020) — value-equivalent model, no reconstruction**
- Three functions: representation s^0 = h(o_1..o_t); dynamics r^k, s^k = g(s^{k-1}, a^k); prediction p^k, v^k = f(s^k). Trained "end-to-end, with the sole objective of accurately estimating these three important quantities." — [MuZero PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265)
- "There is no direct constraint or requirement for the hidden state to capture all information necessary to reconstruct the original observation, drastically reducing the amount of information the model has to maintain and predict; nor is there any requirement for the hidden state to match the unknown, true state of the environment; nor any other constraints on the semantics of state." and "this internal state s^k has no semantics of environment state attached to it". The dynamics function "is represented deterministically; the extension to stochastic transitions is left for future work." — [MuZero PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265)
- Unrolled K = 5 hypothetical steps; loss of each head scaled by 1/K; hidden state min-max scaled to the same [0,1] range as the action planes; Atari network "16 residual blocks" of 256 planes. — [MuZero PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265)

**EfficientZero (Ye et al. 2021) — what MuZero was missing: a latent-consistency term**
- Motivation: "None of the reward, value and policy losses can provide enough training signals to learn the environment model"; "the reward is only a scalar signal and in many scenarios, the reward will be sparse." Fix: the dynamics output ŝ_{t+1} "should be the same as s_{t+1}" (the encoding of the real next observation), trained with "the SimSiam loss function": shared projector, predictor MLPs (3-layer P1, 2-layer P2, 512 hidden / 1024 out), stop-gradient on the target branch, negative cosine similarity. — [EfficientZero HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.00210v2)
- Ablation (Table 3, 26 Atari games, 100k steps): full model normalised mean 1.943 / median 1.090; **without consistency 0.881 / 0.340** — the largest drop of the three components (value prefix removal → 1.482/0.552; off-policy correction removal → 1.475/0.836). — [EfficientZero HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.00210v2)
- Network: representation net is a stride-2 conv with 32 planes, residual blocks and downsampling to a 6×6 map with 64 planes; dynamics net concatenates state and action into 65 planes, one stride-2 conv and one residual block; "reduces MuZero's residual blocks from 16 to 1." — [EfficientZero HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.00210v2)

**SPR (Schwarzer et al. 2021) — action-conditioned latent prediction as an auxiliary loss on a small CNN**
- Transition model: "we apply a convolutional network directly to the 64 × 7 × 7 spatial output of the convolutional encoder… two 64-channel convolutional layers with 3 × 3 filters… We append a one-hot vector representing the action taken to each location in the input to the first convolutional layer"; "maximum prediction depth of K = 5". — [SPR PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929)
- Target encoder is an EMA of the online encoder, θ_m ← τθ_m + (1−τ)θ_o; "We use τ = 0.99 when augmentation is disabled and τ = 0 when enabled"; total loss L_RL + λ L_SPR with λ = 2; prediction loss is a sum of cosine similarities between predicted and target projections; predictor q is a linear layer. — [SPR PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929)
- Collapse: "Using a quadratic loss causes collapse… a variant of SPR that minimizes un-normalized L2 loss (Quadratic SPR in Table 2)… performs only slightly better than random. This is consistent with results from Gelada et al. (2019), who find that DeepMDP's representations are prone to collapse, and use an auxiliary reconstruction objective to prevent this." Also "Projections are critical". SPR "does not use negative samples". τ = 0 (no EMA at all) is the best setting with augmentation, which the authors call "interesting, since the related method BYOL reports very poor representation learning" at τ = 0; "in no case does SPR fail to train." — [SPR PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929)
- Atari 100k median human-normalised score 0.415 (55% over prior SOTA). — [SPR abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05929)

**Genie (Bruce et al. 2024) — latent-action discovery with a tiny discrete codebook**
- Three parts: video tokenizer (ST-ViViT VQ-VAE, patch size 4, "codebook with embedding size 32 and 1024 unique codes", 200M params), latent action model (LAM, 300M), MaskGIT dynamics model (10.1B; input tokens masked at a rate sampled in [0.5, 1]; cross-entropy on tokens; latent actions are added as embeddings). — [Genie HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391v1)
- LAM: encoder sees past frames plus the next frame and outputs latent actions; decoder sees only past frames and latent actions and must predict the next frame; trained with a VQ-VAE objective; codebook kept small, |A| = 8, "to permit human playability and further enforce controllability"; because the decoder only has history and the action, the action "should encode the most meaningful changes between the past and the future." "the entire LAM is discarded at inference time and replaced with actions from the user." — [Genie HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391v1)
- No explicit discussion of collapse/trivial actions beyond the small codebook; "We found a benefit from increasing the number of codes (i.e. number of actions), at the cost of reduced playability." Scaling study: dynamics models "ranging from 40M to 2.7B parameters" with consistently lower loss at each size; a small CoinRun case study on "a single mid range TPU/GPU" yields "consistent playable latent actions" with no quantitative metric. — [Genie HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391v1)

**Genie 2 (DeepMind blog, Dec 2024)**
- "autoregressive latent diffusion model": a video autoencoder compresses frames to latents, a causally-masked transformer dynamics model predicts subsequent latent frames, conditioned on the current (real keyboard/mouse) action and past latents; classifier-free guidance "to improve action controllability"; a distilled version runs in real time at lower quality. Worlds remain consistent "for up to a minute, with most examples lasting 10–20 seconds." No parameter count or dataset size is given. — [Genie 2 blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-2-a-large-scale-foundation-world-model/)

**NVIDIA Cosmos (2025)**
- Tokenizer: continuous (latent dim 16) and discrete (FSQ, levels (8,8,8,5,5,5) ≈ 64,000 vocabulary) variants; image compression 8×8 and 16×16; video compression 4×8×8, 8×8×8, 8×16×16 (T×H×W); causal. Two families: latent diffusion transformers (DiT, EDM formulation) at 7B/14B and Llama3-style autoregressive token predictors at 4B/5B/12B/13B. Conditioning via text (T5), past frames (Video2World), and post-training for camera-pose-conditioned navigation, manipulation and driving. Claims "3D consistent videos with accurate physics" (authors' claim). No localisation or mapping evaluation in the portion read. — [Cosmos HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03575v1)

**Navigation World Models (Bar et al. 2024)**
- Predicts the next latent s_{τ+1} ~ F(· | s_τ, a_τ) where s_i = enc(x_i) is "encoded via a pretrained VAE" (Stable Diffusion's); CDiT (conditional diffusion transformer) with cross-attention to past frames; 1B parameters, 4-frame context. — [NWM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03572v1)
- Actions: translation u ∈ R² (forward/back, left/right), yaw φ, time shift k ("time shifts of up to ±16 seconds"); each scalar mapped to sine-cosine features → 2-layer MLP, summed into one vector ξ that drives AdaLN scale/shift. For unlabelled video (Ego4D) only the time shift is used as conditioning. — [NWM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03572v1)
- Results: standalone planning on RECON ATE 1.13 / RPE 0.35 vs NoMaD 1.93 / 0.52; ranking 32 NoMaD samples gives ATE 1.78 / RPE 0.48; in unfamiliar environments the model "breaks faster and expectedly hallucinates paths" and shows mode collapse toward training data. "The paper does not evaluate robustness to appearance or lighting changes" and makes no localisation claims; robot pose is used only to derive action labels. — [NWM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03572v1)

**Predictive coding (Rao & Ballard lineage, Millidge et al. review)**
- Each layer predicts the layer below; errors go up, predictions go down; separate value units μ and error units ε; precisions (inverse variances) "multiplicatively modulate the importance of the prediction errors." Under Gaussian assumptions free energy is a sum of precision-weighted squared prediction errors (plus log-variance terms); neural activity follows dμ/dt = −∂F/∂μ; weight updates are "entirely local", a Hebbian product of presynaptic error and postsynaptic value. "predictive coding with fixed predictions and identity precisions forms a scheme which can converge to the exact gradients computed by the backpropagation of error algorithm." The review notes "the relatively few instances of deep convolutional predictive coding networks in the literature so far." — [Millidge et al. review HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.12979v4)

**HTM / Thousand Brains (Hawkins et al.)**
- HTM sequence memory (2016): neurons with proximal/basal/apical zones; basal dendritic segments are pattern detectors whose recognition depolarises (predicts) the cell; simulation uses 2048 minicolumns × 32 cells with ~2% active columns ("40 active columns out of 2048"); which cell in a column fires is set by context (depolarised cells fire first and inhibit neighbours); learning is Hebbian at the dendritic-segment level "without the need for a global objective function"; capacity example "approximately 320,000 transitions"; "At up to about 40% cell death there was minimal impact"; unanticipated input makes "all the cells in a column… become active" (bursting = prediction error / anomaly). — [HTM 2016, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2016.00023/full)
- Thousand Brains (2019): grid-cell-like neurons proposed "in every column of the neocortex"; each column pairs sensory features with an object-centric location; location is updated by path integration from a motor efference copy; "The number of locations that can be represented increases exponentially with the number of modules" (one module ~20 locations, 10 modules ~20^10); columns vote via long-range L2/3 connections; a model succeeds "if it predicts the next input from the current state and an anticipated movement." The paper reports no simulation results. — [Thousand Brains, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2018.00121/full)

### Inferences
- The three decoder-free families converge on one recipe: predict the next latent from (current latent, action); make the target a stop-gradient or EMA copy; normalise the latent (cosine in SPR/EfficientZero, SimNorm in TD-MPC2, min-max in MuZero); add task heads. DreamerV3's free bits and unimix are the Dreamer-side answer to the same degeneracy. Nothing in the decoder-free recipe needs a sequence at inference: only the encoder (plus the position head) is deployed.
- SPR's finding that an un-normalised L2 latent-prediction loss collapses to near-random, while cosine with projection heads does not, is the single most actionable "what prevents collapse" result for a small CNN, because it was obtained on exactly that kind of network (64×7×7 conv features) and without negatives.
- The DreamerV2 "no image gradients → 0.04" result is specific to RL where reward is the only other signal; it is not evidence that this project needs reconstruction. MuZero/EfficientZero/TD-MPC2 show that a dense supervised head (here: position) plus latent consistency is sufficient. DreamerPro's natural-background numbers (Dreamer 30 vs 349–444 without reconstruction) are the closest published analogue to "memorised one survey's pixel intensities": reconstruction forces the latent to carry nuisance appearance.
- Genie's LAM solves a problem this project does not have (unknown actions). With known translation/rotation/survey-change, the LAM's bottleneck is unnecessary; its 8-code result is nonetheless a useful data point that a very small discrete "action" vocabulary is enough to carry inter-frame change.
- Cosmos, Genie 2 and NWM are generators of pixels or VAE latents at 1B–14B parameters; their architectural content (DiT, MaskGIT, FSQ tokenizers) has no counterpart that fits in 4 MiB, and their action conditioning (AdaLN from sin-cos action features) is the only piece that is size-independent.

### Gaps
- DreamerV3's per-size parameter counts and its latent shape are in the supplementary material, which I did not read; the HTML text does not list them.
- Genie gives no ablation table on the number of latent actions and no quantitative small-scale numbers; the CoinRun case study is qualitative.
- Cosmos's evaluation section (3D consistency, physics alignment) and any trajectory-conditioning details were in the unread part of a 311k-character page.
- I did not find an explicit statement in any Dreamer paper that reconstruction can be dropped; the evidence that it can comes from the follow-ups (DreamerPro, TPC) and from the decoder-free camp.

## Key question 2 — Which of these ideas have been applied to localisation, mapping, navigation or place memory, and what do they report about robustness to appearance change?

### Takeaway
World models have been applied to navigation (NWM, Memory Maze, Neural Map, CMP, EgoMap, ESM), but every one of them localises by integrating a sequence with known or estimated egomotion, and none evaluates cross-season or cross-sensor appearance change. The only robustness evidence in the lineage is indirect: reconstruction-based latents fail under natural-video backgrounds and prediction-based latents do not. The hippocampal models (TEM, successor representation) are the ones that explicitly factor "where" from "what it looks like", which is the property this project needs.

### Cited Findings

**Memory Maze (Pasukonis, Lillicrap, Hafner 2022) — does an RSSM state encode position and map layout?**
- Online RL at 100M steps (Table 3; Memory 9×9 / 11×11 / 13×13 / 15×15): Oracle 34.8 / 58.0 / 74.5 / 87.7; Human 26.4 / 44.3 / 55.5 / 67.7; IMPALA 23.4 / 28.2 / 27.5 / 17.7; Dreamer 28.2 / 20.4 / 11.6 / 4.1; Dreamer (TBTT) 33.2 / 31.4 / 21.4 / 5.6; Dreamer (TBTT + object-location probe loss, i.e. with position supervision at training) 33.9 / 44.5 / 37.3 / 14.7. — [Memory Maze PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13383)
- Offline probing (Table 4; wall-layout accuracy 9×9 / 15×15, object-location MSE 9×9 / 15×15): RSSM 89.0 / 80.0, 8.3 / 39.5; RSSM (TBTT) 94.0 / 80.5, 6.2 / 33.8; a no-memory baseline already reaches 80.8% on 9×9 Walls "because a relatively small number of grid cells vary between" mazes. The probe is a 4-layer 1024-unit MLP (a linear probe "is not powerful enough"), gradients do not flow into the model, and the agent coordinate is given as an extra input to the probe. — [Memory Maze PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13383)
- Authors' interpretation: "the world model of Dreamer tries to remember as much information about the environment as possible, which may limit the memory horizon", whereas model-free IMPALA's "encoder and RNN… only process and retain task-relevant information and ignore the rest." Adding the object-location probe loss as an auxiliary training loss "shows a significant improvement, indicating that the task performance is indeed bottlenecked by memory." — [Memory Maze PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13383)

**Neural Map (Parisotto & Salakhutdinov 2017)**
- Memory is "a C×H×W feature block"; the base model "requires some oracle to provide the current (x,y) position of the agent" and the paper notes localisation "is far from solved"; writes go only to the current cell (GRU-gated); reads are a global conv read plus attention-based context read. Ego-centric variant: "the map is counter-transformed by" the inverse of the agent's velocity so the agent stays centred (translation only). Results: 2D Goal-Search success Neural Map 91.7%, GRU Neural Map 96.4%, MemNN 83.4%, LSTM 57.4%; Doom unseen maps LSTM+Neural Map 66.6% vs LSTM 52.4%. — [Neural Map ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.08360)

**Cognitive Mapping and Planning (Gupta et al. 2017)**
- Egocentric top-down belief updated as f_t = U(W(f_{t−1}, e_t), f'_t): W is a differentiable bilinear warp by the known egomotion e_t, U fuses the new view's projected features with per-cell confidence; the mapper "is free to write into memory whatever information is most useful for the planner" (no map supervision; DAGGER imitation of shortest paths); unseen-building success 62.5% (RGB) / 78.3% (depth) vs 53.0% / 71.8% for baselines; real TurtleBot RGB 68%. "A central limitations in our work is the assumption of perfect odometry." No appearance-robustness test. — [CMP ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.03920)

**EgoMap and Egocentric Spatial Memory (snippet only)**
- EgoMap argues Neural Map "suffers from poor localization" because an object seen from two directions lands in two map cells, and uses a projective transform into one allocentric map. — [EgoMap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02286) (snippet only)
- Egocentric Spatial Memory Network (Zhang et al. 2018): recurrent update of a top-down global map from egocentric views with pose estimation and loop closure, tested on random walks in 3D mazes against SLAM baselines; the place/grid/boundary-cell analogies are "descriptive analogies, not firm biological equivalents." — [ESM](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~qzhao/publications/pdf/egocentric2018.pdf) (snippet only)

**Navigation World Models** — see Q1: pose used only to label actions; "no localisation claims"; "does not evaluate robustness to appearance or lighting changes." — [NWM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03572v1)

**Hippocampal models that separate structure from appearance**
- Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine: a structural code g is path-integrated "from the previous g, conditional on the current action/relation"; a sensory code x; hippocampal p "inferred as a conjunction between sensory input (x)" and g, stored in Hebbian weights and retrieved by an attractor; objective is to "predict the next sensory observation with high probability" (ELBO, trained by BPTT). "top-layer TEM representations generalise, retaining their properties across different environments" even though "the sensory objects have a different distribution", while place-like p cells remap. — [TEM bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/770495v2.full)
- Successor representation (Stachenfeld et al.): "M(s, s′) is equal to the discounted expected number of times the agent visits state s′ starting from s"; place cells encode rows/columns of M; learned by TD with a "successor prediction error" (η = 0.1, M initialised to identity); "grid fields reflect a low-dimensional eigendecomposition of the SR"; fields "skew opposite the direction of motion" and "warp around obstacles"; the model does not claim invariance to sensory details. — [Predictive map bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/097170v4.full)

**Classical cross-season localisation (not world models; for context)**
- Churchill & Newman store multiple "experiences" of a place to cover cyclic appearance change; a teach-and-repeat feature study found seasonal/lighting robustness mattered more than scale/rotation invariance; one comparison concluded "change removal is more robust and less data-intensive" than GAN season prediction; a 2021 UAV paper localises against an orthophoto map under seasonal change "trained only on satellite images from different times of year." — [Season-invariant GNSS-denied UAV localization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.01967) and [Long-term autonomy survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05196) (snippets only)
- Self-supervised UAV geo-localisation (CAEVL, WACV 2026) trains on satellite reference images only, with an autoencoder whose latent is regularised by a perceptual loss plus non-contrastive regularisation and augmentation that simulates the satellite→UAV shift; "not framed as a world model." — [CAEVL](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02737v1) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- Every world-model navigation system that localises does so with a recurrent or map state and known/estimated egomotion; the Memory Maze probe numbers (object MSE 33.8 on 15×15 even with TBTT) show the RSSM is a weak spatial memory even when it is allowed a sequence. There is no published world-model localiser that works from a single frame, so this lineage offers mechanisms for training, not a deployable architecture.
- The Memory Maze auxiliary-probe result (position supervision during training lifts 11×11 score from 31.4 to 44.5) is a reminder that this project's main advantage, exact position labels at training time, is the signal those agents lacked.
- TEM is the clearest statement of the factorisation this project needs: a code that path-integrates under actions and transfers across environments with different sensory content, conjoined with a sensory code that is allowed to remap. "Different survey of the same ground" is precisely "same structure, different sensory distribution."
- No world-model paper measures the cross-survey/cross-season condition. The robustness evidence available (DreamerPro/TPC) concerns task-irrelevant background video, which is a stronger distractor than survey change but the same failure mode: a latent trained to explain pixels explains the wrong thing.

### Gaps
- I found no paper probing a Dreamer-style latent for absolute position in an outdoor or aerial setting; the only position probing is Memory Maze (indoor mazes, agent coordinate given to the probe).
- I found no world-model or latent-prediction paper evaluated on cross-season or cross-source aerial imagery; the search for "world model + geo-localisation" returned only contrastive/autoencoder UAV work.
- EgoMap and ESM were read from search snippets only; their numbers are not recorded here.

## Key question 3 — Evidence at very small scale (few-million-parameter CNN encoders) and on distilling a world model's encoder into a tiny deployable network

### Takeaway
The decoder-free recipe has been demonstrated at 1M–5M parameters (TD-MPC2's 1M model, SPR's Atari CNN, EfficientZero's one-residual-block dynamics, predictive-coding grid-cell networks with 256–2048 latents); the Dreamer lineage bottoms out around 12M. I found no paper that distils a world model's encoder into a microcontroller-class network.

### Cited Findings
- TD-MPC2's size table includes a 1M-parameter configuration (encoder dim 256, latent 128, 2 Q-functions) and a 5M default; the pixel encoder is "a shallow (4 layers) convolutional encoder" at 64×64. — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)
- SPR's transition model is two 64-channel 3×3 convolutions on the encoder's 64×7×7 output, action appended as one-hot planes, built on the data-efficient Rainbow encoder; it trains stably at τ = 0 (no EMA) with augmentation. — [SPR PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929)
- EfficientZero reduced the dynamics network "from 16 to 1" residual block and runs on a 6×6×64 hidden state; consistency loss is what made the small model work (median 0.340 → 1.090). — [EfficientZero HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.00210v2)
- DreamerV3's smallest configuration is 12M parameters. — [DreamerV3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104v2)
- Predictive-coding grid-cell networks: static PCN with 512 place-cell inputs and 256 latents; temporal tPCN with 2048 latents (grids still appear with 256 or 512 latents, path integration worsens with fewer); learning is local Hebbian; tPCN with one inference iteration matches a 1-step truncated-BPTT RNN, saturating around 20 iterations. — [Tang, Barron, Bogacz HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01022v2)
- Genie's reproducible small case study trains a LAM and dynamics model on CoinRun on "a single mid range TPU/GPU" and reports only that it "generates consistent playable latent actions." — [Genie HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391v1)
- Genie 2: "A distilled version runs in real time with lower output quality" (no sizes). — [Genie 2 blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-2-a-large-scale-foundation-world-model/)
- Distillation of latent dynamics exists only in the RL sense: a privileged-teacher world model regularising a sparse-reward student's latent dynamics (driving), and SimDist freezing a sim-trained "state encoder, reward model, and value function" while adapting only the dynamics on real hardware. Neither targets a size reduction. — [arXiv 2512.04279](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.04279v2) and [SimDist 2603.15759](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15759v1) (snippets only)
- Microcontroller constraints are generic: SpArSe (NeurIPS 2019) frames MCU CNN design as feature maps fitting RAM and parameters fitting flash, with models "up to 7.4× smaller." — [SpArSe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12107) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- The auxiliary-loss machinery (projector, predictor, transition head) is training-only in SPR/EfficientZero/TD-MPC2, so its parameters do not count against the 4 MiB export; the ESP32 budget constrains only the encoder and position head. This is why the decoder-free camp, not the Dreamer camp, is the relevant precedent at this scale.
- The absence of any world-model→MCU distillation paper means there is no published number on how much of a latent-prediction encoder's benefit survives int8 quantisation; this would have to be measured in the loop.

### Gaps
- No source found on distilling a world-model encoder into a few-hundred-KB network; the "distillation" hits are RL teacher-student setups, not compression.
- No published study varies the encoder below ~1M parameters with a latent-consistency loss.

## Key question 4 — Which mechanism could be re-expressed as a training-time auxiliary objective for a single-frame localiser, and what do equivariant / predictive-place-cell / grid-cell papers report?

### Takeaway
The mechanism that transfers cleanly is action-conditioned latent prediction with a stop-gradient/EMA target and a normalised latent (SPR / TD-MPC2 / EfficientZero), where the "actions" are the harness's known translations, rotations and survey changes between crops; seq-JEPA shows that this kind of action conditioning alone makes an encoder equivariant to the action (rotation R² 0.29 → 0.71) without any equivariance-specific loss, and TEM/grid-cell models show that path-integrable location codes separate from sensory content when the objective is to predict the next observation from state plus movement. None of this requires a sequence at inference.

### Cited Findings

**Action-conditioned latent prediction as an auxiliary loss (the template)**
- SPR: total loss L_RL + λ·L_SPR, λ = 2; cosine similarity between a predicted future projection and a target projection from an EMA/stop-gradient encoder; K = 5 steps; the transition network is a small conv net with the action broadcast as one-hot planes; a quadratic (un-normalised) loss collapses. — [SPR PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929)
- TD-MPC2: consistency weight 20 vs 0.1 for reward and value; stop-gradient on the same encoder; SimNorm groups of 8 with softmax "essential to the training stability." — [TD-MPC2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)
- EfficientZero: SimSiam consistency between the dynamics-predicted hidden state and the encoding of the real next observation; removing it is the largest ablation drop (median 1.090 → 0.340). — [EfficientZero HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.00210v2)
- MuZero's framing: the hidden state is "free to represent state in whatever way is relevant to predicting current and future values and policies" — value equivalence rather than observation equivalence. — [MuZero PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265)

**Equivariance from action conditioning, with known transformation parameters**
- seq-JEPA: a backbone f encodes views; each representation is concatenated with its action embedding and aggregated by a 3-layer transformer; a 2-layer MLP predictor takes the aggregate plus the next action and predicts the next view's representation; cosine loss against an EMA target with stop-gradient "to avoid representational collapse"; "No additional loss terms or equivariance-specific predictors are used during training." Actions are relative 3D rotations (3DIEBench), crop/colour/blur parameters (CIFAR100, Tiny ImageNet) or relative patch positions "simulating eye movements" (STL-10). 3DIEBench: seq-JEPA 87.41% top-1 and rotation R² 0.71; SIE 77.49% / 0.58; VICReg 80.48% / 0.20; **seq-JEPA without action conditioning 86.05% / 0.29**. All models use ResNet-18. — [seq-JEPA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03176v3)
- SIE (Garrido et al. 2023): splits the representation into invariant and equivariant parts and uses a hypernetwork predictor conditioned on the transformation parameters so equivariant representations are learned "without collapsing to invariance"; 3DIEBench has 55 classes and >2.5M renders with full control of the transformations. — [SIE abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10283)
- Plannable MDP homomorphisms (van der Pol et al. 2020): "a contrastive loss function that enforces action equivariance on the learned representations" (encoder + action-conditioned latent transition); when the loss is zero the map is a homomorphism of a deterministic MDP and latent planning lifts back to the original MDP; the snippet adds that negative sampling is used because earlier next-state predictors "do not account for the possibility of latent space collapse." — [MDP homomorphisms abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11963)

**Predictive place/grid codes**
- Successor representation: place-cell-like codes are a prediction of future occupancy learned by a TD rule; grid cells are its low-dimensional eigendecomposition; fields skew backward along travel and warp around barriers. — [Predictive map bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/097170v4.full)
- TEM: a structural code g that path-integrates under actions and "generalise[s]… across different environments", conjoined with a sensory code; trained only to predict the next sensory observation. — [TEM bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/770495v2.full)
- Grid cells by predictive coding (Tang, Barron, Bogacz 2024): hexagonal latents emerge in a two-layer predictive-coding net from place-cell inputs with an L1 sparsity term and ReLU non-negativity (removing either, "we also could not obtain hexagonal grid cells"); in the velocity-driven tPCN non-negativity "is necessary"; place inputs must be the difference-of-softmaxed-Gaussian encoding (plain Gaussian place cells gave non-hexagonal latents); "path integration is not a sufficient condition for the emergence of grid cells." — [Tang et al. HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01022v2)
- A 2024 bioRxiv preprint reports a feed-forward network without path integration learns grid representations on par with the path-integrating RNN. — [Self-supervised grid cells without path integration](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.30.596577.full.pdf) (snippet only)
- Thousand Brains: location is a set of grid-cell modules whose combined capacity grows "exponentially with the number of modules" (10 modules ≈ 20^10 locations); the learning criterion is predicting "the next input from the current state and an anticipated movement." — [Thousand Brains, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2018.00121/full)
- HTM: an unanticipated input makes every cell in a minicolumn fire (bursting), which the authors present as the signature of a failed prediction. — [HTM 2016, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2016.00023/full)

**Dropping reconstruction under appearance nuisance** — DreamerPro/TPC natural-background numbers (Dreamer 30 vs 349/444 on Cheetah Run) and Sinkhorn-equipartition + EMA as the anti-collapse mechanism. — [DreamerPro HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14565v1)

### Inferences (how each mechanism would be written as a training-time objective for this project)
- **Translation as an action (the SPR/TD-MPC2 form).** For a training crop A at (x, y, heading) and the crop B one lattice step away (24 m, or any off-lattice offset the harness can cut), add L_trans = 1 − cos(q(d(z_A, a_AB)), sg(p(z_B))) with d a small conv/MLP transition head that takes the offset as sin-cos features (NWM's encoding), p a projector, q a predictor; target from the same encoder with stop-gradient (TD-MPC2) or an EMA copy (SPR; τ = 0 was best with augmentation). Weight it on the order of the position loss (SPR used λ = 2 against the RL loss; TD-MPC2 weighted consistency 200× the task heads, but with cross-entropy heads). Heads d, p, q are discarded at export, so the 4 MiB cap is untouched. seq-JEPA's ablation says this alone should make the latent carry the offset (equivariance), which is exactly the "structure, not intensities" property.
- **Survey change as an action (the TEM form).** Treat "same ground, survey k → survey l" as an action with its own embedding: L_survey = 1 − cos(q(d(z_{A,k}, a_{k→l})), sg(p(z_{A,l}))). If the survey embedding is made tiny (Genie's point about an 8-code vocabulary) or dropped altogether (plain BYOL-style invariance z_{A,k} ≈ z_{A,l}), the latent is pushed to be survey-invariant; TEM's structural/sensory split suggests letting a small sub-vector be survey-equivariant and the rest invariant (SIE's split) rather than forcing full invariance, which would be the honest way to represent "this place looks like this in summer and like that in winter". This is the objective most directly aimed at the known failure; it is only available because three surveys of the same ground exist at training time.
- **Rotation as an action.** Same form with a_AB = Δheading; seq-JEPA's rotation R² result (0.29 → 0.71 from action conditioning alone) is the closest published evidence that the latent will encode heading. The harness already found that decode-time rotation averaging does not fix rural look-alikes, so this should be a training-time regulariser, not an inference step.
- **Grid-cell-style position target.** The Thousand Brains / SR / TEM evidence motivates predicting position as a multi-module periodic code (sin/cos of x and y at several wavelengths, i.e. the capacity-grows-exponentially-with-modules argument) in addition to or instead of the current cell/offset head; under such a code a translation action is a phase shift, so the transition head d becomes close to linear and the auxiliary loss becomes very cheap. Tang et al.'s result that non-negativity and sparsity are needed for hexagonal latents is a hint that a ReLU/softmax (SimNorm-like) latent is the right place for such a code to live. This is an inference from the papers, not something any of them tested on aerial imagery.
- **Predictive-coding error units and HTM bursting** reduce, for a feed-forward CNN trained by backprop, to the residual of the latent-prediction loss (the review itself shows PC with fixed predictions converges to backprop gradients). Their one distinct idea — a large prediction error as an abstention signal — cannot be used at inference here because a single frame has nothing to compare a prediction against. The project's existing belief-pooling confidence is already the closest single-frame analogue.
- **Why collapse is not a risk here even without EMA.** MuZero-style task heads (the position loss) make a constant latent useless; SPR shows cosine + projector are enough without negatives on a small CNN; SimNorm or an L2-normalised latent is cheap insurance. The known collapse hazard is the opposite one: an un-normalised L2 latent loss (SPR's "Quadratic SPR" near-random result; DeepMDP needing reconstruction).

### What does NOT transfer (explicit)
- Anything that needs a sequence or recurrent state at inference: the RSSM deterministic/stochastic split (PlaNet/Dreamer), KL balancing/free bits (they regularise a posterior-vs-prior pair that a single-frame encoder does not have), imagination rollouts, MCTS over a learned model (MuZero), MPPI planning (TD-MPC), Neural Map / CMP / EgoMap map states, Memory Maze-style memory. — [PlaNet PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04551), [MuZero PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265), [Neural Map ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.08360)
- Pixel or VAE-latent generation (Genie, Genie 2, Cosmos, NWM): 1B–14B parameters, diffusion/MaskGIT samplers, and they reconstruct appearance, which is the thing this project must stop memorising. — [NWM HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03572v1), [Cosmos HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03575v1)
- Genie's latent action model: solves unknown actions; here actions are known exactly. — [Genie HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391v1)
- Dreamer's reconstruction term as a necessity: its necessity is demonstrated only against reward-only supervision in RL, and the decoder-free camp plus DreamerPro show it is replaceable when a dense target or a prototype/consistency target exists. — [DreamerV2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02193), [DreamerPro HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14565v1)
- Predictive coding's local learning rules and HTM's sparse-distributed, non-gradient learning: no advantage for a backprop-trained CNN exported to ONNX; the review notes few deep convolutional PC networks exist. — [Millidge review HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.12979v4)

### Gaps
- No paper applies action-conditioned latent prediction with known metric offsets to aerial crops, so there is no published number for how much it helps cross-survey localisation; the closest quantitative evidence is seq-JEPA's rotation R² and DreamerPro's natural-background returns.
- The correct loss weight relative to the position loss is unknown for this setting; SPR (λ = 2) and TD-MPC2 (20 vs 0.1) disagree by two orders of magnitude and are both tied to RL losses of different scale.
- Whether a survey-equivariant sub-vector (SIE-style split) beats plain survey invariance is untested anywhere I found.
- Grid-cell-style periodic position codes have not been evaluated as regression targets in any localisation paper I found; the suggestion above is an inference from the neuroscience models.
