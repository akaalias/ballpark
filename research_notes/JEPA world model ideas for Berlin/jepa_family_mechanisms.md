# JEPA-family world-model mechanisms (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2, DINO-WM, data2vec, BYOL/SimSiam, VICReg/Barlow Twins, LeJEPA) — concrete mechanisms, small-scale evidence, and remote-sensing applications

Scope note for the report writer: everything below was read from the arXiv full-text HTML or the official repo unless marked otherwise. The LeCun 2022 position paper could not be fetched (OpenReview browser check); its content is reported only as a gap. Numbers are verbatim from the sources; where a source is internally inconsistent I say so.

Project framing used throughout the Inferences: a from-scratch CNN of at most 4 MiB exported fp32 ONNX (~1M parameters), 128x128 crops at 1 m/px, trained on three surveys of the same Berlin ground, scored on a fourth survey it never sees; the known failure is memorising one survey's pixel intensities (a +20 brightness shift broke the previous champion).

## Key question 1 — For each method: exact objective, target-encoder trick, masking, predictor, anti-collapse mechanism, loss

### Takeaway
Every method in the family predicts a *representation* (never pixels) of a hidden or differently-viewed part of the input from a visible part, using a target encoder that receives no gradient (EMA copy, or stop-gradient on a shared encoder), a small predictor that breaks the symmetry, and either that asymmetry alone (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, BYOL, SimSiam, data2vec) or an explicit statistical regulariser on the embedding (VICReg, Barlow Twins, LeJEPA SIGReg) to avoid collapse. Losses are plain L2, L1 or smooth-L1 in latent space on layer-normalised targets; nothing in the family needs negatives, a pixel decoder, or a large batch.

### Cited Findings

**I-JEPA (Assran et al., ICCV 2023)**
- Objective: from one context block, predict target-encoder representations of several target blocks of the same image; loss is the average squared L2 distance, `(1/M) Σ_{i=1..M} Σ_{j∈B_i} ‖ŝ_yj − s_yj‖²₂`, over M target blocks. Predictor φ and context encoder θ are trained by gradient descent; the target encoder θ̄ is an exponential moving average of the context encoder. — [I-JEPA, arXiv 2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)
- Masking: M = 4 possibly overlapping target blocks, each with scale in (0.15, 0.2) of the image and aspect ratio in (0.75, 1.5); one context block of scale (0.85, 1.0), unit aspect ratio; any context region overlapping a target block is removed. Target blocks are taken by masking the target encoder's *output*, so the target encoder always sees the whole image. — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)
- Predictor: a narrow ViT, embedding width 384, same number of heads as the backbone; depth 6 for ViT-B/16, 12 for ViT-L/16 and ViT-H, 16 for ViT-G/16. Masked positions are filled with one shared learnable mask token plus a positional embedding indicating the target location. No [cls] token; the target encoder's output is average-pooled for evaluation. — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)
- EMA momentum: starts at 0.996 and increases linearly to 1.0 over pretraining. — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)
- Official ViT-H/14 300-epoch config: `num_enc_masks: 1`, `num_pred_masks: 4`, `enc_mask_scale: [0.85, 1.0]`, `pred_mask_scale: [0.15, 0.2]`, `aspect_ratio: [0.75, 1.5]`, `min_keep: 10`, `allow_overlap: false`, `patch_size: 14`, `ema: [0.996, 1.0]`, `lr: 0.001`, `start_lr: 0.0002`, `final_lr: 1e-6`, `warmup: 40`, `epochs: 300`, `weight_decay: 0.04 → 0.4`, `pred_depth: 12`, `pred_emb_dim: 384`, `batch_size: 128` (per GPU), `crop_size: 224`, `color_jitter_strength: 0.0`, `use_horizontal_flip: false`. — [facebookresearch/ijepa config](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ijepa/blob/main/configs/in1k_vith14_ep300.yaml)
- Masking ablation (ViT-B/16, 300 ep, linear eval on 1% ImageNet): multi-block 54.2, rasterized 15.5, single block 20.2, random 17.6. Predictor-depth ablation (ViT-L/16, 500 ep): depth 6 → 64.0, depth 12 → 66.9. — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)
- Collapse: the paper states an asymmetric architecture between the x- and y-encoders is used to avoid representation collapse and that the EMA target encoder is essential for training joint-embedding architectures with ViTs. — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)
- Target normalisation: standard I-JEPA applies LayerNorm (with learnable affine parameters) to the teacher's output features before they serve as targets; LN "forces all features to have identical L2 norms". — [Elucidating the Role of Feature Normalization in IJEPA, arXiv 2508.02829](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02829v1)

**V-JEPA (Bardes et al., 2024)**
- Objective (Eq. 1): predictor P_φ maps context-encoder output E_θ(x), conditioned on target positions Δ_y, to a prediction of the target representation; loss is the L1 distance to `sg(Ē_θ(y))` where sg is stop-gradient and Ē_θ is an EMA of the encoder; Appendix B averages the L1 distance over the M masked patches. The authors chose L1 because they "found [it] to be more stable"; no L2 comparison is reported. — [V-JEPA, arXiv 2404.08471](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- Masking: multi-block masks applied across the full temporal extent of the clip (tube masking). Short-range mask = union of 8 target blocks covering 15% of each frame; long-range mask = union of 2 blocks covering 70% of each frame; aspect ratios in (0.75, 1.5); average masking ratio ≈ 90%. — [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- Predictor: narrow ViT, 12 blocks, embedding dimension 384; inputs are encoder outputs plus learnable mask tokens with positional embeddings. — [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- EMA momentum: 0.998 increasing linearly to 1.0; schedules stretched 25% beyond the 90K-iteration run. — [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- Masking ablation (Table 4, SSv2): multi-block best at 67.4; random-tube at ratio 0.9 worst at 46.4; causal multi-block 49.8. — [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- Feature vs pixel targets (Table 1, frozen eval): feature prediction 73.7 vs 68.6 on K400, 66.2 vs 66.0 on SSv2, 74.8 vs 73.3 on IN1K; after full fine-tuning the pixel model is nearly as good (85.6 vs 85.4 on K400). — [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- Collapse argument (Sec. 3.1): with an optimal predictor, the L1 objective's expected gradient for the encoder equals the gradient of the median absolute deviation of the target given the encoder output, so the encoder must retain information about the target; the EMA target keeps the predictor near optimal. — [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- Attentive probe: one learnable query token cross-attends over frozen features, residual-added to the query, two-layer MLP with one GeLU, LayerNorm, linear classifier; improves K400 by ~17 points and SSv2 by 16.1 points over average pooling. — [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)

**V-JEPA 2 (Meta, 2025) — pretraining half**
- Same objective (mask denoising in representation space, L1 loss against an EMA target encoder with stop-gradient), with 2×16×16 tubelets and the same multiblock masking as V-JEPA; 3D-RoPE replaces absolute sincos embeddings (feature dim split into temporal/height/width segments, each rotated separately), which the paper says stabilises the largest models; warmup-constant-decay LR; 252K iterations; teacher EMA and weight decay fixed rather than ramped; progressive resolution (most training on 16-frame 256×256, short cooldown at higher duration/resolution, reported 8.4× GPU-time reduction); encoder ViT-g (~1B); predictor is a ViT-small-sized network shared across encoder sizes. — [V-JEPA 2, arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)

**DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2024)**
- Encoder frozen (pre-trained DINOv2 patch embeddings `z_t ∈ R^{N×E}`); extract gives feature shape (14×14, 384) for inputs resized to 196×196 (Table 12 lists 224 — internal inconsistency). — [DINO-WM, arXiv 2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)
- Dynamics: `p_θ(z_{t+1} | z_{t−H:t}, a_{t−H:t})`, H = 3 for Maze/Reacher/Push-T, H = 1 for Wall/Rope/Granular; loss is squared error between predicted latent and the encoding of the next observation, on patch features, with teacher forcing on segments of length H+1; AdamW, predictor LR 5e-5, 100 epochs, batch size 32. — [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)
- Predictor: ViT with tokenisation removed (decoder-only transformer), depth 6, 16 heads, MLP dim 2048, ~19M params; frame-level causal attention. Ablation at h=3: 0.92 success with the causal mask vs 0.08 without. — [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)
- Decoder: transposed-conv stack trained separately with reconstruction loss `‖q_θ(z_t) − o_t‖²`, used for visualisation only; backpropagating it into the predictor *hurt* planning (0.80 vs 0.92 on Push-T). — [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)

**data2vec (Baevski et al., 2022)**
- Student encodes a masked input and predicts the teacher's representations of the *full* input, only at masked time-steps. Targets = average of the top-K FFN-block outputs (taken before the last residual), each normalised first (parameter-less LayerNorm for vision/NLP, instance norm for speech); K = 6 for vision, 8 speech, 10 NLP. — [data2vec, arXiv 2202.03555](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03555v3)
- Teacher EMA: vision uses a constant τ = 0.9998 (ViT-L later 0.9999 in a second stage); speech/NLP ramp τ₀ = 0.999 → τ_e = 0.9999 over 30K / 100K updates. — [data2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03555v3)
- Vision masking: BEiT-style block-wise masking, each block ≥ 16 patches with random aspect ratio, 60% of patches masked (BEiT uses 40%). Loss: Smooth-L1 with β = 2 for vision (β = 4 NLP; plain L2 for speech). — [data2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03555v3)
- Ablations: averaging K layers beats predicting only the top layer; larger teacher context helps and the full unmasked input is best; FFN outputs are the best target, self-attention outputs give an unusable model (100% WER); the paper argues target normalisation prevents collapse but has no dedicated normalisation ablation. — [data2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03555v3)

**BYOL (Grill et al., 2020) / SimSiam (Chen & He, 2021)**
- SimSiam loss: negative cosine `D(p, z) = −(p/‖p‖₂)·(z/‖z‖₂)`, symmetrised `L = ½D(p₁, sg(z₂)) + ½D(p₂, sg(z₁))`; no momentum encoder, both branches share weights. Encoder = backbone + 3-layer projection MLP (2048-d, BN on every FC incl. output, no ReLU on output); predictor = 2-layer MLP 2048→512→2048 with BN on the hidden layer only (bottleneck "more robust"). — [SimSiam, arXiv 2011.10566](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)
- Removing stop-gradient: 67.7% → 0.1% (chance), loss hits −1 immediately. Removing the predictor (identity): 0.1% collapse. Fixed random predictor: 1.5% — not collapse, training fails to converge. Constant (undecayed) predictor LR: 68.1 vs 67.7. — [SimSiam](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)
- Batch-size table (ImageNet linear, 100 ep): 64 → 66.1, 128 → 67.3, 256 → 68.1, 512 → 68.1, 1024 → 68.0, 2048 → 67.9, 4096 → 64.0; SGD, LR 0.05×BS/256. — [SimSiam](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)
- Collapse diagnostic: per-channel std of the ℓ2-normalised output ≈ 1/√d when healthy, 0 when collapsed. CIFAR-10 with a CIFAR-variant ResNet-18, 800 epochs: 91.8% linear eval (SimCLR 91.1). — [SimSiam](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)
- BYOL reproduction momentum coefficients 0.99 / 0.99 / 0.996 for 100 / 200–400 / 800 epochs; BYOL reports 0.3% without its momentum encoder. — [SimSiam](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)

**VICReg (Bardes, Ponce, LeCun, 2022)**
- Invariance `s(Z, Z') = (1/n) Σ_i ‖z_i − z'_i‖²₂` (no normalisation). Variance `v(Z) = (1/d) Σ_j max(0, γ − √(Var(z^j) + ε))`, γ = 1, ε = 1e-4 — std rather than variance because the gradient of Var vanishes near the mean. Covariance `C(Z) = 1/(n−1) Σ_i (z_i − z̄)(z_i − z̄)ᵀ`, `c(Z) = (1/d) Σ_{i≠j} [C(Z)]²_{ij}`. Total `ℓ = λ s(Z,Z') + μ [v(Z)+v(Z')] + ν [c(Z)+c(Z')]`, λ = μ = 25, ν = 1 on ImageNet. — [VICReg, arXiv 2105.04906](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04906v3)
- Variance and covariance are applied to *each branch separately*; the paper names "norm collapse" (prevented by the variance term) and "informational collapse" (prevented by the covariance term). Expander = 2 × (Linear-BN-ReLU) + Linear, all 8192 wide; batch 2048; LARS, 1000 ep. Branches need not share weights or architecture (separate weights cost 2.1% top-1). Removing standardisation from the expander's hidden layers costs 1.2%. Pseudocode: `sim = mse(z_a, z_b)`; `std_loss = mean(relu(1 − sqrt(var + 1e-4)))` over both branches; covariance from `(zᵀz)/(N−1)` after centering, sum of squared off-diagonals / D. — [VICReg](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04906v3)

**Barlow Twins (Zbontar et al., 2021)**
- `L = Σ_i (1 − C_ii)² + λ Σ_i Σ_{j≠i} C_ij²`, λ = 5e-3, C = cross-correlation over the batch of the two branches' batch-normalised (mean-centred, std-divided) embeddings; projector 3 × 8192; "works well with batches as small as 256" (SimCLR drops ~44 points at 256); no predictor, EMA or stop-gradient — adding stop-gradient gives 70.5, a predictor 70.2, both 61.3 vs 71.4 baseline. — [Barlow Twins, arXiv 2103.03230](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.03230)

**LeJEPA (Balestriero & LeCun, Nov 2025)**
- Loss `(1 − λ) · prediction + λ · SIGReg`, recommended λ = 0.05. Prediction: DINO-style 2 global + up to 8 views (local views 96×96); each view's embedding is pulled toward the mean embedding of the global views with squared L2. SIGReg projects embeddings on random directions (1024 recommended, code default 256) and applies the Epps–Pulley characteristic-function test against a standard Gaussian (trapezoid rule, 17 points on [−5, 5]). No EMA, no stop-gradient, no predictor; optional SWA on the encoder for ViTs. — [LeJEPA, arXiv 2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3)
- Stability: ViT-L on IN-1k steady across batch sizes 128–1024 (72.20–74.72% linear); "no collapse on any tested setting"; training loss has ~85% Spearman correlation with linear-probe accuracy (~99% after a λ^α rescale, α = 0.4), presented as label-free model selection. — [LeJEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3)

### Inferences
- The family divides cleanly into two anti-collapse strategies: (a) architectural asymmetry (EMA teacher + predictor + stop-gradient: I-JEPA, V-JEPA, data2vec, BYOL, SimSiam) and (b) explicit embedding statistics (VICReg, Barlow Twins, SIGReg). (b) costs a few lines and no second network; (a) costs a teacher forward pass per step. Both are far below an hour per experiment on a ~1M-parameter CNN at 128×128.
- Every implementation detail the JEPA papers agree on can be transplanted to a CNN: layer-normalised teacher features as targets (I-JEPA/data2vec/V-JEPA/SCOTT), loss only on masked positions (I-JEPA, CNN-JEPA, SCOTT), EMA 0.996→1.0 linear (I-JEPA, CNN-JEPA, SCOTT, REJEPA all use exactly this), a shallow predictor (2–3 layers suffice for CNNs, Q2), and L1/smooth-L1 rather than L2 if training is unstable (V-JEPA, data2vec).
- For the Berlin task the teacher, predictor and any VICReg expander are training-time only; the exported ONNX carries just the encoder and the coordinate head, so none of this touches the 4 MiB gate.
- A minimal CNN-JEPA step that fits one training script (synthesised from I-JEPA + CNN-JEPA + VICReg, not a quoted recipe):
  ```
  x: 128x128 crop; mask M on the 8x8 (or 16x16) final feature grid: 4 target blocks, scale 0.15-0.2, AR 0.75-1.5; context = complement of targets
  with no_grad: T = LN(teacher(x))                     # full image, layer-normed per location
  S = student(x * upsample(context_mask))             # zero masked pixels at input (and zero masked activations per layer if faithful to CNN-JEPA)
  S[masked] = mask_token + pos_emb                    # one shared learnable vector
  P = predictor(S)                                    # 2-3 depthwise-separable 3x3 conv layers, BN, ReLU
  L_pred = mean over masked locations of |P - T|      # L1 (V-JEPA) or MSE (I-JEPA)
  optional: L_vic = 25*var_hinge(S_pooled) + 1*cov(S_pooled) (VICReg, per branch) if collapse is observed
  teacher <- m*teacher + (1-m)*student, m: 0.996 -> 1.0 linearly
  ```

### Gaps
- LeCun's 2022 position paper (JEPA energy formulation, latent variable z and its information-content regulariser, H-JEPA) could not be fetched: OpenReview served a browser-verification page instead of the PDF. The paper's exact equations are therefore not recorded here. From general knowledge (unverified in this session) the proposal is `E(x, y) = D(s_y, Pred(s_x, z))` with z regularised to carry minimal information and s_x, s_y regularised to carry maximal information, trained non-contrastively in the VICReg style; treat that as the writer's background, not as a cited finding.
- I-JEPA's own paper does not explain the predictor's specific role in preventing collapse; the only mechanistic argument in the family is V-JEPA's Sec. 3.1 (optimal-predictor gradient = gradient of the median absolute deviation).
- V-JEPA's full text after character 100,000 (appendix with remaining ablations) was not read.

## Key question 2 — Data efficiency and SMALL encoders: does latent prediction work for CNNs, for ViT-S/Tiny, below ~10M parameters?

### Takeaway
Latent prediction does work for CNNs (CNN-JEPA, ResNet-50, 73.3% ImageNet-100 linear) and for small ViTs on tiny datasets (SCOTT/MIM-JEPA, 13.6–22.4M params, 97% on 8K Flowers images, single RTX 3090; LeJEPA on ~50 sub-20M timm models), but no primary source reports a JEPA-style result below ~6.5M parameters, and the strongest small-model evidence is a warning: SimSiam with ResNet-18 partially collapses on ImageNet (30.0% vs 68.1% for ResNet-50) because capacity is too small relative to data complexity, while the same ResNet-18 is fine on CIFAR-10.

### Cited Findings
- CNN-JEPA adapts I-JEPA to CNNs: the student uses "sparse" convolutions implemented by zeroing each conv layer's output under the upscaled mask (no MinkowskiEngine); the teacher uses standard convs; masked student locations are replaced by one learnable mask token before the predictor; predictor = 3 depthwise-separable 3×3 conv layers (DepthwiseConv-PointwiseConv-BN-ReLU), 2 or 3 layers optimal (73.22 vs 73.26); masks are 32×32 px aligned to the 7×7 final map at 224; mixed masking (multi-block with p = 0.75 else random); random masking alone 57.64 vs 73.26; EMA 0.996 → 1.0; L2 loss only on masked locations; ResNet-50 ImageNet-100 200 ep: 73.26% linear; I-JEPA with ViT-S / ViT-B on the same setup: 42.30 / 46.36. Baselines on the same ResNet-50: SimCLR 76.20, BYOL 79.02, VICReg 74.18 linear. 13 h on 4×A100 for IN-100; no ResNet-18 or smaller results; no target normalisation described; no collapse reported. — [CNN-JEPA, arXiv 2408.07514](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07514v1)
- SCOTT + MIM-JEPA (small data): SCOTT-7/16 = 13.6M params, SCOTT-12/16 = 22.4M, SCOTT-3/16 = 6.5M; convolutional tokenizer = two blocks of sparse 7×7 stride-2 conv + ReLU + sparse MaxBlurPool, giving a 14×14 grid of 384-d tokens; sparse layers compute only where the kernel centre covers an unmasked element so masked regions are not zero-filled. MIM-JEPA: block-wise masking ratio 0.6, EMA 0.996 → 1.0 linear, 3-block predictor, Smooth-L1 only on masked patches against layer-normalised target features, AdamW batch 128, LR 5e-4, WD 0.04 → 0.4; augmentations crop/flip/colour jitter/grayscale/blur. Results: Flowers-102 (8,189 unlabeled images) ViT-12/16 from scratch 71.1 → SCOTT-12/16 MIM-JEPA 97.1 (300 ep), 97.7 (1200 ep), vs I-JEPA ViT-H 630M 93.7; Pets-37 48.3 → 86.2 / 90.7 (I-JEPA 91.7); ImageNet-100 84.9 (I-JEPA 88.7, DINOv2 89.1). Single RTX 3090. Pretraining on only the 2,040-image Flowers train split: 80.69 attentive / 66.92 linear vs 79.13 / 78.54 with no pretraining (linear probe got worse). No collapse analysis. — [SCOTT/MIM-JEPA, arXiv 2502.18056](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18056v1)
- Non-contrastive collapse vs capacity (Li, Efros, Pathak, ECCV 2022): SimSiam on ImageNet, 100 ep, batch 256 — ResNet-18 (11.7M) 30.0%, ResNet-34 (21.8M) 16.8%, ResNet-50 bottleneck (25.6M) 68.1%, ResNet-Bottleneck-26 (16.0M) 61.7%, 2×-wide Bottleneck-26 (39.6M) 62.6%, depth-50 Basic (31.9M) 17.5%: capacity "corresponds more to width than depth". ResNet-18 accuracy *peaks at 5% of ImageNet* and ResNet-34 at 10%, then falls with more data; this is invisible in the SimSiam loss. Diagnosis: partial dimensional collapse (singular-value spectrum), measurable by a rank-based metric; a linear fit of collapse metric + loss predicts probe accuracy. Fix: continual/multi-epoch hybrid training improves ResNet-18 ImageNet linear probe by up to 18 points. The paper notes SimSiam ResNet-18 matches on CIFAR-10. — [Understanding Collapse in Non-Contrastive Siamese Representation Learning, arXiv 2209.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15007)
- SimSiam CIFAR-10, CIFAR-variant ResNet-18, 800 ep: 91.8% linear. — [SimSiam](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)
- VICReg with ResNet-18 is reported only for ESC-50 audio (1,600 train clips): 78.4% vs 72.7% supervised, 75.4% Barlow Twins; the paper says the same λ/μ/ν gave "excellent results" on MNIST/CIFAR without numbers. — [VICReg](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04906v3)
- LeJEPA evaluates ~50 timm models from 8 families under 20M parameters on ImageNet-10, plus ResNet-50 on IN-100 and ResNet-34 / ConvNeXt-V2 Nano in-domain on Galaxy10 (ResNet-34 frozen 78.17 full-data vs DINOv3 ViT-S/16 transfer 71.38; ConvNeXt-V2 Nano frozen 76.52 vs DINOv2 ViT-S/16 67.62); the paper recommends ResNets and ViTs as starting points and not EfficientNet. — [LeJEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3)
- I-JEPA data efficiency: 1% ImageNet fine-tuning ViT-L/16 69.4, ViT-H/14 73.3, ViT-H/16₄₄₈ 77.3 (MAE ViT-L 67.1, ViT-H 71.5); no ViT-S results anywhere in the paper. V-JEPA: no ViT-S; label efficiency at ~29 examples/class K400: V-JEPA ViT-H 67.0% vs VideoMAEv2 37.0%. — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3); [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)
- A TU Delft thesis trains I-JEPA with a ViT-Tiny/8 backbone on Tiny-ImageNet subsets with CIFAR-10 linear probing (search snippet only; the repository refused the connection). — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_531adffb-4f25-43ba-9922-4668bd502ff1?preview=1)
- A 2026 preprint ("Bootleg") argues I-JEPA's last-layer self-distillation is less stable and proposes distilling hidden layers instead (search snippet only, not read). — [arXiv 2603.15553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15553)
- Feature-normalisation paper: replacing the LayerNorm on encoder outputs with DynTanh (`tanh(a·x)`, per-channel learnable scale, no affine) raised ViT-S I-JEPA ImageNet linear probe from 38.02% to 42.71% at 600 epochs, with the argument that LN equalises token norms and removes the "energy" signal of high-norm (foreground) tokens; its abstract's depth-RMSE claim (0.08) does not match the body (~0.011), and its stated parameter counts (236M encoder for "ViT-Small") are implausible — treat its numbers with caution. — [arXiv 2508.02829](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02829v1)

### Inferences
- Nothing in the primary literature is below 6.5M parameters; a ~1M-parameter CNN is a full order of magnitude smaller than any JEPA the papers have trained. The result most relevant to that regime is the collapse paper's finding that the capacity-to-data-complexity ratio, not absolute size, determines whether a non-contrastive objective collapses — and that a small ResNet is fine on a small, low-complexity dataset. One city at 1 m/px with three surveys is closer to "small dataset" than to ImageNet, so a tiny CNN is plausibly on the safe side, but this is unmeasured.
- The collapse paper's operational advice transfers directly: prefer width over depth for the encoder at fixed parameter budget; watch the singular-value spectrum of pooled features (or SimSiam's per-channel std ≈ 1/√d) as a collapse monitor, because the loss itself does not reveal partial collapse.
- CNN-JEPA's "zero the conv outputs under the mask at every layer" trick and SCOTT's "compute only where the kernel centre is unmasked" trick both exist to stop masked regions leaking zeros into the context representation; for a 128×128 input with a stride-16 encoder, the mask grid is 8×8, so target blocks of scale 0.15–0.2 are ~3×3 cells — the I-JEPA block geometry still fits but is coarse; at stride 8 (16×16 grid) it matches CNN-JEPA's 7×7-cell regime better.
- CNN-JEPA ran *below* BYOL/SimCLR/VICReg on the same ResNet-50 (73.3 vs 79.0 for BYOL). For a CNN, the simpler BYOL/VICReg two-view objectives are at least as strong as masked latent prediction, and are cheaper to implement; masked prediction's distinctive value is that it forces *spatial* structure prediction, which the two-view objectives do not.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives any JEPA-style number below 6.5M parameters, or any result for a mobile CNN (MobileNet/ShuffleNet class). The question "does latent prediction work at ~1M parameters" is unanswered in the literature found.
- The TU Delft ViT-Tiny I-JEPA thesis could not be downloaded, so its hyperparameters and low-data curves are unknown.
- Bootleg (2603.15553) was not read; its claim about last-layer instability may matter for small encoders but is unverified.

## Key question 3 — DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2: how is action/time conditioning done in latent space, and what evidence shows structure rather than appearance?

### Takeaway
Both condition a transformer predictor on actions by projecting each action (and, where available, proprioceptive state) with a learned affine/MLP map and placing it as a token (V-JEPA 2-AC) or concatenating it to every patch token (DINO-WM) at the corresponding timestep, with causal/block-causal attention over time, a frozen encoder, and an L1 (V-JEPA 2-AC) or L2 (DINO-WM) loss against the encoder's embedding of the next frame; the "structure not appearance" evidence is indirect — planning success, generalisation to unseen layouts, and the finding that patch-level spatial features beat global features and that adding a pixel decoder to the gradient path hurts.

### Cited Findings
- V-JEPA 2-AC predictor: ~300M-parameter transformer, 24 layers, 16 heads, hidden 1024, GELU, output mapped back to the encoder dimension; frames encoded independently by the frozen ViT-g into 16×16×1408 feature maps; actions, end-effector states and flattened patch features each pass through separate learnable affine maps into the hidden dimension; 3D-RoPE on patches, temporal rotary only on action and pose tokens; block-causal attention (a patch at time t attends to action, state and patches at t and earlier). — [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)
- V-JEPA 2-AC sampling and losses: 4 s clips at 4 fps = 16 frames; action = 7-D change in end-effector state between adjacent frames (xyz, Euler, gripper, relative to robot base); teacher-forcing loss `L_tf = (1/T) Σ_k ‖P_φ((a_t, s_t, E(x_t))_{t≤k}) − E(x_{k+1})‖₁`, T = 15; rollout loss `L_rollout = ‖P_φ(a_{1:T}, s_1, z_1) − z_{T+1}‖₁` with T = 2 so gradients flow through one recurrent step; total `L = L_tf + L_rollout`; encoder frozen; data ≈ 62 h of unlabeled DROID video (~23k trajectories incl. failures). — [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)
- V-JEPA 2-AC planning: energy `E = ‖P(â_{1:T}; s_k, z_k) − z_g‖₁` to a goal image's embedding; CEM with 800 samples, 10 iterations, horizon T = 1, ~16 s per action, receding horizon; actions clipped to an L1 ball of radius 0.075 (~13 cm) because larger actions are out of distribution; Figure 9 shows a smooth, locally convex energy near the true action (minimum ≈ (0, −0.05) vs ground truth (0, −0.1)). — [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)
- V-JEPA 2-AC failure modes: action axes are inferred from an uncalibrated monocular camera — when the robot base is not visible the axes are ill-defined and the model errs, and the authors manually tried several camera positions; autoregressive error accumulation over long horizons; exponential search cost in horizon; goal must be an image. — [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)
- DINO-WM conditioning: actions pass through an MLP action encoder to a 10-d embedding that is concatenated to *each patch vector*; proprioception is concatenated to the observation latents; frameskip 5 (1 for Rope/Granular); history H = 3 or 1; frame-level causal attention, which is decisive with longer history (0.92 vs 0.08 at h = 3). — [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)
- DINO-WM planning: CEM/MPC, 100 samples per iteration, 10 optimisation steps, receding horizon; CEM beat gradient descent; planning horizon and elite count not stated in the extract. — [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)
- DINO-WM evidence on structure: pre-trained-encoder ablation (Table 2) — R3M, ResNet-18 and DINO CLS (all single global vectors) lose to DINOv2 *patch* features on every task, which the paper attributes to patch features preserving spatial information; generalisation — WallRandom (unseen wall/door positions) 0.82 vs DreamerV3 0.76, PushObj (two unseen shapes) 0.34, GranularRandom lowest Chamfer 0.63; a pixel decoder trained jointly hurt (0.80 vs 0.92). The paper offers no direct probe separating structure from appearance. — [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)
- V-JEPA 2's rationale for representation-space prediction is that it "lets the model focus on predictable aspects of a scene, such as an object's trajectory, and ignore unpredictable pixel-level detail like individual blades of grass" — a stated motivation, not an experiment; motion-heavy benchmarks (SSv2 77.3, Jester, Diving-48, EK100 anticipation) are the indirect evidence. — [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1)

### Inferences
- For a localisation model the "action" analogue is a known geometric transform between two views of the same ground (translation in metres, rotation, or the survey identity). The DINO-WM pattern (embed the transform, concatenate to every spatial cell, predict the other view's latent map) is the cheapest way to make a small CNN predict *where things will be* rather than *what colour they are*; a conditioning vector of 10 dimensions was enough for DINO-WM.
- V-JEPA 2-AC's camera-axis failure is the mirror image of the Berlin problem: the model silently learned a frame-dependent coordinate convention from one camera. The orthophoto equivalent is learning one survey's colour pipeline. The remedy used there was data variety at the input (several camera positions tried), which for Berlin means mixing all three surveys in both context and target roles, not photometric augmentation alone.
- The structure-vs-appearance evidence in both papers rests on *downstream task success under distribution shift* (unseen layouts, new object shapes), never on a direct intervention like the +20 brightness test. The Berlin loop already has a sharper structure test than either paper; the fourth-survey score is exactly the DINO-WM "WallRandom" style of evidence.

### Gaps
- Neither paper reports a direct experiment distinguishing structural from photometric content in the learned latent; the question is answered only by proxy.
- DINO-WM's planning horizon and number of CEM elites were not in the read portion; V-JEPA 2's remaining ~128K characters (further ablations) were not read.

## Key question 4 — Which pieces produce invariance to photometric nuisance (brightness, colour, season) vs geometric/structural content? Documented failure modes and their prevention

### Takeaway
Photometric invariance in this family comes almost entirely from *what is paired as positive/target*, not from the latent-prediction objective itself: BYOL-style bootstrapping is markedly more robust than contrastive learning to dropping colour augmentation (63.4 vs 45.7 without colour; 60.1 vs 40.2 with crop+flip only), I-JEPA uses no colour jitter at all and so encodes whatever the images share, and the only method that explicitly separates "invariant to season/survey" from "invariant to synthetic colour change" is SeCo's three-head design. Documented failure modes are complete collapse (fixed by stop-gradient/EMA/predictor), partial dimensional collapse in small models (fixed by width, continual training, or VICReg/Barlow/SIGReg terms), masked-region leakage in CNNs (fixed by sparse/zeroed convs), and the colour-histogram shortcut in contrastive learning (fixed by colour distortion or by bootstrapping).

### Cited Findings
- BYOL augmentation ablation (ImageNet linear, Table 17): baseline BYOL 72.5 / SimCLR 67.9; remove colour jitter only: 71.8 / 63.7; remove colour (jitter + grayscale): 63.4 / 45.7; crop + flip only: 60.1 / 40.2. BYOL's summary: it is "more resilient to changes of image augmentations". — [BYOL, arXiv 2006.07733](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733); independently re-tabulated in [TiCo, arXiv 2206.10698](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.10698) (TiCo 62.7 without colour; TiCo loses 11.3% with crop-only vs 27.7% for SimCLR).
- Barlow Twins is "not robust to removing some types of data augmentations", unlike BYOL (Figure 3; per-augmentation numbers not in the text). — [Barlow Twins](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.03230)
- I-JEPA's official config disables colour augmentation and flips (`color_jitter_strength: 0.0`, `use_horizontal_flip: false`); the method is described as not relying on hand-crafted augmentations. — [ijepa config](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ijepa/blob/main/configs/in1k_vith14_ep300.yaml); [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)
- SeCo (Seasonal Contrast): query = image at date t0; keys k0 = seasonal view t1 + artificial augmentations, k1 = seasonal view t2 only, k2 = artificial augmentations of t0 only. Three heads on a shared backbone: Z0 invariant to everything (all keys positive); Z1 invariant to season, *variant* to artificial augmentation (positive k1; k0 and k2 are negatives); Z2 invariant to artificial augmentation, variant to season (positive k2; k0 and k1 negatives). MoCo-v2 InfoNCE, τ = 0.07, a 16,384-negative queue per head, 2-layer MLP heads with 128-d l2-normalised output; ResNet-18/50, 200 ep, batch 256. Transfer uses the shared embedding, not one head. — [SeCo, arXiv 2103.16607](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.16607)
- SeCo results (ResNet-18, 1M images): BigEarthNet linear-probe 100% — ImageNet-sup 66.40, MoCo-v2 (artificial aug only) 70.79, MoCo-v2 + temporal positives 73.60, SeCo 77.00; EuroSAT — random 63.21, ImageNet 86.44, MoCo-v2 83.72, MoCo-v2+TP 89.51, SeCo 93.14; OSCD change detection F1 (frozen) — random 29.44, ImageNet 36.20, MoCo-v2 40.71, MoCo-v2+TP 40.12, SeCo 46.94. Data: ~200K locations × 5 dates ~3 months apart (jittered up to a year), Sentinel-2 RGB only, patches ~2.65 km, <10% cloud, locations Gaussian-sampled (σ = 50 km) around the 10K largest cities (Gaussian-around-cities beats uniform-in-continent: LP-100 75.52 vs 72.59 at 100K images). — [SeCo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.16607)
- CACo (CVPR 2023) extends SeCo by additionally using images with long time differences as negatives and reports gains over plain SeCo on EuroSAT and DynamicEarthNet (poster only). — [CACo poster](https://research.cs.cornell.edu/caco/static/pdfs/caco_poster.pdf)
- Geography-Aware SSL also builds temporal positive pairs from spatially aligned images over time and adds geo-location pretext tasks (search snippet). — [arXiv 2011.09980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980v4)
- Complete collapse and its fixes: SimSiam without stop-gradient → 0.1%; without predictor → 0.1%; BYOL without momentum encoder → 0.3%. — [SimSiam](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)
- Partial dimensional collapse in small SimSiam models (ResNet-18 30.0% on ImageNet, peak at 5% of the data), invisible in the loss, measured by a rank metric; "collapse exists on a spectrum". — [arXiv 2209.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15007)
- VICReg names "norm collapse" (variance term) and "informational collapse" (covariance term) and reports that adding the variance term to other methods "helps stabilize the training". — [VICReg](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04906v3)
- data2vec: targets built from self-attention outputs yield an unusable model (100% WER) while FFN outputs work; per-layer normalisation of targets is argued to prevent collapse; contextualised (full-input) teacher targets beat local ones. — [data2vec](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03555v3)
- Masking-pattern shortcuts: I-JEPA random/rasterized/single-block masks collapse to 15–20% on 1% IN1K vs 54.2 multi-block; V-JEPA random-tube 0.9 gives 46.4 vs 67.4 multi-block; CNN-JEPA random masking alone 57.64 vs 73.26. — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3); [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1); [CNN-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07514v1)
- CNN-specific leakage: CNN-JEPA zeroes each conv layer's output under the upscaled mask so masked regions cannot carry information through receptive fields; SCOTT computes sparse convs only where the kernel centre is unmasked "so masked regions are not filled with zeros". — [CNN-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07514v1); [SCOTT](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18056v1)
- LayerNorm on targets equalises token L2 norms and (per the feature-normalisation paper) suppresses the energy of high-norm foreground tokens, visible as checkerboard artefacts in loss maps. — [arXiv 2508.02829](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02829v1)
- Interpretation found in secondary commentary (not a tested result): colour-histogram shortcuts may suffice to solve a contrastive instance-discrimination task, whereas BYOL is incentivised to keep additional global information, explaining its lower sensitivity to removing colour distortion. — [BYOL search summary citing towardsai](https://towardsai.net/?p=23008)

### Inferences
- The decisive design lever for "survey-invariant, structure-preserving" features is the *pairing*: SeCo's result that temporal positives alone beat artificial augmentations alone (MoCo-v2+TP 73.60 vs MoCo-v2 70.79 on BigEarthNet; 89.51 vs 83.72 on EuroSAT) is the closest published analogue to "three surveys of the same ground", and it was obtained with ResNet-18 at batch 256 on a single kind of data. For Berlin, the three surveys *are* the temporal positives; the fourth is the held-out season.
- SeCo's split-head trick directly addresses the +20 brightness failure: a head trained to be invariant to the survey (Z1) while another is forced to remain sensitive to synthetic colour change (Z2) prevents the backbone from solving the task by collapsing colour entirely, yet guarantees that at least one subspace cannot read survey identity. The localisation head should hang off the survey-invariant subspace.
- JEPA-style masked prediction supplies the complement: it enforces that the latent carries *spatial layout* (predicting a 3×3-cell block's feature from its surroundings), which two-view instance objectives do not. Combining the two (cross-survey masked prediction: context from survey A, targets from survey B at the same ground, LN'd teacher features) is the configuration no paper here has run but every mechanism above supports.
- Monitoring: log (i) per-channel std of ℓ2-normalised pooled features (healthy ≈ 1/√d), (ii) the singular-value spectrum / effective rank of pooled features, and (iii) the +20 brightness probe, because the collapse paper shows the training loss hides partial collapse and the Berlin history shows the mission score hides photometric memorisation until the held-out survey is scored.

### Gaps
- No primary source quantifies, for any JEPA, how much of the latent variance is photometric vs structural; the BYOL ablation is the only quantitative photometric-robustness result and it concerns augmentation removal, not a test-time brightness shift.
- SeCo has no ablation of temporal-positives-only *inside* SeCo (only MoCo-v2+TP vs full SeCo), so the marginal value of the variant head over plain temporal positives is 3.4 BigEarthNet points by difference, not a controlled ablation.

## Key question 5 — JEPA / latent prediction on aerial, satellite, remote-sensing, or multi-temporal imagery of the same place

### Takeaway
Three JEPA remote-sensing papers exist (REJEPA for retrieval, Sat-JEPA-Diff for forecasting, HQ-JEPA for SAR/optical pairs), all ViT-B-scale on Sentinel/fMoW data and none at sub-metre resolution, none for localisation, and none below 80M parameters; the multi-temporal same-place line is contrastive (SeCo, CACo, Geography-Aware SSL) and is the most directly relevant evidence for the Berlin setting.

### Cited Findings
- REJEPA (Choudhury et al., 2025): I-JEPA-style context/target encoders and predictor, all ViT-B/16; predictor is a lightweight ViT with 384-d embedding and depth 12; target encoder EMA from momentum 0.996 increasing linearly; *disjoint* context and target masks sampled independently with an "optimal masking ratio of 0.25"; prediction loss L2 over M masked target tokens; plus VICReg terms `L_VICReg = λ_v v(z) + λ_c c(z) + λ_i L_inv` with `L = L_pred + L_VICReg` (coefficient values not legible in the extracted text); mask tokens with positional embeddings, predictor applied M times. Datasets: BigEarthNet-14K (14,832 Sentinel-1/2 pairs, 19 classes), fMoW-RGB, fMoW-Sentinel; reports F1 gains of 7.4% on BEN-14K S2, 6.0% on fMoW-RGB, 10.1% on fMoW-Sentinel over Mask-VLM (225.82M params) and SatMAE/ScaleMAE-class baselines; attention heatmaps are the only qualitative evidence. No multi-temporal same-place evaluation. — [REJEPA, arXiv 2504.03169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03169)
- Sat-JEPA-Diff (ICLR 2026 ML4RS workshop): an I-JEPA predictor's semantic representations condition a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone via cross-attention to forecast Sentinel-2 imagery; GSSIM 0.8984, FID 0.1475 on a global Sentinel-2 dataset (search snippet). — [arXiv 2603.13943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13943)
- HQ-JEPA: hybrid quantum-classical JEPA on paired Sentinel-1/Sentinel-2, evaluated on GeoBench (search snippet only). — [arXiv 2605.31068](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31068)
- SeCo / CACo / Geography-Aware SSL: multi-temporal same-location positives — details and numbers in Key question 4. — [SeCo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.16607); [CACo](https://research.cs.cornell.edu/caco/static/pdfs/caco_poster.pdf); [arXiv 2011.09980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980v4)
- SeCo's OSCD change-detection result (F1 46.94 vs 40.71 MoCo-v2, frozen backbone) is a same-place, two-date task, i.e. the features retain what *changed* between dates while being trained to be invariant to the season. — [SeCo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.16607)

### Inferences
- The remote-sensing JEPA papers add nothing mechanistically beyond I-JEPA except REJEPA's two choices: independent (disjoint) context/target masks at a low 0.25 ratio, and VICReg terms bolted onto the pooled embedding to prevent collapse — the second is the one confirmation in this literature that someone training JEPA on satellite data felt the need for an explicit anti-collapse regulariser.
- Resolution matters: all remote-sensing JEPA work is at 10 m/px (Sentinel) or fMoW scales; 1 m/px orthophotos with 128 px crops cover 128 m of ground and are closer in texture statistics to natural images than to Sentinel tiles, so Sentinel-derived mask ratios should not be assumed to carry over.
- The only published evidence about multi-temporal same-place imagery is contrastive, ResNet-18 scale, and consistent: temporal positives are worth 3–6 points over artificial augmentation alone, and the gain is largest on tasks that need spatial detail (change detection).

### Gaps
- No paper found applies JEPA or any latent-prediction method to sub-metre aerial orthophotos, to multi-year surveys of one city, or to absolute localisation / scene-coordinate regression; the Berlin configuration has no direct precedent in this literature.
- REJEPA's VICReg coefficient values and the exact masking-block geometry were not legible in the PDF text; HQ-JEPA and Sat-JEPA-Diff were seen only through search snippets.
