# Bringing self-supervised / world-model representations down to tiny deployable CNNs, and the ESP32-P4 deployment envelope

Scope of these notes: (1) distilling a large SSL/world-model encoder into a few-million-parameter CNN and what survives; (2) running SSL objectives directly on 1-5M-parameter CNNs; (3) the ESP32-P4 + esp-dl envelope (ops, int8 flow, memory, latency, attention feasibility, fp32-vs-int8 size accounting); plus teacher licences and M1 feasibility, and whether int8 interacts with invariance. Context: a from-scratch ~0.8M-parameter CNN (2.94 MB fp32 ONNX, 0.78 MB int8) memorising Berlin at 1 m/px from 128x128 crops, trained on three surveys and scored on a fourth; training on an M1 in under an hour per experiment.

A note on provenance: a few numbers below (marked "measured here") come from a timing script I ran on this laptop's M1 GPU during the research, not from a publication. Everything else carries a URL.

## Key Question 1 — Which distillation objective preserves the teacher's features and invariances best in a tiny student? What is lost?

### Takeaway
On every published ImageNet comparison with 4-11M-parameter students, *relational* objectives (student matches the teacher's similarity distribution over a bank of anchors: SEED, CompRess, BINGO) and *bottleneck-widened feature regression with an auxiliary SSL loss* (DisCo) both beat naive L2 feature regression by 3-10 points, and all of them beat from-scratch SSL on the tiny student by 8-30 points. Plain L2 regression of the teacher embedding barely beats from-scratch (55.3 vs 52.5 for ResNet-18) unless the student's projector is widened or the targets are batch-normalised; the "bottleneck" is the student's own head, not the teacher. No paper measures cross-illumination or cross-season invariance of a *tiny distilled CNN* specifically — that is a gap — but the evidence that exists (transfer to VOC/Places/CIFAR, domain-shift robustness of distilled vs pruned students) says distilled students inherit most of the teacher's transferability, and Proteus shows feature-level MSE (not logits) is what preserves it.

### Cited Findings

**Baseline fact: SSL on tiny networks from scratch is very weak, which is why distillation matters**
- SEED: with MoCo-v2 pretraining from scratch, EfficientNet-B0 reaches 42.2% ImageNet linear top-1 and MobileNet-v3-Large 36.3%, versus 77.3% / 75.2% supervised; with SEED distillation they reach 67.6% / 68.2% — [SEED, arXiv 2101.04731](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731)
- DisCo's parameter counts for the students used across this literature: EfficientNet-B0 4.0M, EfficientNet-B1 6.4M, MobileNet-v3-Large 4.2M, ResNet-18 10.7M, ResNet-34 20.4M; teachers ResNet-50 22.4M, ResNet-101 40.5M, ResNet-152 55.4M, ResNet-50x2 55.5M — [DisCo, arXiv 2104.09124](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.09124v7)
- CompRess: a MoCo ResNet-18 trained from scratch for 1,000 epochs reaches 54.5% linear / 41.1% NN on ImageNet; distilled from a MoCo ResNet-50 teacher (70.8%) it reaches 62.6% linear / 53.5% NN after far less training — [CompRess, arXiv 2010.14713](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713)

**Objective-by-objective numbers (ResNet-18 student, ImageNet linear top-1, unless noted)**
- *Plain L2 feature regression*: SEED's ablation (ResNet-50 teacher): l2-distance 55.3, K-means 51.0, online clustering 56.4, binary contrastive 57.4, SEED+MoCo-v2 57.6, SEED 57.9 — [SEED](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731). CompRess's regression baselines: "Reg" 52.2 linear / 41.7 NN; "Reg-BN" (batch-normalised targets) 58.2 / 47.3; CompRess-1q 62.6 / 53.5 — [CompRess](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713). Without softmax/temperature/memory-bank (plain similarity matching), MoCo R50→R18 gets 53.6 — [CompRess](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713)
- *SEED* (soft cross-entropy between teacher and student softmax-similarity distributions over a FIFO queue of K = 65,536 teacher embeddings, τ_T = 0.01, τ_S = 0.2): R-18 57.9 (R-50 teacher), 58.9 (R-101), 59.5 (R-152), 63.0 (R50x2 SwAV). Teacher temperature matters a lot: τ_T 0.3 → 54.8, 0.1 → 54.9, 0.05 → 56.5, 0.01 → 57.9, 1e-3 → 57.6; on CIFAR-100 transfer the same sweep goes 46.6 → 60.6. Queue size 256 → 65,536 adds ~1.5 points — [SEED](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731)
- *CompRess* (KL between teacher and student similarity distributions over a memory bank of anchors; optimum temperature 0.04, τ = 1 "degrades results dramatically"; larger bank = better): R-18 62.6 (MoCo R50), 65.6 (SwAV R50, teacher 75.6); MobileNet-V2 65.8 linear / 54.8 NN (supervised MobileNet-V2 71.9). Teacher caching (precomputing teacher embeddings once) costs only −0.5 linear (61.7 → 61.2) and −0.4 NN while running ~3x faster — [CompRess](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713)
- *DisCo* (squared-L2 between student and teacher final embeddings for both views + MoCo-v2 contrastive loss on the student, λ = 1, teacher frozen, 200 epochs): R-18 60.6 (R-50), 62.3 (R-101), 65.5 (R-152), 65.2 (R50x2); MobileNet-v3-L 64.4 (R-50) — 9.2 points above SEED with the same teacher; EfficientNet-B0 66.5 (R-50), 69.1 (R50x2) — [DisCo](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.09124v7)
- *DisCo's "distilling bottleneck"*: the student's default MLP head (512→512→128) is the bottleneck. Varying the hidden width (Eff-B0 / Mob-v3 / R-18): no MLP 52.5 / 60.3 / 52.5; 512 → 62.5 / 62.8 / 57.1; 1024 → 65.0 / 63.8 / 59.2; 2048 → 66.5 / 64.4 / 60.6. For ResNet-18 the widening alone is worth +3.5 points (57.1 → 60.6), ResNet-34 +3.6 — [DisCo](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.09124v7)
- *BINGO* (teacher-defined "bags" of k = 5 nearest neighbours; intra-sample loss pulls student/teacher views of one image together; inter-sample loss pulls bag-mates toward the anchor; 65,536 memory bank, temperature 0.2, 200 epochs): R-18 61.4 (R-50 at 67.4), 64.0 (R-50 at 71.1), 65.9 (R-152), 65.5 (R50x2); R-34 69.1 (R-152). Under matched cost, R-34 from R-152 reaches 67.8 at 100 epochs vs SEED 62.7 at 200 — [BINGO, arXiv 2107.01691](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.01691)
- *Proteus* (distilling DINOv2 using only ImageNet-1K images and no labels; three MSE objectives — CLS-token, pre-classifier feature, and iBOT-style masked patch prediction of the teacher's patch tokens; all weights 1): with ViT-S/14 from DINOv2-B at 300 epochs, logit-based KD gives fine-grained avg 71.5-80.5 while feature ("hint") MSE gives 85.3; adding the patch-level objective lifts ADE20K from 47.4 to 50.0 mIoU. Smallest student ViT-Ti/14 from DINOv2-S: ImageNet linear 75.2, fine-grained avg 80.2, ADE20K 40.7 (DeiT-Ti supervised: 74.5 / 73.3 / 35.8). Batch 1024, 8xA100, ~30 h for ViT-S — [Proteus, arXiv 2407.10366](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10366v2)
- *TinyViT* (supervised KD with sparse top-K teacher logits precomputed to disk; K = 10 for ImageNet-1k = 16 GB, K = 100 for ImageNet-21k = 481 GB): TinyViT-5M 79.1 from scratch → 80.7 with distilled pretraining; 11M 81.5 → 83.2; 21M 83.1 → 84.8. Small models "tend to saturate ... bottlenecked by the model capacities"; TinyViT-21M fits only 53.2% of ImageNet-21k training set vs 57.1% for Swin-L. Using ground-truth labels alongside distillation slightly *hurts* (84.3 vs 84.5) — [TinyViT, arXiv 2207.10666](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.10666)
- *Lightly's production recipe*: DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (no registers) teacher, torchvision ResNet-18 default student, MSE between student and teacher features for the same image, identical strong augmentations to both, 1 projection layer with hidden dim 2048, 224 px, batch 128-2048, 100-3000 epochs ("up to 10,000 may help" for datasets under 100k images) — [LightlyTrain distillation docs](https://docs.lightly.ai/train/stable/methods/distillation.html)

**Training-recipe findings that transfer to a small single-domain dataset**
- "Function matching" (Beyer et al.): give teacher and student the *same* crop and augmentation, use aggressive mixup (coefficients uniform in [0,1]), and train for thousands of epochs. ResNet-50 student on ImageNet: 300 ep 80.30, 1200 ep 81.54, 4800 ep 82.18, 9600 ep 82.31. A *fixed* teacher (precomputed outputs for one view) starts overfitting after 600 epochs: 77.76 at 300 ep falling to 76.95 at 9600 ep. On small datasets with a ResNet-50 at 128 px: Pets37 300 → 30,000 epochs gives 82.75 → 91.06% (teacher 91.03); Flowers102 needs 1,000,000 epochs to reach 96.93 (teacher 97.82); Food101 and SUN397 match the teacher by 3,000 epochs — [Beyer et al., arXiv 2106.05237](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.05237)
- Capacity gap: "larger models do not often make better teachers"; chaining distillation steps does not fix it; early-stopping the teacher helps — [Cho & Hariharan, arXiv 1910.01348](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01348). Counter-evidence at the DINOv2 scale: distilling a probed DINOv2 ViT-g into ViT-S, "a large capacity gap between teacher and student did not hurt distillation"; ViT-g and ViT-L teachers work about equally; probed (frozen + linear head) teachers beat finetuned ones — [On Good Practices for Task-Specific Distillation, arXiv 2402.11305](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11305)
- Same paper, randomly-initialised ResNet-50 student on small datasets, task-specific KL distillation (T = 2, α = 0.5): CUB 73.3 → 79.1, Aircraft 85.0 → 87.8, DTD 63.5 → 69.4, ADE20K 37.8 → 42.1, VOC 67.5 → 73.9 (with Stable-Diffusion mixing; ~+2% without); "the ResNet-50 benefits more from distillation than the pretrained ViT-S does" — [arXiv 2402.11305](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11305)
- SEED teacher-pretraining ablation (R-18 student): MoCo (60.6% teacher) → 52.1 student, *below* the 52.5 from-scratch baseline; SimCLR 65.6 → 57.5; MoCo-v2 67.4 → 57.9; MoCo-v2 800 ep 71.1 → 60.5; SwAV 75.3 → 62.0-62.6. A weak teacher can make the student worse than no teacher — [SEED](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731)

**What survives / transfer and robustness evidence**
- CompRess AlexNet student (from SimCLR ResNet-50x4) matches supervised on VOC classification (79.7 vs 79.9 mAP), detection (58.1-59.3 vs 59.1) and beats it on Places (40.4 vs 39.4) and Cars196 retrieval (28.4 vs 26.6 R@1) without fine-tuning — [CompRess](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713)
- SEED on VOC07 detection with ResNet-18: AP +0.0/+0.7/+0.7 for R-50/R-101/R-152 teachers (i.e. dense/localisation transfer gains are small compared with the +5 to +10 linear-probe gains) — [SEED](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731)
- DisCo on ResNet-34: VOC AP +2.9, COCO AP +1.9, mask AP +1.9 over MoCo-v2 — [DisCo](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.09124v7)
- Under Office-31 domain shift (supervised KD, ResNets at 11.19M parameters): distilled students average 68.58-69.05% target-domain accuracy vs 61.79% (pruned ResNet-50) and 41.80% (pruned ResNet-152 to the same size) — distillation retains cross-domain behaviour far better than pruning to the same size — [Robustness to distribution shifts of compressed networks, arXiv 2401.12014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.12014)
- Place-recognition distillation of DINOv2 exists (D2-VPR: two-stage distillation + fine-tuning with a feature-recovery module, ~64% fewer parameters and ~63% fewer FLOPs than CricaVPR) but the student remains a DINOv2-small ViT, not a CNN — [D2-VPR, arXiv 2511.12528](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12528v4)

### Inferences
- For a ~0.8M-parameter student the ordering seen at 4-11M will, if anything, be more extreme: from-scratch SSL will underperform badly (the 4M students' from-scratch numbers are 36-47%), and the student's head width will be a first-order knob (DisCo's +3.5 to +4 points from 512 → 2048 hidden). A distillation head can be as wide as needed at training time and dropped before export, so the 4 MiB gate does not constrain it.
- Relational losses (SEED/CompRess/BINGO) are the right default when the goal is *geometry of the embedding space* (which cells look alike); feature MSE with a wide head + an auxiliary loss (DisCo/Proteus) is the right default when the goal is *dense, localisable* features (Proteus's patch-level term is what moved ADE20K). For a localisation model the Proteus-style patch-token target is the natural fit, since each 14x14-px teacher patch is a 14x14 m ground patch at 1 m/px.
- CompRess's teacher-caching result (−0.5 point for 3x speed) and Beyer's fixed-teacher overfitting result are not contradictory: CompRess caches the teacher over the *un-augmented* bank used as anchors, while the online query still passes through the teacher; Beyer precomputed the teacher output for one fixed view of each training image. On an M1 budget the practical compromise is to cache teacher embeddings per (crop, survey) — three surveys give three distinct teacher views of each ground patch for free, which is exactly the invariance the fourth-survey test needs — and only use the teacher online for views that are new (rotations, jitter). That is an inference about this project's setting, not a published result.
- Cross-survey invariance will not be "preserved" by the objective so much as *encoded in which targets you match*: if the three surveys of the same ground cell are forced to the same teacher similarity distribution (CompRess/SEED over a bank of ground cells) or the same patch target, the student is trained on exactly the invariance the fourth survey tests. No paper measures this for a tiny CNN; it has to be measured here.
- Weak-teacher warning transfers: SEED showed a MoCo teacher at 60.6% made the student *worse* than from-scratch. A teacher that is itself poor at distinguishing Berlin blocks (e.g. a 30 m/px multispectral model, see Q2) could do the same.

### Gaps
- No paper measures cross-illumination / cross-season / cross-survey invariance of a distilled student under 5M parameters. Nordland-style cross-season VPR numbers exist only for ViT students or 30M-class CNNs (D2-VPR, LPS-VPR). This must be measured in-project.
- No published distillation into a student below ~4M parameters on an image task; all tables bottom out at EfficientNet-B0 / MobileNet-v3 (4.0-4.2M). Behaviour at 0.8M is extrapolation.
- I did not find a comparison of relational vs feature-MSE distillation on *aerial/satellite* imagery specifically.

## Key Question 2 — Teachers: licences and feasibility on an M1 over ~218k 128x128 crops per epoch

### Takeaway
DINOv2 (ViT-S/14 21M, ViT-B/14 86M) is Apache-2.0 for code *and* weights and is the only large SSL teacher that is unambiguously permissive, relevant to RGB imagery, and fast enough: measured here, a ViT-S/14-sized network at 126 px runs ~206 img/s fp32 / ~292 img/s fp16 on this M1 (218k crops in 17.6 / 12.5 min), so one cached teacher pass per survey fits the hour, but running the teacher online every epoch does not. I-JEPA is CC-BY-NC; V-JEPA 2 is MIT but is a video model; MobileCLIP weights are under Apple's own model TOU; ConvNeXt-V2 weights are CC-BY-NC; Prithvi-EO-2.0 is Apache-2.0 but expects six HLS bands at 30 m/px; SatMAE's licence is not stated on its README.

**Other feasibility facts**
- CompRess caches teacher embeddings and loses only 0.5 linear / 0.4 NN points while running ~3x faster — [CompRess](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713)
- DINOv2 patch size is 14 for all variants — [facebookresearch/dinov2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2)
- Proteus distilled DINOv2-S into ViT-Ti/14 with ImageNet-1K only (1.2M images, 300 epochs, batch 1024) and reached 75.2 linear / 80.2 fine-grained — proof the DINOv2 feature targets survive a 1.2M-image proxy dataset with no overlap with the teacher's data — [Proteus](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10366v2)

### Inferences
- With 128-px crops the DINOv2 input must be 126 px (9x9 patches) or 140 px (10x10); at 1 m/px each teacher patch token then covers 14x14 m of ground, which is coarser than the champion's 10 m p10 error but finer than the 100 m usable radius — the patch grid is a natural target for a localisation head.
- Budget arithmetic for the hour: one ViT-S/14 fp16 pass over 218k crops is ~12.5 min, so three surveys' worth of cached embeddings is ~38 min *once*, reusable across experiments; ViT-B is ~73 min fp32 per survey and only viable as a one-off cache. Running any ViT teacher online per epoch is out of the budget; cached targets plus the student's own cheap augmentations is the workable shape (cf. CompRess's −0.5 point for caching).
- Cache size is small: 218k crops x 3 surveys x (1 CLS + 81 patch) tokens x 384 dims x fp16 = ~31 GB for full patch tokens, or ~0.5 GB for CLS/pooled only. Patch-token caching is feasible on disk but worth pooling to a 3x3 grid (~3.5 GB) if the loader is the bottleneck.
- Prithvi/SatMAE-Sentinel are the wrong scale (30 m, 10 m) and the wrong bands; at 1 m/px RGB orthophotos the "remote-sensing foundation model" family offers no licence or domain advantage over DINOv2, and SEED's weak-teacher result suggests a mismatched teacher could hurt.
- V-JEPA 2 is the only *JEPA* with an MIT licence; using it on single frames means tubelet patches over a repeated frame, and its smallest checkpoint is 80M (V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B/16) — cost similar to ViT-B above. Not measured here.

### Gaps
- SatMAE licence not confirmed (README does not state it).
- DINOv3's actual licence text was not fetched (secondary sources only).
- Throughput was measured with random weights and PyTorch MPS; MLX or CoreML could be faster, and real DINOv2 has registers/interpolated positional embeddings that add negligible cost — not measured.
- I did not find published M1 throughput for DINOv2.

## Key Question 3 — From-scratch SSL at tiny scale (BYOL/SimSiam/VICReg/JEPA on ResNet-18 or smaller, small single-domain datasets): what works and which hyperparameters

### Takeaway
On CIFAR-scale data a ResNet-18 (11M) learns good features from scratch with BYOL, MoCo-v3, Barlow Twins or VICReg at batch 256 (CIFAR-10 linear 92-93%, CIFAR-100 68-71%) but only with ~1000 epochs, LARS, a 4096-wide projector/predictor and (for BYOL) EMA τ 0.99 → 1.0; SimSiam, DINO, SwAV and W-MSE lag by 1-4 points and SimSiam specifically suffers partial dimensional collapse when the model is small relative to the data (ResNet-18 ImageNet 30.0 vs BYOL 47.6 vs MoCo-v3 53.8). Below ResNet-18, and for I-JEPA on tiny CNNs, there are no published successes; the closest is an ~11M ViT I-JEPA reimplementation on STL-10 at ~77% linear.

### Cited Findings
- solo-learn, ResNet-18, 1000 epochs, CIFAR-10 linear top-1: MoCo V3 93.10, MoCo V2+ 92.94, BYOL 92.58, Barlow Twins 92.10, VICReg 92.07, NNCLR 91.88, SimCLR 90.74, ReSSL 90.63, SimSiam 90.51, DINO 89.52, SwAV 89.17, W-MSE 88.67 (SupCon 93.82). CIFAR-100: Barlow 70.90, BYOL 70.46, MoCo V2+ 69.89, NNCLR 69.62, MoCo V3 68.83, VICReg 68.54, DINO 66.76, SimSiam 66.04, ReSSL 65.92, SimCLR 65.78, SwAV 64.88, W-MSE 61.33. ImageNet-100 (400 ep, offline linear): Barlow 80.16, BYOL 80.32, MoCo V3 80.36, VICReg 79.40, SimSiam 78.72, DINO 74.92, SwAV 74.28 — [vturrisi/solo-learn](https://github.com/vturrisi/solo-learn)
- solo-learn's CIFAR BYOL config: batch 256, 1000 epochs, LARS (eta 0.02, clip_lr, exclude bias/norm), lr 1.0, weight decay 1e-5, EMA base_tau 0.99 → final_tau 1.0, projector 4096 → 256, predictor hidden 4096, warmup-cosine, fp16 — [solo-learn byol.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vturrisi/solo-learn/main/scripts/pretrain/cifar/byol.yaml)
- BYOL target-decay ablation (ImageNet, 300 ep): τ_base 1 (random fixed target) 18.8; 0.999 → 69.8; 0.99 → 72.5; 0.9 → 68.4; 0 → 0.3 (collapse). Main runs use τ_base 0.996 with a cosine increase to 1; projector = linear 4096 → BN → ReLU → linear 256, predictor identical. Batch size: "remains stable over a wide range of batch sizes from 256 to 4096" while SimCLR "rapidly deteriorates" — [BYOL, arXiv 2006.07733](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.07733). Secondary sources put the 4096 → 256 drop at −0.6/−0.7 points for BYOL vs −3.4/−3.6 for SimCLR — [towardsai summary](https://towardsai.net/?p=23008) (blog; exact figure is only in BYOL Fig. 3a)
- VICReg: batch 256 and 128 cost 0.7 and 1.2 points; projector dimensionality 256 → 55.9 top-1 vs 16384 → 68.8, levelling off at 8192-16384 (ImageNet) — [VICReg, arXiv 2105.04906](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.04906). A CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 reimplementation uses encoder 512 / projector 1024 and switches to Adam at batch 256 — [augustwester/vicreg](https://github.com/augustwester/vicreg). VICReg and Barlow Twins "are known to be sensitive to embedding dimension, typically requiring dimensions larger than 2048" — [T-REGS, arXiv 2510.23484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23484)
- SimSiam and small models: "SimSiam performance drops significantly when the model capacity is too small relative to the data complexity." ImageNet linear probe, multi-epoch: ResNet-18 30.0 ± 1.8, ResNet-34 16.8 ± 3.2, ResNet-50 68.1; BYOL ResNet-18 47.6, MoCo-v3 53.8. Collapse is partial dimensional collapse (PCA explained-variance AUC); k-NN accuracy of ResNet-18 *peaks* at a 5% subset of ImageNet and then falls; width matters more than depth; a 10-epoch LR warmup does not fix it (28.4). The hybrid continual-then-multi-epoch recipe recovers 48.3 (R-18) / 50.3 (R-34); single-pass training does not help BYOL (44.2) or MoCo-v3 (48.9) "because those methods do not collapse". Hyperparameters: batch 256, SGD lr 0.05, momentum 0.9, cosine, 100 epochs — [arXiv 2209.15007](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.15007)
- SimSiam vs BYOL stability across batch sizes: SimSiam's predictor "doesn't depend on batch statistics the way BYOL's EMA target does"; for small batches BYOL/DINO's EMA "is more affected by the batch centers computed over the small batches" — [The Common Stability Mechanism behind most SSL approaches, arXiv 2402.14957](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14957)
- I-JEPA at small scale: original paper reports only large ViTs on ImageNet — [I-JEPA, arXiv 2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08243v3.pdf); a community reimplementation on unlabeled STL-10 with an ~11M-parameter ViT reports ~77% linear top-1 — [simple-ijepa](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Ffilipbasara0%2Fsimple-ijepa); a convolution-augmented ViT with a JEPA-style masked objective works on Flowers-102 / Pets / ImageNet-100 without external data — [arXiv 2502.18056](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18056v1). No I-JEPA result on a CNN of any size was found.
- SSL from scratch on 4M-parameter CNNs (ImageNet, MoCo-v2): EfficientNet-B0 42.2-46.8, MobileNet-v3-L 36.2-36.3, vs 52.2-52.5 for ResNet-18 and 67.4 for ResNet-50 — [SEED](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731); [DisCo](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.09124v7)

### Inferences
- The tricks that make SSL work at ResNet-18/CIFAR scale are consistent across sources: batch ~256 is fine for BYOL/VICReg/Barlow (not SimCLR); EMA momentum must be *lower* than the ImageNet default (0.99 rather than 0.996) and annealed to 1; projector/predictor stay wide (4096) even though the backbone is small — i.e. the head does not shrink with the backbone; schedules are long (1000 epochs of a 50k-image set = 50M samples). For 218k crops that is ~230 epochs-equivalent for the same sample count — roughly 4 h at the ResNet-18-at-128-px forward rate measured above, before backward passes — so a from-scratch SSL run does not fit a one-hour experiment at ResNet-18 scale, and at 0.8M parameters the SEED/DisCo from-scratch numbers (36-47% on ImageNet for 4M models) say the representation would be poor anyway.
- SimSiam is the wrong choice for a tiny model on a large single-domain corpus: its collapse is worst exactly when "the model is too small relative to the data", and k-NN accuracy *falling* with more data is the signature to watch for. BYOL/MoCo-v3-style EMA targets or VICReg are safer; VICReg needs a wide projector (>2048).
- The project's constraint (memorise one box, be invariant across surveys) is closer to supervised-with-known-positives than to generic SSL: the three surveys give true positive pairs without augmentation. That favours contrastive/relational objectives with cross-survey positives over augmentation-only SSL, and favours distillation (Q1) over from-scratch SSL on the tiny student.

### Gaps
- No published BYOL/VICReg/SimSiam results on CNNs below ~11M parameters or on aerial single-city data.
- The exact BYOL batch-256 number is in a figure only; secondary sources disagree by 0.1 point.
- No I-JEPA on a CNN; no I-JEPA under 11M parameters.

## Key Question 4 — ESP32-P4 + esp-dl deployment envelope: supported ops, int8/QAT flow, memory, latency, attention feasibility, published small-CNN projects

### Takeaway
esp-dl 3.3.x supports 64 ONNX operators on ESP32/S3/P4 (same list, different speed) including Conv (groups 1 or depthwise only), Gemm, MatMul up to 4D, Softmax (float32 output), LayerNormalization, Transpose/Reshape/Resize, and all activations except ReLU/PReLU via an 8-bit LUT; quantisation is symmetric power-of-two int8 (per-channel on P4 for Conv/Gemm), int16, or mixed w8a16, via esp-ppq (PTQ + label-free TQT finetuning, and a QAT example). MobileNetV2-int8 at 224 px runs 164.6 ms single-core / 125.1 ms dual-core on P4 at 400 MHz (70.44% top-1 vs 71.88% fp32). The chip has 768 KB internal L2MEM, 16 KB I / 64 KB D L1 cache and 16-32 MB in-package PSRAM at 250 MHz; active current is 66-110 mA single-core at 400 MHz. Attention blocks are expressible but no latency figure for a ViT on P4 is published.

### Cited Findings

**Operator support (esp-dl operator_support_state.md; columns int8 / int16 / w8a16 / float32)**
- 64 operators listed; the list is the same for ESP32, ESP32-S3 and ESP32-P4 — ESP32 has no PIE instructions (all C, "significantly slower"), S3 has PIE V1, P4 has PIE V2; P4 rounds half-to-even, S3/ESP32 round half-up — [operator_support_state.md](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/operator_support_state.md)
- Supported (int8/int16/f32): Conv 1D/2D (groups of 1 or input_channels only — no grouped conv between), Gemm, MatMul (up to 4D; w8a16 only with static second input), Softmax (output float32), LogSoftmax (float32 output), LayerNormalization, RMSNormalization, LpNormalization, Transpose, Reshape, Flatten, Squeeze, Unsqueeze, Concat, Split, Slice, Gather, ScatterND, Pad (no wrap), Resize (1D/2D nearest/linear/bilinear, int8 only, no antialias), ConvTranspose (as InsertZeros+Conv), DepthToSpace, SpaceToDepth, GlobalAveragePool, AveragePool (no dilation), MaxPool (no f32), Add, Sub, Mul, Div, Pow, Sqrt, Exp, Log, Neg, Mod (fmod=1), Clip, Relu, LeakyRelu, PRelu, Elu, Sigmoid, HardSigmoid, HardSwish, Swish, Tanh, Greater/Less/Equal and variants, Reduce{Mean,Max,Min,Sum,Prod,L1,L2,LogSum,LogSumExp,SumSquare}, GRU, LSTM, ReverseSequence — [operator_support_state.md](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/operator_support_state.md)
- *Not listed*: Gelu, Erf, BatchNormalization, InstanceNorm, GroupNorm, Upsample, Expand, Where — [operator_support_state.md](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/operator_support_state.md). (BatchNorm is normally folded into Conv at export; GroupNorm/InstanceNorm would need decomposition.)
- "All activations except ReLU and PReLU use an 8-bit LUT, so any activation function has the same computational cost"; per-channel quantisation for Conv and Gemm is ESP32-P4 only; dual-core scheduling exists for Conv2D and DepthwiseConv2D; a static memory planner places each layer "in the best memory location based on the internal RAM size the user specifies"; requires ESP-IDF ≥ 5.3; `.espdl` is a FlatBuffers format (zero-copy) viewable in Netron — [espressif/esp-dl README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl)

**Quantisation flow**
- esp-ppq (`pip install esp-ppq`, renamed from ppq July 2025) takes ONNX (PyTorch/TF via ONNX). Modes: `w8a8` (default, fastest), `w16a16`, `w8a16` (int8 weights / int16 activations for Conv/Gemm/MatMul with static weights, esp-dl ≥ 3.3.11; depthwise falls back to int16), `none` (fp32 debug). Strategy is "symmetric quantization + POWER OF TWO". ESP32/S3: per-tensor, ROUND_HALF_UP; P4/S31: per-channel for Conv/GEMM, per-tensor elsewhere, ROUND_HALF_EVEN; models for different targets must not be mixed. PTQ uses a calibration dataloader; mixed precision via a per-operator dispatching table; only batch size 1 is supported at inference; "board-side ESP-DL results align with ESP-PPQ" so PC-side accuracy is the deployed accuracy — [how_to_quantize_model](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_quantize_model.html)
- TQT (Trained Quantization Thresholds): finetunes scales (in log2 so they stay powers of two) and weights with an MSE loss between float and quantised outputs on an unlabeled calibration set, straight-through estimator; requires esp-ppq ≥ 1.2.7; the doc explicitly contrasts it with full QAT. MobileNetV2 8-bit on P4: fp32 71.878 → per-channel PTQ 70.850 → TQT 71.775 — [quantize_model_with_TQT.rst](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/docs/en/tutorials/quantize_model_with_TQT.rst). The quantisation page lists "Quantization Aware Training" as an advanced method with a YOLO11n-pose QAT example but does not document a PyTorch fake-quant flow — [how_to_quantize_model](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_quantize_model.html)
- MobileNetV2 deploy tutorial settings: 224 px, 1,024 ImageNet calibration images, KL activation calibration, layerwise equalisation (4 iterations, threshold 0.1, opt level 2), TQT on (P4: lr 5e-5, 800 steps, block 4; S3: lr 1e-5, 1000 steps), ReLU6 → ReLU. Accuracy: fp32 71.878; P4 int8 70.441 / 89.353 top-5; S3 int8 70.543 / 89.525 — [how_to_deploy_mobilenetv2](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_deploy_mobilenetv2.html)

**Latency and memory figures**
- MobileNetV2 int8 (224x224x3, `CONFIG_COMPILER_OPTIMIZATION_PERF`): ESP32-S3 (240 MHz, 64 KB D-cache, octal PSRAM 80 MHz) 437.9 ms single / 389.7 ms dual, 70.543%; ESP32-S31 (320 MHz, PSRAM 250 MHz) 292.2 / 302.1 ms; ESP32-P4 (400 MHz, 256 KB L2 cache, 32 MB PSRAM at 250 MHz) 164.6 ms single / 125.1 ms dual, 70.441%; preprocessing 2.7 ms on P4. Dual-core is slower than single on S31 (only one core has SIMD) — [esp-dl models/imagenet_cls README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/models/imagenet_cls/README.md). The example log in the 3.3.14 component reports 176.7 / 121.6 ms; versions 3.3.7-3.3.11 reported ~330 / ~225 ms, i.e. a ~1.9x speed-up in the 2026-10 release — [mobilenetv2_cls example 3.3.14](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp-dl/versions/3.3.14/examples/mobilenetv2_cls?language=en); [esp-dl README news](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl)
- Operator-level benchmark: Conv geometric-mean speed-up over plain ESP32 is 22.9x on S3 and 38.8x on P4 (78 test cases); total mean operator time over common cases 11.49 s (ESP32), 0.963 s (S3), 0.321 s (P4) — [benchmark_report.md](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/benchmark_report.md)
- Older mobilenet_v2 example (esp-dl 3.1.0) logged "partition size is 7900 KB" and "Maximum memory size: 1705984" bytes peak — [mobilenet_v2 example 3.1.0](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp-dl/versions/3.1.0/examples/mobilenet_v2); the deploy guide's partition table reserves a 4000K `model` data partition (spiffs subtype) — [search summary of deploy guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_deploy_mobilenetv2.html)
- Community data points (S3, not esp-dl-optimised): YOLO11n COCO example "takes 26 sec" on S3; an Ultralytics reply estimates ~13 s and suggests imgsz 128-160; an Edge Impulse user saw ~9.5 s and constant output from a custom int8 EfficientNet-Lite — [esp32.com thread](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=153290); [Ultralytics community](https://community.ultralytics.com/t/is-it-impractical-for-me-to-run-a-yolo11n-quantization-model-on-an-esp32s3/1628); [Edge Impulse forum](https://forum.edgeimpulse.com/t/critical-esp32-s3-failure-my-own-efficientnet-lite-int8-tflite-is-stuck-with-constant-output/17285). No published custom-CNN-at-128-px benchmark on P4 was found.

**Chip facts (ESP32-P4 Series Datasheet v0.7, extracted locally with pdftotext from the saved PDF)**
- HP system: 32-bit RISC-V dual-core up to 400 MHz, RV32IMAFC + Zc/Zb + custom "Xhwlp" loop extension + custom AI/DSP extension "Xai" (datasheet §4.1.1.3; the TRM has the instruction detail); 6.92 CoreMark/MHz dual-core; 128 KB HP ROM, **768 KB HP L2MEM**, 32 KB LP SRAM, 8 KB SPM; two-level cache: 16 KB L1 I-cache (4-way) + 64 KB L1 D-cache (2-way) + an L2 cache (esp-dl's README quotes 256 KB for the P4 benchmark board); in-package 16 MB or 32 MB PSRAM (16-line, 1.8 V); MIPI CSI and DSI; H.264 encoder; 2D-DMA — [ESP32-P4 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-p4_datasheet_en.pdf)

**Attention / transformer feasibility**
- MatMul (≤4D), Softmax (int8 input, float32 output), LayerNormalization, Transpose, Reshape, Gather are all supported; Gelu/Erf are not listed, Swish/HardSwish/Sigmoid/Tanh are (via LUT). PP-OCRv6 "runs on ESP32-P4" and the 2026-10 release optimised MatMul, Transpose, Reduce and LUT — [operator_support_state.md](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/operator_support_state.md); [esp-dl README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl). No ViT/attention latency figure on P4 is published in the esp-dl docs or model zoo.

### Inferences
- A latency estimate for the project's model: MobileNetV2 at 224 px is ~300 M MACs and costs 164.6 ms single-core on P4; MAC count scales with pixel area, so the same network at 128 px would be ~(128/224)^2 = 0.33x → ~54 ms single-core, ~41 ms dual-core. A 0.8M-parameter CNN at 128 px will be bounded by its own MAC count, not its parameter count; the 250 ms host-CPU fp32 proxy is not convertible without the MAC count, but the §2 schedule (one fix per 5-10 s cruise, 1-2 s on approach) leaves a budget of hundreds of ms, so the MobileNetV2-class figure already fits with room.
- Power: 66-110 mA at 400 MHz single-core (datasheet, at the chip's supply, PSRAM traffic extra) is ~0.2-0.4 W at 3.3 V by my arithmetic — well inside the §2 ceiling (6-9 W) and the "ideally under 2 W" target, even before duty-cycling between fixes.
- Memory: 768 KB internal L2MEM is the fast tier; the int8 weights (0.78 MB for the champion) plus activations will not all fit internally, so esp-dl's planner will place some tensors in PSRAM (250 MHz on P4 vs 80 MHz on S3 — the S3's 2.7x slower MobileNetV2 time is consistent with that). Designing the 128-px model so its largest activation tensor stays under a few hundred KB keeps the hot path in L2MEM.
- Attention is *expressible* in esp-dl (MatMul + Softmax + LayerNorm + Transpose all int8/int16) but Softmax emits float32 and Gelu is absent, so a ViT block would need GELU → Swish/HardSwish substitution (free via LUT) and a re-quantisation after Softmax. With no published ViT timing on P4 and Conv enjoying a 38.8x PIE speed-up, a CNN body with at most a small attention/pooling head is the lower-risk design; measure before committing.
- The gating function in `pipeline/score.py` could be made deployment-faithful cheaply: export ONNX → esp-ppq `w8a8` with 1,024 calibration crops → read the `.espdl` size and run the PC-side quantised evaluation (which the docs say matches on-device bit-for-bit). That gives an int8 accuracy figure and an on-flash size without hardware.

### Gaps
- No published esp-dl latency for any attention/transformer block or ViT on P4.
- No published P4 latency for a custom CNN at 128 px; only MobileNetV2 at 224.
- The `.espdl` size of the MobileNetV2 example is not stated (the 7900 KB figure is a partition size); exact on-flash overhead of the FlatBuffers container is undocumented beyond "16-byte aligned weights, test I/O included in `.info`".
- esp-ppq's full QAT (fake-quant in the PyTorch graph) is referenced by example only; whether it supports arbitrary custom CNNs end-to-end was not verified.
- Peak RAM for MobileNetV2 on current esp-dl (the 1.7 MB figure is from v3.1.0) not re-measured.

## Key Question 5 — Does int8 quantisation (PTQ or QAT) interact with invariance / cross-condition robustness? And how does 4 MiB fp32 vs ~1 MiB int8 size accounting work?

### Takeaway
At 8 bits the measured robustness cost is small and specific: QAT'd ResNet-18 loses ~1.1 points of average ImageNet-C accuracy (32.78 → 31.70), with impulse noise worst and *brightness least affected* (~10% drop across bit-widths); int8 PTQ costs ~1.2 points of target-domain accuracy under Office-31 shift versus ~13 points for pruning/distillation to the same size; and a CLIP study finds quantisation acts as a low-pass filter that slightly *improves* blur/noise robustness while slightly degrading ImageNet-R/A and adding 1.2-1.7 points of vulnerability to spurious background correlations. For size: 4 MiB fp32 ≈ 1.05M parameters; esp-dl int8 stores one byte per weight plus per-channel power-of-two scales in a FlatBuffers `.espdl`, which is why the 2.94 MB champion becomes 0.78 MB.

### Cited Findings
- 8-bit QAT (DoReFa / PACT / LSQ, ResNet-18, ImageNet-C average over corruptions): fp32 32.78 → 8-bit 31.70 / 31.69 / 31.70; across bit-widths impulse noise has the largest effect (~50% decrease) and brightness the smallest (~10%); the paper's emphasis is that low-bit (2-4) models are "more vulnerable under natural corruptions" — [arXiv 2304.03968](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03968)
- Int8 post-training static quantisation under Office-31 domain shift (Amazon → Webcam / DSLR): ResNet-18 63.77/66.87 → 63.02/65.26; ResNet-50 72.45/75.30 → 71.45/72.69; ResNet-101 75.09/76.71 → 74.21/74.50; average target-domain drop ~1.2% for quantised vs ~13% for pruned and distilled models at 50% compression; authors recommend PTQ "when about a 4x size reduction is sufficient" and caution that the apparent adversarial robustness may be gradient masking — [arXiv 2401.12014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.12014)
- CLIP (OpenCLIP ViT-B/32, B/16, L/14; PTQ, SmoothQuant, RaanA; QAT-LoRA, Basic-QAT, QAT-LSQ; W8A8 to W4A6): quantisation "acts as a low-pass filter", suppressing high-frequency fine-grained features and increasing reliance on coarse features and textures; QAT improves ECE by ~15% (WIT models) but degrades it for LAION models; QAT "consistently slightly degrades" ImageNet-R and ImageNet-A; QAT adds +1.2% (WIT) / +1.7% (LAION) vulnerability on CounterAnimals (spurious correlations); QAT improves robustness to low-level corruptions such as blur and noise on CIFAR; PTQ can wreck OOD-detection AUROC (CIFAR10→100 NegLabel 95.02 → 80.10) — [arXiv 2509.21173](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21173v2)
- PTQ reliability benchmark: worst-case subgroup performance under distribution shift "is influenced by numerous factors, including the distribution of the calibration set, PTQ setting, and calibration metrics" — [arXiv 2303.13003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.13003)
- esp-dl accuracy cost on a real deployment: MobileNetV2 fp32 71.878 → P4 int8 (per-channel + TQT) 70.441 (−1.44) — [how_to_deploy_mobilenetv2](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_deploy_mobilenetv2.html); TQT doc gives 71.775 for its own run (−0.10) vs 70.850 for PTQ per-channel — [TQT doc](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl/blob/master/docs/en/tutorials/quantize_model_with_TQT.rst)
- Size accounting facts: esp-dl stores quantised weights in `.espdl` (FlatBuffers) and `.info`, "weights and test input/output are 16-byte aligned, with zero padding"; scales are symmetric powers of two (so a scale is one small integer per channel/tensor, not an fp32 pair) — [how_to_quantize_model](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_quantize_model.html); `w8a16` keeps int8 weights with int16 activations, so weight storage is unchanged while activation precision doubles — [esp-dl README](https://github.com/espressif/esp-dl)
- The project's own measurement (from CLAUDE.md, this repo): Berlin champion 2.94 MB fp32 ONNX → 0.78 MB int8; `MODEL_MAX_BYTES = 4 * 1024 * 1024` weighs the fp32 ONNX — [CLAUDE.md, this repository](/Users/alexisrondeau/Workshop/low-light-geolocalization-autoresearch/CLAUDE.md)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic: 4 MiB fp32 = 1,048,576 parameters; an int8 `.espdl` is ~1 byte/parameter + per-channel scale bytes + graph metadata, so the same gate expressed in int8 is ~1 MiB and the fp32 ONNX overstates deployed size by ~3.8x (the champion's 2.94 → 0.78 ratio = 3.77, consistent with 4-byte → 1-byte weights plus small overhead). If the gate were re-expressed as "≤ 4 MiB *int8*" the parameter ceiling would be ~4M — MobileNetV2-class — which the P4 latency figure above shows is still well inside the fix schedule.
- Interaction with the project's known failure (memorising one photograph's pixel intensities): the corruption literature says int8 hurts *high-frequency* robustness (impulse noise) most and *brightness* least, and the CLIP study says QAT pushes models toward coarse texture. For cross-survey robustness that cuts both ways — the survey-to-survey differences that matter are low-frequency (illumination, colour balance, season), which int8 handles well; but a student already biased toward pixel-intensity shortcuts could lean harder on them after QAT (the +1.2-1.7 point spurious-correlation effect). The right experimental control is to score the *quantised* model on the fourth survey, not only the fp32 one; esp-ppq's PC-side evaluation makes that a few-minute step.
- QAT vs PTQ: for a 0.8M-parameter model the ~1-point PTQ loss seen at MobileNetV2 scale could be larger (fewer channels per layer means coarser per-channel scales); TQT (label-free, MSE to the float model, 800 steps) is the cheap middle path and does not require touching the training loop. Full QAT in the PyTorch training loop must use *power-of-two symmetric* fake-quant to match esp-dl — standard PyTorch affine QAT would not.

### Gaps
- No study measures int8's effect on *cross-season or cross-survey aerial-imagery* invariance, nor on a sub-1M-parameter CNN; the numbers above are ImageNet-C / Office-31 / CLIP proxies.
- No source quantifies the FlatBuffers/metadata overhead of `.espdl` beyond "16-byte aligned"; the ~3.8x ratio is the project's own measurement.
- Whether esp-ppq's QAT path can be driven from the project's existing training loop (fake-quant with power-of-two scales) was not verified against its source.
