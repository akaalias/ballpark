# Self-supervised and invariance-learning methods for multi-temporal aerial/satellite imagery of the same ground

Scope note for the report writer: everything below was read from the primary PDFs (SeCo, CACo, GASSL, SSL4EO-S12, MATTER, SatDINO, Burgert et al. 2026, Kinnari et al. 2022, LSVL, MTGL40-5, STAR/UniT-1652) except where a finding is marked as coming from an abstract page only. Table numbers were transcribed from the extracted text; where a table's column order was ambiguous after extraction I say so rather than guess.

## Key question 1 — Catalogue: for each method, what pairs are formed, what loss, what augmentation, what invariance was measured

### Takeaway
Every multi-temporal SSL method in the remote-sensing literature boils down to one mechanism: two acquisitions of the same pixel-aligned ground, taken on different dates, are declared a positive pair under an InfoNCE / MoCo-v2 (or DINO) objective, usually with the standard photometric+geometric augmentations applied on top. The refinements are (a) SeCo's three projection heads so that the backbone keeps a season-*variant* subspace too, (b) CACo's "push the long-term pair apart only if the ground actually changed" gate with a self-bootstrapped change estimate, (c) GASSL's extra geo-cluster classification head, (d) MATTER's patch-level (7×7 px receptive field) texture-residual consistency across dates. The MAE family (SatMAE, Prithvi) does not learn invariance at all: it reconstructs masked patches across time with a temporal embedding. All of the above used ResNet-18/34/50 or ViT and global pretraining; the only single-region, from-scratch, small-CNN instance is Kinnari et al.'s season-invariant UAV matcher (see Q4).

### Cited Findings

**Seasonal Contrast (SeCo), Mañas et al. 2021** — [arXiv 2103.16607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607), code [github.com/ElementAI/seasonal-contrast](https://github.com/ElementAI/seasonal-contrast)
- Data: ~200K locations sampled as Gaussians (σ = 50 km) around the 10k most populated cities; at each location 5 Sentinel-2 images "from different dates separated by approximately 3 months", dates jittered up to a year, tiles with >10% cloud rejected; patches of ~2.65 × 2.65 km; ~1M patches total. RGB bands only were used for training. — [SeCo §3.1, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- Pair construction: from one location draw 3 images at different times x_t0, x_t1, x_t2 at random. Query = x_t0 untouched. Key k0 = T(x_t1) (seasonal + artificial), key k1 = x_t2 (seasonal only), key k2 = T(x_t0) (artificial only), where T is the MoCo-v2 augmentation set. — [SeCo §3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- Loss: three separate 128-d MLP heads h0, h1, h2 on a shared backbone embedding V, each with its own InfoNCE (τ = 0.07) and its own 16,384-entry MoCo queue. Z0 is invariant to everything (positive k0). Z1 is invariant to season but variant to artificial augmentation (positive k1; k0 and k2 become extra hard negatives). Z2 is invariant to artificial augmentation but variant to season (positive k2; k0 and k1 extra hard negatives). Final loss = sum of the three. Transfer uses V, not any Zi. — [SeCo §3.2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- Augmentations T: color jittering, random grayscale, Gaussian blur, horizontal flip, random-resized crop (the MoCo-v2 set). Backbone ResNet-18 or ResNet-50; 200 epochs, batch 256, SGD momentum 0.9, wd 1e-4, lr 0.03 stepped ÷10 at 60%/80%. — [SeCo §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- Measured effect, BigEarthNet mAP, ResNet-18 pretrained on 1M images, linear probing with 10%/100% labels: MoCo-v2 69.28/70.79; MoCo-v2+TP (temporal positives only, Ayush-style) 72.58/73.60; SeCo 76.05/77.00. Fine-tuning 100% labels: 85.23 / 86.59 / 87.27. The paper's own wording: "temporal positives (TP) improve the performance of MoCo-v2 by a narrow margin". — [SeCo Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- EuroSAT, ResNet-18, 1M images (Table 3): random init 63.21, ImageNet 86.44, MoCo-v2 83.72, MoCo-v2+TP 89.51, SeCo 93.14. — [SeCo Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- OSCD change detection F1 (frozen ResNet-18 + U-Net on feature differences): MoCo-v2 40.71, MoCo-v2+TP 40.12, SeCo 46.94. The authors attribute MoCo-v2+TP's loss to its "representations being invariant to temporal variations, which is not a desirable property in a change detection task". — [SeCo Table 4 and §4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- Motivation sentence worth quoting for the augmentation question: "no amount of artificial augmentation can show how a snowy mountain summit looks like when the snow melts down, or how the different stages of a crop change through the seasons." — [SeCo §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)

**CACo (Change-Aware Contrastive), Mall, Hariharan, Bala, CVPR 2023** — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html), code [github.com/utkarshmall13/CACo](https://github.com/utkarshmall13/CACo), project page [research.cs.cornell.edu/caco](https://research.cs.cornell.edu/caco)
- Data: per location two *sets* of Sentinel-2 images around time points t1 and t2 that are 4 years apart; within each set images span at most 1 year ("only short-term or seasonal differences"). Each training iteration samples 4 images per location (2 short-term, 2 long-term), so a batch holds half as many locations as SeCo. Sampling σ = 5 km around cities (vs SeCo's 50 km) and ocean rejection; 100k and 1M image datasets, trained 1000 and 200 epochs respectively. — [CACo §3.3, §3.5, §4.1](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- Loss: built on SeCo's three subspaces and MoCo-v2 (batch 256, queue 16,384, same optimizer/lr/τ as SeCo). In the season-invariant subspace the positive stays the short-term pair; the negative set is extended to include the same location's images from the other time set (Eq. 2), i.e. long-term pairs are pushed apart. — [CACo §3.3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- Change gate: the long-term push is applied *only if* the location is estimated to have changed. Change estimate per location = ratio of feature distances, R = ‖f(x_t1+Δ1) − f(x_t2+Δ1)‖² / ‖f(x_t1+Δ1) − f(x_t1+Δ2)‖² (long-term distance divided by seasonal distance, computed with the momentum encoder), smoothed with an exponential moving average over epochs, then split into change / no-change by a 2-component GMM. The feature extractor is bootstrapped from random init, alternating "train with current change labels" and "re-estimate change". — [CACo §3.4, Eq. 3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- Why the ratio and not raw distance: "urban locations with less vegetation look the same over the seasons, but temperate forests will change a lot with the seasons", so the ratio normalises per-location scale. Ablation (EuroSAT, RN-18, 100k): raw long-term distance 91.17; ratio without EMA 91.98; seasonally-aligned long-term pairs 92.92; ratio+EMA 93.08. — [CACo Table 8](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- Component ablation (EuroSAT acc / DynamicEarthNet mIoU, RN-18, 100k): SeCo 90.05 / 46.83 → +improved sampling 91.42 / 47.24 → +long-term contrast 92.56 / 49.68 → +change-awareness 93.08 / 50.20. — [CACo Table 6](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- EuroSAT linear probe, RN-18, 100k: MoCo v2 87.22, GSSL 87.74, SeCo 90.05, CACo 93.08; OSCD F1 RN-18: MoCo 38.21, GSSL 44.06, SeCo 46.84, CACo 50.29. Works with SimCLR too (SeCo 87.97 / CACo 90.83 EuroSAT). — [CACo Tables 1, 3, 7](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- Short-term sudden change is rare enough to ignore: of the top 200 short-term change scores only 3 showed real sudden change. — [CACo §3.4](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- Licence of code/data is CC BY-NC 4.0 (project page). — [Search result summarising project page](https://research.cs.cornell.edu/caco)

**GASSL (Geography-Aware SSL), Ayush et al. 2021** — [arXiv 2011.09980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)
- Data: fMoW; number of temporal views per area T_i ranges 1–21, "on average there is about 2.5–3 years of difference between the images from an area"; the paper's Fig. 2 notes "changes in contrast, brightness, cloud cover etc." across those views. — [GASSL §3.1, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)
- Pair construction (TemporalInfoNCE, Eq. 2): pick x_t1, randomly pick another spatially aligned x_t2 of the same location, then apply the MoCo-v2 perturbations ("random color jittering, random horizontal flip, random grayscale conversion") to *both*; when t1 = t2 it degenerates to ordinary MoCo-v2. — [GASSL §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)
- Geo head: k-means over lat/lon into K = 100 clusters; cross-entropy on a linear layer over the query embedding; total loss αL_temporal + βL_geo with α = β = 1. ResNet-50, lr 1e-3, batch 256, queue 65,536, τ = 0.2, SGD, 200 epochs. — [GASSL §4.2–4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)
- Measured effect, fMoW top-1 (frozen / fine-tuned): MoCo-v2 60.69 / 64.34; +Geo 64.07 / 69.04; +TP 68.32 / 71.55; +Geo+TP 66.33 / 70.60; supervised from scratch 69.05. So temporal positives alone give +7.6 points frozen. xView AP50: MoCo 15.45 → TP 17.65; SpaceNet mIoU 78.05 → 78.48; NAIP land cover 55.18 → 57.10. — [GASSL Tables 1, 3, 4, 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)
- Stated invariance: "our objective in equation 2 enforces the representations to be invariant to changes over time ... for a change detection task ... can be ... undesirable". — [GASSL §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)

**SSL4EO-S12, Wang et al. 2023 (IEEE GRSM)** — [arXiv 2211.07044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044), code [github.com/zhu-xlab/SSL4EO-S12](https://github.com/zhu-xlab/SSL4EO-S12)
- Data: per location 4 images, one per season, from 30-day windows around Mar 20 / Jun 21 / Sep 22 / Dec 21 (2021, 2020 as buffer), <10% cloud, 2640 × 2640 m patches, overlap-filtered, 250k+ locations, ~1M S1/S2-L1C/S2-L2A triplets. — [SSL4EO-S12 §III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)
- Mechanism "RandomSeasonContrast": for MoCo and DINO "the input views are randomly picked from two seasons"; for MAE and data2vec "one random season is assigned for each patch". MoCo-v2 augmentations: RandomResizedCrop, RandomBrightness/Contrast, RandomGrayscale, RandomGaussianBlur, RandomHorizontalFlip, RandomSeasonContrast; SGD lr 0.03 cosine. — [SSL4EO-S12 §IV.A and Appendix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)
- The key ablation (ResNet-50 MoCo-v2, 50k-location subset, linear probing, BigEarthNet-10% / EuroSAT): **fixed** season (one season per patch throughout) 75.1 / 93.1; **random** (same randomly chosen season for both views) 76.7 / 94.0; **augment** (two views from two different seasons) 77.6 / 96.2. — [SSL4EO-S12 Table IX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)
- A second "natural augmentation" they test: Sentinel-2 L1C vs L2A (with/without atmospheric correction) — models pretrained on the same product level as the downstream task have "a slight edge (~1%)". — [SSL4EO-S12 §VI.A.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)
- Backbones ResNet-50 and ViT-S/16; 100-epoch pretraining takes 7–25 h on 4 A100s. — [SSL4EO-S12 Table II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)

**MATTER, Akiva, Purri, Leotta, CVPR 2022** — [arXiv 2112.01715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715)
- Data: Sentinel-2 over "regions with limited human development" chosen to cover several climates; up to 100 images per region; 27 regions, 1217 km², three years; 14,857 tiles of 1096² px; "little to no change is assumed between every consecutive pair of sampled images". — [MATTER §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715)
- Pairs: anchor x_a, positive x_p = the temporally succeeding image of the same region, negative x_n from a different region; all tiled into small patches with spatially aligned anchor/positive patches. Training patch size 7 × 7 px, i.e. the largest receptive field during training is 49 px, deliberately "to restrict the receptive field by removing features from the global spatial context, and to prevent the model from learning higher level features ineffective in describing surfaces". — [MATTER §3, §4.1, §5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715)
- Loss: NCE over *residual encodings* (Deep-TEN-style soft affinity to Υ = 64 learned cluster centres, patch-wise). Backbone ResNet-34 with a parameter-free Texture Refinement Network (pixel-adaptive convolution weighted by cosine similarity of centre pixel to neighbours ÷ local variance) inserted after layer 1; batch 32, lr 0.01. — [MATTER §3.1–3.2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715)
- Stated invariance: "illumination and viewing angle invariant material and texture representations". Effect on OSCD change detection: "surpassing self-supervised SeCo by 24.22%, and fine-tuned SeCo by 2.08%", converging in 30 epochs vs SeCo's 100. — [MATTER §1, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715)

**SatMAE, Cong et al. NeurIPS 2022** — [arXiv 2207.08051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08051) (abstract page only)
- Mechanism: masked autoencoder "with a temporal embedding along with independently masking image patches across time"; objective is MAE reconstruction, not invariance. Gains reported: up to 7% supervised, up to 14% transfer on land cover. Backbone size and dataset are not stated on the abstract page. — [SatMAE abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08051)

**Prithvi-100M, Jakubik et al. 2023** — [arXiv 2310.18660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18660) (abstract page only)
- "Transformer-based geospatial foundational model", "100 million parameter model", pretrained on >1 TB of Harmonized Landsat-Sentinel-2 imagery; multi-temporal only appears as downstream tasks (cloud-gap imputation, crop segmentation); no invariance claim. — [Prithvi abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18660)

**SatDINO, Straka & Gruber 2025** — [arXiv 2508.21402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21402), code [github.com/strakaj/SatDINO](https://github.com/strakaj/SatDINO)
- Mechanism tested: "temporal augmentation, where multiple images of the same area, taken at different times, are used as individual views ... two different images of the same area for global views and a third image for local views" inside DINO on fMoW-RGB. — [SatDINO §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21402)
- Result: kept default DINO augmentations and dropped temporal views, because "performance on downstream classification tasks consistently decreased when temporal augmentations were used". Numbers in Q2. — [SatDINO §5.1, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21402)

**Kinnari, Verdoja, Kyrki 2022 (RA-L), season-invariant UAV localisation** — [arXiv 2110.01967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967), model+data [irobotics.aalto.fi/sivl](https://irobotics.aalto.fi/sivl)
- Pairs: 18 areas with 3–15 satellite images each (Goforth & Lucey's released data plus Google Earth historical imagery "to include seasonal variation"); per epoch, per grid location: pick one random image of the area, crop a 96 × 96 m patch at 1 m/px with random yaw and small random translation = anchor; the *same crop parameters* applied to *another image of the same area* = positive; a random location/orientation in the same area = negative. 21,870 training locations, 1,392 test, 5,568 for the likelihood-calibration set. — [Kinnari §III.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- Loss: Siamese CNN (Zagoruyko & Komodakis-style two branches + decision MLP) outputting a similarity in [0,1], trained with binary cross-entropy (1 for anchor/positive, 0 for anchor/negative), Adam lr 1e-5, wd 1e-8, 1000 epochs. Input 96×96×3 per branch; conv stack 30×30×96 → 15×15×96 → 8×8×256 → 3×3×256, flattened 32768 → 512 → 1. — [Kinnari Fig. 2, §III.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- Augmentations (Albumentations): random flips and transposes; Gaussian noise, several blurs, sharpening, emboss, brightness, contrast and colour changes "to gain additional robustness to illumination changes and imaging noise"; small geometric transforms for orthoprojection error. — [Kinnari §III.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- Invariance measured: winter UAV-view vs summer map ("significant") and summer-vs-different-summer ("minor"), see Q4/Q5.

### Inferences
- The entire family is one line of code away from any supervised trainer: "sample the *view* of location p from a random acquisition each time p is drawn, and (optionally) add a consistency term between two acquisitions of p". SSL4EO's three-way ablation is exactly the ladder the loop would climb: fixed (one survey) → random (all surveys as independent images) → augment (explicit cross-survey pair).
- SeCo's multi-head design is a warning that pure invariance throws away a useful signal: for a model whose job is *position*, the analogue is to let a side head predict "which survey is this" from the shared features so the trunk is not forced to erase it, while the position head is trained on cross-survey-consistent features.
- CACo's ratio statistic (cross-survey distance ÷ within-survey-augmentation distance, EMA-smoothed, GMM-split) is directly reusable as a per-cell "this ground changed between surveys" detector with no labels, and could drive the confidence output or down-weight changed cells.
- MATTER's choice to cap the receptive field at 49 px is the strongest published mechanism for "keep texture/material, discard layout"; for memorisation of position the opposite bias (keep layout, discard texture/colour) is wanted, so MATTER is more useful as a negative example of what *not* to constrain.

### Gaps
- SatMAE's and Prithvi's backbone sizes, exact temporal sampling (number of dates, spacing) and any invariance measurement could not be confirmed from their abstract pages; the full PDFs were not fetched. No invariance measurement is claimed in either abstract.
- Scale-MAE and SatMAE++ were not researched; neither is multi-temporal by construction (scale/resolution rather than date), and no source was fetched for them.
- No BYOL or VICReg variant with temporal positives on satellite data was found in these sources; SSL4EO-S12 covers MoCo, DINO, MAE, data2vec only.
- Kinnari et al. do not report the parameter count of their Siamese CNN; from the layer list it is dominated by the 32768→512 linear layer (~16.8M weights), so it is not a 4 MiB model as published, but the convolutional trunk is tiny.

## Key question 2 — Evidence that temporal positives beat (or need) synthetic photometric augmentation; ablations

### Takeaway
Four ablations exist and they disagree in a pattern: temporal positives help a lot when they *replace* photometric augmentation (SatDINO: +3.8 kNN with no other augmentation; GASSL: +7.6 top-1 over MoCo-v2), help modestly when *added on top of* full augmentation (SeCo: +3.3 linear-probe mAP; SSL4EO: +0.9 BE / +2.2 EuroSAT over using the same dates as independent images), and can be neutral or slightly negative when the comparison is data-equalised and the task is fine-grained (Burgert 2026: mixed, −4.3 on EuroSAT in favour of SeCo but +3.4 on So2Sat-random against it; SatDINO: −1.2 kNN, −2.0 RESISC45 with default augmentation). Nobody found that temporal positives make photometric augmentation unnecessary; every positive result keeps both.

### Cited Findings
- SeCo, same data, same backbone (RN-18, 1M images), linear probe on BigEarthNet 100% labels: MoCo-v2 (artificial aug only) 70.79 → MoCo-v2+TP (temporal + artificial) 73.60 → SeCo 77.00. EuroSAT: 83.72 → 89.51 → 93.14. OSCD change F1: 40.71 → 40.12 → 46.94 (temporal invariance alone *hurts* change detection). — [SeCo Tables 1, 3, 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- GASSL, fMoW frozen-feature top-1, RN-50: MoCo-v2 (artificial aug only) 60.69 → MoCo-v2+TP (temporal positives with the same artificial aug applied to both) 68.32, i.e. +7.63 points, closing the gap to supervised (69.05) to under 1 point. — [GASSL Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)
- SSL4EO-S12, RN-50 MoCo-v2, 50k subset, linear probe BE-10% / EuroSAT: one fixed season per location 75.1 / 93.1; all four seasons used but both views from the same (random) season 76.7 / 94.0; two views from two different seasons 77.6 / 96.2. All three settings use the full photometric augmentation set. — [SSL4EO-S12 Table IX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)
- SatDINO (DINO, fMoW-RGB; columns kNN / top-1 / top-5 / RESISC45 / WHU-RS19 / SIRI-WHU), Table 6a:
  - no temporal views: default aug 68.89 / 71.22 / 91.87 / 82.17 / 97.69 / 87.55; **no aug at all 63.35 / 65.98 / 88.81 / 80.30 / 95.27 / 88.28**; soft (0.25×) 68.60 / 71.03 / 91.49 / 81.20 / 97.21 / 84.69; mid (0.75×) 68.96 / 71.28 / 91.80 / 82.44 / 97.09 / 87.92.
  - with temporal views: default 67.69 / 71.24 / 91.96 / 80.17 / 97.21 / 85.63; **no aug + temporal 67.19 / 69.85 / 91.15 / 80.10 / 96.00 / 86.93**; soft 67.19 / 70.96 / 91.60 / 79.32 / 97.33 / 83.23; mid 68.28 / 71.72 / 92.01 / 81.04 / 97.45 / 86.20.
  - Authors' conclusion: fMoW results "very similar, with temporal augmentations occasionally leading to slight improvements", but downstream classification "consistently decreased", so they excluded temporal views; a repeat run (Table 8c) "again confirmed that temporal augmentations do not improve performance". — [SatDINO Table 6a, §5.1, §5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21402)
- Burgert, Hackel, Rota, Demir 2026 (data-equalised re-test): "the setup of Wang et al. implicitly favors seasonal contrast: although both models are trained on the same number of locations, the seasonal contrast model sees four times more images". Equalised by treating all timestamps as independent images; ~62,500 locations × 4 timestamps; k-NN protocol. Table 5 (Seasonal Contrast vs No Seasonal Contrast): BEN-V2 57.64 vs 58.12; EuroSAT 86.22 vs 81.88; S4A-rand 64.42 vs 65.46; S4A-tiles 36.89 vs 37.01; So2Sat-rand 91.52 vs 94.90; So2Sat-block 75.69 vs 74.29. Authors: "Explicitly enforcing temporal contrast can overconstrain the model in some cases". — [arXiv 2601.02289 §4.4, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02289)
- Kinnari et al. ran the inverse ablation on a localisation task: the same network and the same photometric/geometric augmentations but with winter imagery removed from training ("Ours, w/o winter"). "Degradation of convergence and mean error performance on our model trained without winter data show the value of training on data that contains the expected variability." The same held for a retrained Samano-style embedding network. — [Kinnari §IV.B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- Kinnari's augmentation set was chosen explicitly for illumination/noise ("brightness, contrast and color changes to gain additional robustness to illumination changes"), i.e. photometric augmentation and real seasonal positives were used *together*; no ablation removing the photometric set is reported. — [Kinnari §III.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- SSL4EO-S12's "fixed" vs "random" rows separate "more dates as more data" (+1.6 / +0.9) from "dates as paired views" (+0.9 / +2.2 on top). — [SSL4EO-S12 Table IX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)

### Inferences
- The SatDINO "no aug" rows are the cleanest head-to-head available: real cross-date views alone recover ~70% of what the full DINO photometric stack gives on fMoW kNN (63.35 → 67.19 vs 68.89), and overshoot it on SIRI-WHU; with the full stack already present, adding temporal views is within noise or slightly negative on classification. Read with SeCo/GASSL, the consistent story is that temporal positives and photometric jitter are largely *substitutes* for the colour-pipeline/brightness part of the invariance and *complements* for the vegetation/shadow/season part.
- For this project's known failure (champion broke under +20 brightness on its own raster) the literature says photometric augmentation alone would fix the brightness symptom, but only real cross-survey pairs expose the model to leaf-off vs full-canopy and to a different orthophoto colour pipeline, which no jitter range synthesises.
- The Burgert and SatDINO negatives are both from global multi-class pretraining evaluated by kNN/linear probe on land-cover semantics; a memorisation model evaluated on *position* has a different objective, so their "over-constraining" concern is weaker here — the loop wants the representation of one place to be constant across surveys, which is precisely the constraint they found too strong for semantic transfer.

### Gaps
- No paper reports an ablation where the *held-out* evaluation acquisition is a different survey and the comparison is "photometric aug only" vs "temporal positives only" vs "both". All ablations above measure transfer to land-cover semantics or in-distribution retrieval, not cross-acquisition generalisation of a position regressor.
- SatDINO's reference [8] for the temporal-view scheme was not identified from the extracted text.

## Key question 3 — What do these methods do about the ground itself changing (construction, cars, leaf state)? Mechanisms that keep structural and discard transient features

### Takeaway
Three distinct mechanisms exist: (1) CACo detects per-location long-term change without labels (ratio of cross-date to seasonal feature distance, EMA, GMM) and *stops* enforcing invariance there, and separately treats short-term/seasonal differences as always-invariant; (2) SeCo keeps a season-variant subspace so change information survives in the trunk; (3) MATTER assumes unchanged ground by sampling rural regions and shrinks the receptive field so only material/texture, not layout, is compared. For transient objects (cars, leaf state) nobody models them explicitly; they are absorbed by declaring short-gap pairs positive. STAR (2026) adds structural (road/building semantic) affinities as a training-time regulariser for UAV retrieval, but its temporal benchmark is synthetic.

### Cited Findings
- CACo distinguishes "temporary, seasonal change" (invariance wanted) from "permanent, long-term change" (sensitivity wanted), with the long-term pair pushed apart "iff" the bootstrapped change estimate says the location changed; "in remote areas away from human activity, there may be no change even over several years. For such locations, pushing apart features of temporally distant image pairs may be counterproductive and destroy needed invariances." — [CACo §3.3–3.4](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- CACo's change estimate R normalises by the seasonal distance so that places whose seasonal appearance swings a lot (forests) are not mistaken for changed places; GMM over the EMA-smoothed ratio gives the two clusters, and Fig. 3 shows the high cluster corresponds to "major construction and landcover changes" and the low cluster to "only seasonal changes". — [CACo §3.4, Fig. 3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- SeCo: Z2 head is "invariant to artificial augmentations but variant to seasonal changes", so "the shared representation will contain both time-varying and invariant features". On OSCD the single-head temporal-invariant model (MoCo-v2+TP) scores F1 40.12 while SeCo scores 46.94, which the authors attribute to "disentangling image augmentations from temporal variations". — [SeCo §3.2, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- GASSL explicitly accepts that temporal invariance is undesirable for change tasks and argues it is harmless for classification/detection: "for image classification or object detection task, learning temporally invariant features should not degrade the downstream performance". It also notes that fMoW's cross-time views already carry "changes in contrast, brightness, cloud cover". — [GASSL §4.1, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)
- MATTER avoids the problem by data selection ("regions with limited human development", "little to no change is assumed between every consecutive pair") and by receptive-field constraint (7 × 7 training patches, max RF 49 px) so the compared quantity is surface material/texture rather than scene layout. — [MATTER §3, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715)
- Kinnari et al. observed that their similarity network's response peak is ~40 m wide in translation and "a few degrees in rotation", and that adjacent road intersections "produce high scores", i.e. the learned season-invariant feature is also partially layout-invariant; they attribute this to augmentation by small geometric transforms and alignment errors. Forest areas, where trees are geometrically distorted in the UAV orthoprojection, are where similarity is least reliable. — [Kinnari §IV.C, Fig. 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- STAR (Wang et al. 2026): "geographic structures, such as road layouts and building footprints, offer more stable cues" than appearance; a frozen LoveDA-pretrained semantic encoder produces structural responses used as pairwise affinities that regularise the retrieval embedding at training time only. From University-1652 to its temporally shifted variant, Sample4Geo drops 4.88 points UAV→Sat R@1 and STAR drops 1.64. — [STAR / UniT-1652](https://www.sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.26599/COMMTR.2026.9640056.pdf)
- Caveat on that benchmark: UniT-1652 is "a synthetic robustness extension of University-1652 that simulates severe winter conditions" via Albumentations snow/fog/brightness/contrast (50% unaltered, 25% light snow + brightness 1.5, 25% heavy snow + fog + brightness/contrast perturbation), applied to both views — it contains no real second acquisition. — [STAR §4.1](https://www.sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.26599/COMMTR.2026.9640056.pdf)

### Inferences
- CACo's gate is the only published label-free way to find "this cell changed between surveys", and it is cheap (one feature-distance ratio per training location, updated as a running average). In the Berlin setting with three surveys it would flag construction sites between 2022 and 2023/summer and could either (a) exclude those cells from the cross-survey consistency term or (b) lower the confidence target for those cells.
- Transient small objects (cars, leaf state) are never handled by a mechanism — every method relies on them averaging out under the positive-pair constraint. For a 128 px / 1 m crop that is plausible, since cars occupy a few pixels and leaf state is the single biggest between-survey difference the pairs are built to absorb.
- A structural prior (STAR's road/building affinities) is the only mechanism that names *layout* as the invariant; it needs a semantic model at training time, which is feasible on a laptop but not within the 4 MiB deployed model. It is training-time-only, so the size cap is not touched.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much of the between-survey appearance difference is leaf state vs shadow vs colour pipeline vs real change; the methods treat all short-gap difference as one bucket.
- No evidence on cross-survey pairs for very-high-resolution (≤1 m) orthophotos in a contrastive framework was found; all the pretraining papers use 10 m Sentinel-2 or ~0.3–2 m fMoW/NAIP, and the two ≤1 m UAV papers (Kinnari, LSVL) use Google Earth at 1 m/px.

## Key question 4 — Results with small CNNs or from-scratch training on a single city/region

### Takeaway
Only Kinnari et al. 2022 trains a small CNN from scratch on a handful of areas (18, each a 4.8 × 3 km orthoimage) with real multi-date positives and evaluates on an acquisition the training never saw; it localises to ~15–30 m after convergence and generalised to real autumn UAV flights against a summer map (26.5–30.6 m mean error after 2 km). SeCo and CACo report ResNet-18 throughout; every other method is ResNet-50 or larger and globally pretrained. No per-city memorisation result with a ≤4 MiB model exists in the sources.

### Cited Findings
- Kinnari et al.: Siamese branches with input 96 × 96 × 3 and feature maps 30×30×96 → 15×15×96 → 8×8×256 → 3×3×256, then 32768 → 512 → 1 with sigmoid; trained from scratch (no pretrained init is mentioned) with BCE for 1000 epochs. Inference 0.13 s per update for 1000 particles (batches of 100 patches) on a laptop RTX 3000; "the update can be run in real time, also on more resource constrained platforms". — [Kinnari Fig. 2, §III.C, §IV.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- Kinnari et al. training areas: 18 areas with 3–15 satellite images each at 1 m/px; evaluation orthoimages are 4800 × 2987 m at 1 m/px; MCL initialised with 1000 particles in a 100 × 100 m prior, image every ~100 m of travel. — [Kinnari §III.C, §IV.A–B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- Kinnari et al. real UAV flights (autumn: "deciduous trees are showing autumn colors" in dataset 1, "have dropped leaves" in 2 and 3) against a map "acquired during summer": mean error after 2 km of travel 26.5 m, 29.1 m, 30.6 m. — [Kinnari §IV.C, Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- SeCo's ResNet-18 vs ResNet-50 (1M images, BigEarthNet linear probe 100%): 77.00 vs 80.35; "a wider performance gap between ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 when linear probing than when fine-tuning". — [SeCo Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- CACo reports ResNet-18 as its primary setting (EuroSAT 93.08 linear, OSCD F1 50.29, DynamicEarthNet 50.20 mIoU with 100k images) and notes that CACo on 100k images matches SeCo on 1M. — [CACo Tables 1, 3, 4](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- LSVL (Kinnari et al. 2023 follow-up) is *not* small: ResNet-50 pretrained on Places365 + FC to D ∈ {8,16,32,128}, 57M trainable parameters (42M with capsule head); but it trains on only 9 regions (4–15 Google Earth images each, 100 × 100 m at 1 m/px, 45k samples per epoch, 500 epochs, triplet loss batch-all margin 0.2) and localises real flights from a 100 km² prior against a summer-2021 orthophoto. — [LSVL arXiv 2212.03581 §VI.C–D, §VII](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03581)
- LSVL's embedding-dimension finding: "low-dimensionality descriptors are an efficient way of expressing what is important for localization and dimensionality of description is not the main hindering factor" (D = 8 to 128 all converge; D affects error after convergence only marginally). — [LSVL §VII](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03581)
- Goforth & Lucey (ICRA 2019): CNN features "trained on readily available satellite data" to handle seasonal and viewpoint differences, <8 m average error on a 0.85 km flight at 0.2 km altitude; Kinnari et al. reuse its released dataset and add Google Earth historical images. — [CMU RI page](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/gps-denied-uav-localization-using-pre-existing-satellite-imagery); [Kinnari §III.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- MTGL40-5 (satellite-to-satellite, 40 port/airport sites, 0.5 m, five years in 2014–2022): the best of eight off-the-shelf backbones was DenseNet121; the authors note that same-year query/database pairs "may have identical pixels ... This can potentially lead to overfitting of the model", so training pairs are always cross-year. — [MTGL40-5, Remote Sens. 2023 §3.1, §4](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/17/4229)

### Inferences
- Kinnari's recipe is the closest existing template to this project's harness: known position and heading of every crop, same-crop-different-acquisition positives, 1 m/px, ~100 m crops, small from-scratch CNN, evaluation on an acquisition outside training. The differences are that it learns a pairwise *similarity* (needs the map at inference) rather than a direct position regressor, and it never reports a parameter count.
- The absence of any ≤4 MiB multi-temporal result means the loop would be producing a new data point, not reproducing one; the evidence that transfers is mechanism-level (pair construction, ablation direction), not magnitude-level.

### Gaps
- No source trains a scene-coordinate/absolute-position regressor on multiple surveys of one city and tests on a fourth; the one-city results found are all retrieval or pairwise matching.
- No MobileNet/ESP32-class model with temporal positives was found.
- Kinnari et al.'s Fig. 5 numbers (median errors per condition) are present in the PDF only as figure annotations and could not be reliably assigned to methods from the extracted text; the paper's prose claims (faster convergence, lower error than six baselines, degradation without winter data) are reported here instead.

## Key question 5 — How many acquisitions per place; is holding out one acquisition for evaluation in the literature; what seen-vs-unseen gap is reported

### Takeaway
Published counts range from 3 (Kinnari's minimum per area) through 4 (SSL4EO, one per season; CACo's 2+2 across a 4-year gap) and 5 (SeCo) to up to 100 (MATTER); none ablates the count. Holding out an entire acquisition for evaluation appears in Kinnari et al. (map = one summer orthoimage, queries from a second summer or a winter orthoimage; plus real autumn flights against a summer map) and in MTGL40-5 (query year and database year never seen together in training). MTGL40-5 is the only source that puts a number on the seen-vs-unseen-acquisition gap: DenseNet121 R@1 on training year-pairs 79.7–82.5% vs 59.0% on the validation year-pair and 55.8% on the test year-pair, a drop of 20.7–23.5 points even though training itself was cross-year.

### Cited Findings
- Acquisitions per location: SeCo 5 (≈3 months apart, within one year) — [SeCo §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607); SSL4EO-S12 4 (one per season of 2021) — [SSL4EO-S12 §III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044); CACo 2 sets 4 years apart, each spanning ≤1 year, 4 images drawn per iteration — [CACo §3.3, §4.1](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html); GASSL/fMoW 1–21 per area, mean spacing 2.5–3 years — [GASSL §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980); MATTER up to 100 per region over 3 years — [MATTER §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715); Kinnari 3–15 per area over 18 areas — [Kinnari §III.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967); LSVL 4–15 per region over 9 regions — [LSVL §VI.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03581); MTGL40-5 5 years per site, non-consecutive within 2014–2022 — [MTGL40-5 §3.1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/17/4229).
- SeCo says more dates are easy to add but did not test it: "we can easily gather more data (more locations, more dates per location). In this work, however, we limit the scale to a total of 1M images". — [SeCo §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)
- Kinnari's held-out-acquisition protocol: for each of two test sites, "One orthoimage acquired during summer was used as map. Another orthoimage acquired during summer was used for generating measurements for the minor seasonal appearance case and one orthoimage acquired during winter was used for generating measurements for the significant seasonal appearance case." 100 simulated flights per condition, random start and yaw, 100 updates. The calibration sets for p(c|s), p(c|u) "use satellite images from areas that were not used in training". — [Kinnari §IV.B, §III.D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- Kinnari's reported gap between seen-like and unseen conditions: no numeric seen/unseen table; qualitatively the winter-vs-summer case is where "major improvements with respect to reference methods" appear and where training without winter data degrades; real autumn flights converge after ~2 km with 26.5–30.6 m mean error, with "time to convergence ... longer than with the orthoimage experiment" and pre-convergence error exceeding the simulated 5–95% band. — [Kinnari abstract, §IV.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)
- MTGL40-5 year-pair split: years re-labelled 0–4; training on 0→1, 1→2, 2→0; validation on 4→3; test on 3→4. DenseNet121 R@1-Q / R@1-K: training 82.33/100, 82.45/97.50, 79.69/95.00; validation 58.99/92.50; test 55.76/87.50. The authors: "the R@1-Q/R@1-K of the validation set decrease 23.34%/7.5% (over 0→1), 23.46%/5% (over 1→2), and 20.70%/2.5% (over 2→0). This proves that it is hard for DenseNet121 to accurately capture time-invariant features ... resulting in overfitting on the training set." Within training, the widest gap (2→0) scored lowest. — [MTGL40-5 §4.4.2, Table 4](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/17/4229)
- MTGL40-5 per-site spread: "simple" sites reach >95% while "hard" sites fail, attributed to "the degree of land cover changes over long periods of time". — [MTGL40-5 §4.4.3](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/17/4229)
- LSVL evaluates against a map "constructed from aerial images taken over the operating area in summer 2021" while training only on Google Earth imagery of other Finnish regions, i.e. both the acquisition and the sensor/colour pipeline of the test map are unseen. — [LSVL §VI.C](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03581)
- SSL4EO-S12 "fixed" vs "random" row isolates the value of having 4 acquisitions vs 1 with no pairing: +1.6 BE-10% / +0.9 EuroSAT. — [SSL4EO-S12 Table IX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)

### Inferences
- With three training surveys this project sits at the published minimum (Kinnari's 3-image areas), and above SSL4EO's effective count once "two views from two different seasons" is the mechanism (any 2 of 3 surveys gives 3 distinct pairs per location per epoch).
- The MTGL40-5 numbers are the best available prior for what to expect: a model that sees *only* cross-year pairs in training still loses ~20 points of R@1 on an unseen year-pair; a model that sees same-year pairs overfits to identical pixels. Translated, the loop should expect the fourth survey (TrueDOP 2024) to score markedly below any within-training-survey diagnostic, and should treat a small gap as the real signal of structural learning rather than expecting parity.
- Kinnari's "significant" condition (winter query vs summer map) is a harder shift than Berlin's leaf-off-vs-spring, so the fact that a tiny from-scratch CNN handled it with 3–15 images per area is encouraging for the 3-survey case.

### Gaps
- No ablation over the number of acquisitions per location exists in any source; the only count-sensitive result is SSL4EO's 1-vs-4 comparison.
- No paper reports a seen-survey vs held-out-survey comparison for the *same* model in a dense-localisation (metres) setting; Kinnari reports held-out error but not a seen-survey counterpart, and MTGL40-5 reports the gap only for retrieval R@1.
- The Burgert 2026 and SatDINO negatives are not evaluated on held-out acquisitions at all, so they do not bear on cross-survey generalisation directly.
