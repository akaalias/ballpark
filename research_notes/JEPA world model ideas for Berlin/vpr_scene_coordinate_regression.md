# VPR, absolute pose regression and scene-coordinate regression: robustness to appearance change, and what has been achieved at few-million-parameter scale

Scope note for the report writer. Research date 2026-10-30. The target system is a ~3 MB from-scratch CNN (hard cap 4 MiB ONNX, int8 via esp-dl on ESP32-P4, 250 ms single-thread CPU proxy) that memorises one 7 x 7 km Berlin box from 1 m/px orthophotos in 128 x 128 crops, as a ~3,000-cell map-cell classifier with 3x3 belief pooling for confidence and a residual offset head. It trains on three pixel-aligned survey photographs and is scored on a fourth it never sees. Known failure: the previous champion memorised one photograph's pixel intensities and stayed ~97 % confident while wrong on a foreign photograph. Retrieval databases may not ship on the aircraft; retrieval-style *training* objectives are fine. Everything below is filtered through that lens. Where a cited number comes from a secondary table rather than the original paper, that is stated.

---

## Key question 1 — Scene-coordinate regression (SCR) and absolute pose regression (APR): what do they report about cross-condition generalisation, and which mechanisms helped?

### Takeaway
Plain SCR (ACE, GLACE) trained on one capture essentially does not survive a condition change: on Aachen Day-Night, ACE and GLACE score 0–1 % at night versus 7–9 % by day, and the ACE-G authors state outright that an SCR head "overfits" to the mapping views. Every method that fixed this did one of three things: (a) replaced the from-scratch feature extractor with a frozen, condition-robust one (DINOv2, DeDoDe) — the largest single lever, (b) trained the regressor on *mapping-vs-query splits* so it is optimised to bridge the view/condition gap rather than to reproduce the mapping images, or (c) added a global, pose-conditioned encoding with graph-based augmentation. No SCR/APR paper applies this to top-down aerial orthophotos; that is an open space.

### Cited Findings

**How badly plain SCR fails on condition change**
- ACE (CVPR 2023) is a scene-agnostic backbone (first 10 layers of the DSAC\* network, trained once on 100 ScanNet scenes, 11 MB) plus a scene-specific MLP head of 8 1x1-conv layers 512 wide; the per-scene map is ~4 MB in float16 (2.5 MB and 5.5 MB variants also tested). Training takes ~5 min (291 s on a V100). The paper "does not evaluate robustness to appearance change, such as lighting or seasonal variation." — [ACE, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14059)
- ACE's head-training augmentation is weak: brightness/contrast jitter of only 10 %, no colour jitter, rescaling 320–720 px, in-plane rotation up to 15°. The *backbone* pre-training used far stronger augmentation: 40 % brightness/contrast, 30 % saturation/hue, random rescale 240–960 px, homography warps from random 3D rotations up to 40°. — [ACE, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14059)
- On Aachen Day-Night, trained on 4,328 daytime images only: ACE (ensemble of 50, 205 MB map) 6.9 / 17.2 / 50.0 % by day and **0.0 / 1.0 / 5.1 % at night** (thresholds 0.25 m/2°, 0.5 m/5°, 5 m/10°); GLACE (27 MB) 8.6 / 20.8 / 64.0 day, **1.0 / 1.0 / 17.3 night**. — [R-SCoRe, CVPR 2025, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2)
- ACE-G (ICCV 2025) abstract: SCR "fails when query conditions differ because the regressor is trained to encode the training views and so overfits to them"; the introduction adds that ACE relies on shift invariance and "simple augmentation such as color jitter." Figure 1 shows ACE failing under changed lighting and changed objects while ACE-G's predictions remain sensible. — [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)
- HSCNet (hierarchical scene coordinate classification + regression, 179 MB on Aachen) trained with "no night-time training data": day 71.1 / 81.9 / 91.7 %, night 32.7 / 43.9 / 65.3 % (night thresholds looser: 0.5 m/2°, 1 m/5°, 5 m/10°). ESAC (50 experts) 42.6 % day vs 3.1 % night at the tightest threshold. — [HSCNet, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.06216)
- Bayesian PoseNet (APR, 2015): direct pose regression "struggles with views far from the training trajectories" per the ECCV 2020 multimodal-regression paper. — [Bui et al., ECCV 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.04807)

**Mechanism (a): a frozen condition-robust feature extractor under the scene-specific head**
- R-SCoRe local-encoding ablation on the strong-illumination Hyundai B1 scene (0.1 m/1°): ACE backbone 14.1 %, LoFTR features 29.4 %, **DeDoDe (MegaDepth-trained) features reduced to 128-D by PCA 57.7 %** — a 4x gain purely from swapping the frozen feature extractor. — [R-SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2)
- Full R-SCoRe on Aachen night (day-only training, 47 MB map): **64.3 / 89.8 / 96.9 %** vs ACE 0.0 / 1.0 / 5.1 — the authors call it ~10x more accurate than prior SCR at comparable map size. Adding MVS depth supervision: 66.3 % night, 79.0 % day. — [R-SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2)
- R-SCoRe's photometric augmentation is minimal — brightness/contrast factors uniform in [0.9, 1.1], rotation ±15°, resize 320–720 px; no hue, saturation, gamma or night-specific augmentation. Robustness is attributed to the DeDoDe features, the covisibility global encoding and multi-hypothesis decoding, not augmentation. — [R-SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2)
- ACE-G's "DINO-ACE" control (standard ACE training on a DINOv2 encoder) vs ACE on Indoor-6: 91 % vs 70 % within 5°/25 cm; on RIO10 (multi-week re-captures): median 83.8 cm vs 358.4 cm. The paper says DINO features "help considerably." — [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)
- A follow-up to R-SCoRe adds geometrically-consistent global descriptors and reports +1.1 % day / +6.1 % night at 0.25 m/2° over R-SCoRe. — [arXiv 2512.17226](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17226)

**Mechanism (b): train the regressor across a mapping/query split so it learns to bridge the gap**
- ACE-G pre-trains a scene-agnostic coordinate regressor (cross-attention ViT blocks + 2-layer MLP) over 240,000 mapping/query buffers from ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, MapFree, BlendedMVS and WildRGBD; in "query iterations" the per-scene map code is frozen and only the regressor is updated on frames from *disjoint* sequences, so it is trained "to bridge the gap between mapping views and unseen query views (different time, lighting, or viewpoint), instead of only memorizing the mapping images." The only scene-specific state is a map code of 4096 x 768 embeddings (~12 MB fp32; ACE's is 4 MB), optimised from scratch with the regressor frozen. — [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)
- Results: Indoor-6 ACE 70 % → ACE-G 95 % (5°/25 cm); RIO10 median 358.4 cm → 41.1 cm; but Cambridge Landmarks (single-condition, same-session) ACE 25.1 cm vs ACE-G 35.1 cm, i.e. the generalisation machinery costs some same-condition accuracy. — [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)
- Loss: Laplace negative log-likelihood log σ + √2‖ŷ − y‖/σ, so the regressor predicts a per-patch uncertainty that is used to pre-filter correspondences (p = 0.1, f = 2.0) before PnP+RANSAC. — [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)
- Pre-training cost: 4.4 M iterations on 8 A100s — far outside a one-hour laptop budget; only the idea (split mapping and query frames, update the shared part only on query frames) transfers. — [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)

**Mechanism (c): global encodings + graph-based augmentation**
- GLACE adds global encodings to ACE's local ones and argues that coarse-first pipelines (retrieve then regress) generalise poorly because "novel views of a point observed in training cannot be associated with it anymore." — [GLACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04340)
- R-SCoRe replaces GLACE's retrieval-based global feature with a 256-D Node2Vec encoding of the *training covisibility graph* (covisible images get similar codes), and augments by swapping an image's code with a random covisibility neighbour's with p = 0.5. Ablation (Hyundai 1F, 0.1 m/1°): base 34.1 → +multi-hypothesis 42.1 → +covisibility augmentation 62.0 → +covisibility encoding 72.3. At test time it retrieves 10 nearest training images with NetVLAD, runs PnP per hypothesis and keeps the one with most RANSAC inliers. — [R-SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2)
- R-SCoRe's depth-adjusted reprojection loss (errors normalised by predicted depth, Geman-McClure) raised 62.3 → 70.6 % on the same split; ground-truth depth 76.8 %. — [R-SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2)

**Multi-session data as a mechanism**
- 4Seasons benchmark: learned feature descriptors "significantly outperform classic methods under challenging conditions," SuperPoint+SuperGlue best overall; the dataset supplies cross-seasonal pixel-wise correspondences precisely so dense features can be trained across seasons. — [4Seasons, IJCV 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-024-02230-4)
- RIO10 (multi-week re-captures of the same rooms) is the one SCR benchmark close to "same ground, different photograph": ACE median 358 cm, ACE-G 41 cm. — [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)

**Aerial / top-down SCR**
- A search for scene-coordinate regression on top-down UAV orthophotos found no such paper. The closest, OrthoTrack (2026), is training-free: it matches keyframes against a public orthophoto and lifts correspondences via a surface model. — [OrthoTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25245v2)

### Inferences
- The Berlin champion is structurally an ACE-style head on a from-scratch backbone trained on one photograph with weak augmentation — exactly the configuration that scored 0 % at night on Aachen. Its 97 %-confident-while-wrong behaviour on a foreign photograph is the expected failure of that configuration, not an anomaly.
- The largest documented lever (4x in R-SCoRe's ablation, 70 → 91 % in ACE-G's control) is the *frozen feature extractor*, not augmentation or loss. The loop cannot ship DINOv2 or DeDoDe (size), but it can use them as teachers: the mechanism that transfers is "features under the head were trained on data with the variability the test will have" (see KQ2 for distillation into small CNNs).
- ACE-G's mapping/query split maps directly onto the three available Berlin surveys: hold one survey out as "query" during training, and update the shared trunk only on query-survey patches while the cell head is optimised on the mapping surveys. This is implementable at laptop scale; the expensive part of ACE-G (multi-dataset pre-training) is not what makes the split work.
- R-SCoRe's covisibility-graph augmentation has a cheap top-down analogue: the covisibility graph of a lattice is the neighbourhood graph, so "swap the global code with a neighbour's" is the same idea as the 3x3 belief pooling, moved from decode-time into the training signal.
- ACE's own numbers show the backbone (trained once with 40 % brightness/contrast, hue/saturation and 40° homographies) is where augmentation lives; the head gets only 10 % jitter. The current loop augments only with rotation (memory: known-limit-augmentation-and-scale), which is below even ACE's head setting.

### Gaps
- No SCR/APR paper reports same-date vs cross-date numbers on aerial orthophotos; all condition-change numbers are street-level or indoor.
- ACE-G gives no parameter counts for the regressor or encoder and no augmentation ablation; the isolated contribution of the mapping/query split (vs the DINOv2 encoder) is not separable from the published tables.
- R-SCoRe's per-component ablation is on an indoor split, not on Aachen night; the night-specific contribution of each component is not published.

## Key question 2 — VPR descriptors with condition invariance: objectives, data, small backbones, and distillation of foundation features into tiny CNNs

### Takeaway
Condition invariance in VPR comes from (1) training data that spans the conditions (MSLS, Nordland seasons, multi-date aerial imagery) under a metric or cell-classification loss, and (2) since 2023, from frozen DINOv2 features. At small scale the picture is sober: a MobileNetV2 trained on 1.6 M MSLS images reaches only 22–27 % R@1 on Nordland, int8 post-training quantisation costs ~7 points of R@1 on average, and no paper distils DINOv2 into a sub-4 MB CNN for VPR. Cross-architecture feature-MSE distillation of DINOv2 into a from-scratch ResNet-18 exists in robotics (X-Distill) and is the most directly reusable recipe.

### Cited Findings

**What gives invariance**
- CosPlace trains VPR as classification over geographic cells with a cosine-similarity classifier and large-margin softmax; EigenPlaces keeps the classification framing but builds classes from a shared focal point so that different viewpoints of the same place fall in one class, with lateral and frontal losses. — [EigenPlaces, ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Berton_EigenPlaces_Training_Viewpoint_Robust_Models_for_Visual_Place_Recognition_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- MixVPR reports 58.4 % R@1 on Nordland with under half the parameters of CosPlace and NetVLAD; the authors flag lightweight backbones as unexplored. — [MixVPR](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2303.02190)
- DINOv2 SALAD (fine-tuned DINOv2 + optimal-transport aggregation) reaches 76.0 % R@1 on Nordland and 75.0 % on MSLS Challenge and trains in under an hour; the paper credits AnyLoc with first using frozen DINOv2 for VPR. — [SALAD, CVPR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15937v2)
- A 2026 evaluation lists CosPlace and EigenPlaces at 512-D on ResNet-18 and 2048-D on ResNet-101 and reports that descriptor size and backbone depth have only a small effect for both methods (second-hand; the per-dataset table was not fetched). — [arXiv 2603.13917](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13917v1)

**Small backbones, measured**
- Grainge et al. (RA-L 2024) trained seven backbones on MSLS (>1.6 M images) and tested on Nordland, St Lucia, Pittsburgh30k. MobileNetV2 is ~14 MB fp32 / 6.8 MB fp16; ResNet-50 ~97 MB. MobileNetV2 1024-D fp16: GeM R@1 Pitts30k 72.12 %, **Nordland 22.22 %**, St Lucia 86.20 %; MAC: 73.27 / **27.16** / 85.52. — [Grainge et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09028v1)
- Same paper on quantisation: fp16 keeps recall and halves memory; **int8 post-training quantisation cuts memory 69.5 % and latency 62.7 % but lowers R@1 by 6.87 points on average**; GeM pooling "drops sharply" under int8, so MAC is recommended for int8; mixed precision collapses below ~10 average bits. Descriptor 512 → 4096-D raised Nordland R@1 by 7.4 points for 8x memory. Recommendation: prefer low-bit quantisation of a larger net over a smaller net. — [Grainge et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09028v1)
- HOGNet (2025, compact CNN with HOG supervision): a MobileNet baseline scores 22.99 R@1 in its table and HOGNet_441 reaches 67.7 % while cutting compute vs VGG16 (search snippet only; the article is behind a browser check and the dataset for those numbers was not confirmed). — [HOGNet, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13493866/)
- UltraVPR (RA-L 2025 / ICRA 2026): "lightweight, rotation-invariant" aerial VPR trained unsupervised on one-direction data, evaluated on VPAir and UAV-VisLoc; ONNX export provided; ~44 Hz TensorRT on Jetson Orin NX. Parameter count not stated in the sources reached. — [UltraVPR repo](https://github.com/cbbhuxx/UltraVPR)

**Distillation of foundation features into small CNNs**
- No paper was found that distils DINOv2 into a tiny CNN *for VPR*. The VPR distillation line is ViT-to-ViT or large-to-mid: D2-VPR distils DINOv2-base (CricaVPR) into a DINOv2-small student (~22 M params) in two stages. — [D2-VPR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12528v1)
- TeTRA-VPR (RA-L 2025) is a ternary-weight transformer trained by progressive distillation; third-party summaries claim ~78 % descriptor compression at ~94 % of full-precision performance (unverified). TAT-VPR uses a DINOv2-BoQ teacher for a ternary student. — [TeTRA-VPR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.02511); [TAT-VPR](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16447)
- TSCM distils a 194 M-param / 81.6 GFLOP teacher (ResNet + ViT + inter-transformer) into a 13 M / 3.4 GFLOP student (ResNet branch + one conv) with L_total = L_hard (triplet) + L_soft (student-teacher distance on anchor, positive and negative) + L_cm (cross terms: d(S(a), T(p)) + d(S(p), T(a))). Student R@1 0.735 vs teacher 0.730 on Pitts30k, 0.684 vs 0.680 on Pitts250k. No Nordland/MSLS numbers. — [TSCM](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01587)
- X-Distill (2026, robotics): DINOv2 ViT-L/14 teacher, **from-scratch ResNet-18 student**, plain MSE between teacher and student features with the student's last layer resized to the teacher's dimension; DINOv2-S and -L teachers gave little difference; the ResNet-18 student beat a same-size ViT student by 33.5 % on their visuomotor tasks. — [X-Distill](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11269)
- OmniDS distils two frozen teachers including a DINO encoder into a MobileNetV2 U-Net via weighted feature-level MSE with a higher weight on the DINO term. — [OmniDS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03038)
- A few-shot defect-segmentation paper reports that CNN students fit ViT/DINO teachers less well than ResNet teachers, yet DINO distillation still beat the ViT originals. — [arXiv 2502.01216](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01216)

**Self-supervised / JEPA-class ideas applied to localisation**
- No paper applying I-JEPA or V-JEPA to VPR or localisation was found. I-JEPA is "a non-generative approach" that predicts representations of masked regions in latent space. — [I-JEPA, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Assran_Self-Supervised_Learning_From_Images_With_a_Joint-Embedding_Predictive_Architecture_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- VG-SSL benchmarks self-supervised representation-learning methods for visual geo-localisation (closest match; details not fetched). — [VG-SSL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00090)
- TF-VPR learns place recognition self-supervised from temporal neighbourhoods and learnable feature neighbourhoods without ground-truth poses. — [TF-VPR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09315)

### Inferences
- The Nordland numbers (22–27 % for MobileNetV2 vs 58 % MixVPR vs 76 % DINOv2-SALAD) say that under a seasonal change, a small from-scratch-style CNN with a metric loss retains roughly a third of what a foundation-feature model retains. The Berlin loop should not expect invariance to emerge from a 3 MB network trained on three surveys with a cell loss alone.
- The reusable recipe for "feature-level supervision from a frozen robust backbone" at this scale is X-Distill's: run DINOv2 (small is enough) over the training crops once, cache the patch features, and add an MSE term from an intermediate layer of the ~3 MB CNN to those features. It is a from-scratch student, needs no DINOv2 at inference, and the cache makes it cheap on a laptop. TSCM's cross-term (student on anchor matched to teacher on positive) is the VPR-specific addition: match the student's feature for survey A's crop to the teacher's feature for survey B's crop of the same ground.
- Grainge's int8 finding (−6.9 R@1 on average; GeM collapses) means the loop's esp-dl int8 path will cost accuracy that the fp32 ONNX score does not see; the open "size gate measures the wrong artifact" finding in CLAUDE.md has an accuracy twin — the gate should eventually score the int8 model, and avoid GeM-style pooling.
- Trained-from-scratch classification-over-cells (CosPlace/EigenPlaces) is already what the champion does; the invariance those methods get comes from SF-XL / MSLS spanning conditions, which the three Berlin surveys partly supply. The missing ingredient is that the training surveys must be *treated as separate conditions* (cross-survey positives), not pooled as more samples of the same thing.

### Gaps
- No small-model (< 5 M params) VPR entry with Nordland/MSLS numbers beyond the MobileNetV2 baseline was found; VPR-Bench/MSLS leaderboards for sub-4 MB models were not reached.
- No DINOv2-to-tiny-CNN distillation for VPR exists in the literature found; the transfer of X-Distill's recipe to place memory is an inference, not a measured result.
- Grainge et al. give no parameter counts and only figure-based latencies.
- The JEPA line has not been applied to localisation in anything found; "world-model" framing is absent from the VPR/SCR literature reached.

## Key question 3 — Confidence and uncertainty that stay honest when the test photograph is foreign

### Takeaway
The best evidence (Zaffar et al., CVPR 2024) is that *learned* aleatoric uncertainty (BTL, STUN) transfers worst to a new reference map, a plain descriptor distance is better, and only geometric verification stays reliable under strong appearance change (Nordland AUC-PR: learned 0.05–0.07, L2 0.18, SUE 0.26, DELF-RANSAC 0.84). For a cell classifier without geometry, the honest signals are agreement across independent hypotheses (RANSAC inlier count in SCR, lowest-uncertainty-wins across models in Bayesian PoseNet, consistency of top-k) rather than the softmax of one head.

### Cited Findings
- Zaffar, Nan, Kooij (CVPR 2024), AUC-PR of uncertainty estimates for VPR match correctness: on Nordland (seasonal change) L2-distance 0.18, PA-score (1st/2nd NN ratio) 0.21, **BTL 0.07, STUN 0.05**, SUE 0.26, SIFT-RANSAC 0.15, Super-RANSAC 0.50, **DELF-RANSAC 0.84**; averaged over six datasets L2 0.69, BTL 0.28, STUN 0.54, SUE 0.77, DELF-RANSAC 0.93. The authors argue that aleatoric uncertainty learned from training data "does not necessarily generalize to the test reference map," and note that saturated queries get high L2 uncertainty because that saturation never appeared in the references. — [Zaffar et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00546)
- SUE: fit a weighted Gaussian over the *poses* of the K = 10 nearest references with weights decaying exponentially in feature distance; uncertainty = trace of the pose covariance; 1.08 ms. — [Zaffar et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00546)
- "Through the Lens of Doubt" (RA-L 2025): three training-free metrics on the similarity-score vector — Similarity Distribution (median top-k / best), Ratio Spread (mean of ranks 2..k over top), and their combination — evaluated across nine VPR methods and six datasets with AUC-PR. — [Miller et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13464v1)
- Bayesian PoseNet: MC dropout (p = 0.5) only after inception layer 9 and the FC layers (dropout everywhere cost ~10 %); ~40 samples suffice; uncertainty = trace of the covariance of a Gaussian fit to samples, normalised per scene via a Gamma CDF. Uncertainty is "strongly correlated with metric error" and roughly linear, with slopes that differ per scene. Scene detection by lowest normalised uncertainty across per-scene models: ~78 % on Cambridge Landmarks, ~52 % on 7-Scenes. Cost: 5.4 ms for 40 samples vs 5 ms deterministic, since only the last two FC layers are re-sampled. — [Kendall & Cipolla, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05909)
- Multimodal APR (ECCV 2020): mixture of Bingham (rotation) and Gaussian (translation) hypotheses; removing the most uncertain samples lowers overall error. — [Bui et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.04807)
- SCR confidence in practice is the RANSAC inlier count: ACE has "no separate confidence score"; its ensemble returns the sub-map pose with the most inliers; R-SCoRe keeps the hypothesis with most inliers among 10. ACE-G predicts a per-correspondence Laplace σ and drops low-confidence correspondences before PnP. — [ACE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14059); [R-SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2); [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)
- The Berlin detour already measured that decode-time consensus across four rotations of the same frame *raised* false fixes (3.75 → 4.25 %), i.e. rotation-averaged belief is not independent evidence (project record, CLAUDE.md Prignitz section; not an external source).

### Inferences
- The champion's ~97 % confidence on a foreign photograph is the Zaffar finding in miniature: its confidence head was trained on the same photograph as its features, so it learned "how sure I am on this photograph," which is the BTL/STUN failure (0.05–0.07 AUC-PR under condition change).
- The signals that survived condition change in the literature are all *agreement among independent pieces of evidence* (inliers, multi-model agreement, pose spread of top-k). The on-device analogues that need no database: (i) a lightweight spatial-consistency check — predict cells for several sub-crops of the frame and require that their predicted cells plus residual offsets be geometrically consistent (an inlier count without a map); (ii) MC-dropout on the last layers only (Bayesian PoseNet shows it is almost free at inference); (iii) train the confidence head only on the held-out "query" survey, never on a survey the features were fitted to, so it measures cross-photograph reliability.
- SUE's idea (spread of the *poses* of the top-k) is nearly what belief pooling does; the difference is that SUE uses pose spread as the uncertainty itself. A cell classifier can emit the spatial variance of its top-k cells as the abstention statistic instead of pooled mass.

### Gaps
- No paper measures confidence calibration of a cell/retrieval classifier specifically on a *foreign photograph of the same ground* (the Berlin setting); the closest proxies are Nordland (same track, different season) and RIO10.
- Bayesian PoseNet gives no abstention threshold; scene detection is argmin, not reject.
- No source was found on evidential/Dirichlet heads for localisation under condition change.

## Key question 4 — Classification-over-cells geolocalisation: tricks for a cell classifier to generalise

### Takeaway
PIGEON's evidence is the strongest: distance-aware (haversine) label smoothing is the single most important component in its ablation (removing it moves mean error 251 → 990 km and country accuracy 92 → 72 %), and the authors attribute it to every sample also training coarser granularities and to boundary samples getting similar targets across neighbouring cells. Hierarchical refinement and semantic cell shapes matter far less. HSCNet shows coarse-to-fine cell classification with per-pixel FiLM-style conditioning working as an SCR substitute at 73–179 MB, but all of these use large pretrained backbones.

### Cited Findings
- PIGEON (CVPR 2024) haversine smoothing: target for cell i is y_i = exp(−(Hav(g_i, x_n) − Hav(g_n, x_n)) / τ) with τ = 75 (PIGEON) / 65 (PIGEOTTO), over 2,203 / 2,076 geocells; the loss weights each cell's log-probability by this soft target. Cells are merged GADM admin-2 polygons (never across borders), split by OPTICS + Voronoi when oversized. Backbone: CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 px, encoder frozen for the linear head. — [PIGEON](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05845v6)
- Cumulative ablation (5,000 Street View locations; country acc / mean km / median km): full 91.96 / 251.6 / 44.35; − refinement 91.14 / 251.9 / 50.01; − contrastive pretraining 89.36 / 316.9 / 55.51; − semantic geocells 87.96 / 299.9 / 60.63; − multi-task heads 87.90 / 312.7 / 61.81; − panorama 74.74 / 877.4 / 131.1; **− haversine smoothing 72.12 / 990.0 / 148.0**. Within-1-km accuracy falls 5.36 % → 1.28 % when panorama and smoothing are removed. — [PIGEON](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05845v6)
- PIGEON's stated reasons smoothing helps: per-sample hierarchies ("the soft targets adapt to each location"), boundary samples get similar targets for neighbouring cells, "every sample also trains coarser granularities, so parameters are shared across implicit hierarchy levels," and the loss depends on distance to the true location without regressing coordinates directly. — [PIGEON](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05845v6)
- PIGEON's hierarchical refinement: OPTICS clusters inside each cell with mean CLIP embeddings as prototypes; top-K cells (5 / 40) re-weighted by a softmax over embedding distances (temperatures 1.6 / 0.6). — [PIGEON](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05845v6)
- PlaNet (ECCV 2016): subdivides the earth into "thousands of multi-scale geographic cells" trained on millions of geotagged images; in a 50-round human game it won 28 rounds with median error 1131.7 km vs 2320.75 km for humans. — [PlaNet, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05314)
- HSCNet: hierarchical k-means over the scene point cloud, two levels with branching factor 25 (625 fine clusters); cross-entropy at each level plus a heavily weighted Euclidean regression of offsets from fine-cluster centres; fine predictions are conditioned on coarse labels via per-pixel FiLM-like 1x1-conv generators; ground-truth label maps during training, own predictions at test. Compact variant 73 MB vs 165 MB with < 1 point loss. — [HSCNet](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.06216)
- GeoToken (2025) frames geolocalisation as hierarchical next-token prediction and lists PIGEON's distance-aware smoothing loss as the baseline to beat. — [GeoToken](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01082)

### Inferences
- The Berlin champion's belief pooling acts at *decode* time; PIGEON's result says the equivalent prior belongs in the *loss*: a soft target over the 3x3 (or Gaussian-in-metres) neighbourhood, with τ expressed in metres (e.g. ~1 cell), is the closest cheap analogue and is what "label smoothing across neighbours" means in a measured source.
- PIGEON's within-1-km collapse (5.36 → 1.28 %) when smoothing is removed is a fine-scale effect, i.e. smoothing improved the head's precision, not only its coarse accuracy — relevant to the residual offset head.
- HSCNet's coarse-conditioned fine classifier is a route to more cells (finer than 128 m) without a 3,000-way flat softmax growing, and its 73 MB "compact" result shows the hierarchy tolerates capacity cuts; a 2-level 25-branch tree of 625 cells per coarse region is a plausible small-model layout.
- Multi-scale cells (PlaNet) and feature-level mixup were not found with measured cross-photograph effects; they remain plausible but unevidenced for this problem.

### Gaps
- None of PlaNet/PIGEON/GeoToken test on a second photograph of the same ground; their generalisation is across *places* and *cameras* in web imagery, not across surveys of one box.
- No source measured feature-level mixup or multi-scale cell heads for cross-condition robustness in a cell classifier.
- All results use large pretrained backbones (CLIP ViT-L, Inception); nothing at the few-million-parameter scale.

## Key question 5 — Aerial/UAV absolute localisation against orthophotos evaluated across dates: the same-date vs cross-date gap and what closed it

### Takeaway
The only groups that evaluate across seasons on orthophotos (Aalto/Saab: Kinnari et al. 2022, LSVL 2023) report convergence to 12.6–18.7 m lateral error across winter-summer gaps, and in both cases the mechanism is the same: train the descriptor/similarity network on *multiple dates of the same ground* (4–15 images per region, Google Earth historical imagery) with same-location-different-date positives, plus strong photometric and small geometric augmentation; removing the winter data measurably degraded convergence. No paper reports a clean same-date vs cross-date number; the LASED benchmark (10 yearly orthophoto samples, 2011–2021) is the right data for it but publishes only aggregates.

### Cited Findings
- Kinnari et al. (RA-L 2022): Siamese CNN (Zagoruyko-Komodakis style) + decision network outputs a similarity in [0,1]; binary cross-entropy on anchor/positive/negative; **positive = same crop parameters on a different-date image of the same area**, negative = random location in the same area; 96 x 96 m patches with random yaw and small translation; data = Goforth & Lucey satellite sets plus Google Earth historical images, 18 areas with 3–15 images each; augmentations: flips/transposes, Gaussian noise, blur/sharpen/emboss, brightness/contrast/colour, small geometric warps "to tolerate orthoprojection errors." Real flights: 26.5 / 29.1 / 30.6 m mean error after ~2 km; **"w/o winter" training data degraded convergence and error**; similarity peak ~40 m wide; failures over forests (orthoprojection distortion) and low-texture terrain. — [Kinnari et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.01967)
- LSVL (RAS 2023): ResNet-50 (Places365-pretrained) + FCN or CapsNet projection to D ∈ {8..128}; triplet loss (batch-all, margin 0.2), positives = same location and orientation on a different image; training data = Google Earth images of 9 regions in southern Finland, **4–15 images per region at different times with seasonal variation**, 100 x 100 m at 1 m/px; localisation maps = Lantmäteriet 0.16 m/px summer-2021 orthophotos; augmentation: translation 0–35 m, rotation noise σ = 6°, Gaussian noise, motion blur, brightness/contrast, hue/saturation. Result: 12.6–18.7 m mean error after 23.2–44.4 updates from an uninformed start on maps up to 100 km², "under significant winter-summer appearance differences." FCN D = 128 Bayesian: 23.2 updates, 12.6 m; CapsNet D = 16 Bayesian: 35.2 updates, 12.8 m; abBRIEF baseline 4,112 m. No per-season-pairing retrieval breakdown. — [LSVL](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.03581v1)
- LASED (2025): ~1 M orthophoto crops from 170 k Estonian locations, yearly samples 2011–2021 (10 longitudinal samples), 10–40 cm/px, 400 m circles; test set in Valais (2017 and 2020 captures). Steerable ResNets with C4/C8 groups; multi-similarity loss; rotation augmentation + RandAugment. LASED-Test R@1: CosPlace 57.92, sresnet50c8 64.50; **without rotation augmentation 22.41 vs 64.50**. The authors credit the decade-long temporal span for robustness to "changing seasons, evolving infrastructure" but do not isolate it; no same-date vs cross-date split is reported. — [Papapetros et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15089)
- Multi-date satellite feature matching (155 diachronic pairs, Omaha): SuperPoint+LightGlue best for season-invariant correspondences; seasonal change, snow, clouds, shadows and land-cover change named as the obstacles. — [arXiv 2607.26973](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26973)
- DiffusionUavLoc names seasonal change a major source of domain shift and surveys generative pseudo-satellite synthesis as a bridge. — [DiffusionUavLoc](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.06422)
- A 2025 cross-domain remote-sensing localisation paper treats the temporal gap as one of three domain gaps (with viewpoint and modality), caused by seasonal vegetation, illumination and shadows. — [arXiv 2511.02489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02489)
- UAV-VisLoc provides drone images with lat/lon/alt and capture date paired with satellite maps. — [UAV-VisLoc](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11936)
- Semantic-map routes (SASGeo 2026) weight stable classes (roads, buildings) over unstable ones for GNSS-denied UAVs, but as a synthetic proof of concept, not a measured seasonal study. — [SASGeo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07737)

### Inferences
- Every aerial result that survives a date change was trained with *cross-date positives on the same ground*. The three Berlin surveys are exactly that resource; the champion has been using them as three copies of one label distribution. The minimal change with the most literature behind it: sample (anchor from survey A, positive from survey B at the same lattice cell), add a cross-survey consistency term (feature distance, or TSCM-style cross terms) next to the cell loss, and hold the fourth survey out as the scorer does.
- Both Finnish papers combine a ~40 m-wide similarity peak with a filter; the Berlin cell size (128 m) with residual offset is coarser than their peak width, so the reported 12–19 m is with temporal filtering, not single-shot — the single-frame number the Berlin scorer measures is not published anywhere found.
- LASED's 22 → 64 R@1 from rotation augmentation alone is a reminder that geometric augmentation is cheap and large; the Berlin loop already has rotation but no scale/translation jitter (the "continuous-vantage ±12 px jitter" from the Prignitz detour is the LSVL "translation 0–35 m" augmentation, untried).
- Photometric augmentation in these papers is broad (hue/saturation, blur, noise, emboss) — several times ACE's head setting and far beyond rotation-only. Given the project's own probe (+20 brightness alone pushed the champion from 0.040 to 1.453), this is the cheapest experiment with the clearest literature support.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a same-date vs cross-date single-frame accuracy pair on orthophotos; the gap the Berlin scorer measures (0.040 same-photograph vs 1.97 foreign) has no published counterpart.
- Parameter counts for the Kinnari/LSVL networks are either absent or ~42–57 M; nothing in the aerial cross-date literature is near 4 MB.
- "WildNav" and "SatLoc" by those names were not found in the search results reached; the Finnish line and LASED are the measured cross-season aerial sources.
