# Cross-survey latent prediction beats pixel memorisation

The one idea from the world-model family that transfers to a 4 MiB single-frame localiser is the training recipe, not the architecture: predict a *representation* of a related view (a different survey of the same ground, a crop one lattice step away, a masked block) from the current crop, against a stop-gradient or EMA target, with a normalised latent and a small discardable predictor — and never reconstruct pixels. Every mechanism the Berlin loop needs is deployable in that form because the teacher, predictor, projector and any VICReg expander are training-time only and the exported ONNX keeps just the encoder and the cell head. The literature is unanimous that the decisive lever for "same ground, different photograph" is *what is paired*: real cross-date positives of the same pixel-aligned ground beat any synthetic photometric augmentation for the seasonal and colour-pipeline part of the gap (GASSL +7.6 top-1, SSL4EO +0.9/+2.2 over using the same dates as independent images, Kinnari's small from-scratch CNN measurably degrading when winter imagery was withheld), while photometric jitter remains a necessary complement that no positive result drops. The single largest documented effect on cross-condition localisation is replacing a from-scratch feature extractor with a frozen condition-robust one (R-SCoRe's DeDoDe swap 14 → 58 %, ACE-G's DINO control 70 → 91 %), which at 0.8M parameters can only be had by cached distillation from the Apache-2.0 DINOv2-S — a 12.5-minute one-off pass per survey on the M1. What does not transfer is everything that needs a sequence or a decoder at inference (RSSM, KL balancing, imagination, MCTS, Genie/Cosmos/NWM pixel generation), Genie's latent-action discovery (actions are known exactly here), from-scratch SSL on a sub-1M CNN inside an hour, and any decode-time consensus (already refuted in-project). The evidence base is honest about its scale: no JEPA-style result exists below 6.5M parameters, no distillation study goes below 4M, and no paper trains a position regressor on several surveys of one city and scores it on a fourth — the loop would be producing the first such number. The ranked shortlist at the end puts cross-survey latent consistency first, broad photometric and geometric augmentation second, cached DINOv2 patch distillation third, and action-conditioned (translation/rotation) latent prediction fourth; masked cross-survey JEPA, a mapping/query split for the confidence head, and distance-aware soft targets follow.

## The champion failed the way the literature says a one-photograph SCR head fails

The current Berlin model is, structurally, an ACE-style scene-specific head on a from-scratch trunk: a four-layer stride-2 conv stack feeding a 2,970-way cell softmax and an offset head, trained with rotation as its only augmentation (`model/model.py`, `model/train.py` in this repository). That configuration has a published failure signature. ACE and GLACE trained on 4,328 daytime Aachen images score **0.0 / 1.0 / 5.1 %** and 1.0 / 1.0 / 17.3 % at night against 7–9 % by day, and the ACE-G authors state that an SCR head "overfits" to the mapping views because it is "trained to encode the training views" ([R-SCoRe Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2); [ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)). ACE's own head training uses only 10 % brightness/contrast jitter and ±15° rotation; the stronger augmentation (40 % brightness/contrast, 30 % hue/saturation, 40° homographies) lives in its backbone pre-training ([ACE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14059)). The Berlin champion's +20-brightness collapse (0.040 → 1.453) and its 1.87–1.97 scores on every other photograph of the same ground are the same phenomenon at 1 m/px: a head fitted to one raster's intensities with less augmentation than even ACE gives its head.

Two other lineages predicted the failure independently. In the world-model literature, a Dreamer latent trained to reconstruct pixels "fails completely" when task-irrelevant natural video is pasted into the background (Cheetah Run **30 ± 2** vs 349–444 for the reconstruction-free DreamerPro and TPC), because a latent trained to explain pixels explains the wrong thing ([DreamerPro](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14565v1)). In the cross-date aerial literature, MTGL40-5 reports that a DenseNet121 trained *only* on cross-year pairs still drops **20.7–23.5 points of R@1** on an unseen year-pair (79.7–82.5 % on training year-pairs, 59.0 % validation, 55.8 % test), and warns that same-year query/database pairs "may have identical pixels", leading to overfitting ([MTGL40-5](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/17/4229)). The baseline of the capture-holdout era — 1.651 on the held-out 2024 photograph, with the within-training diagnostic reading 0.015 on the survey the cosine schedule ended on — is a sharper version of that gap, and the lesson is that the fourth survey should be expected to score markedly below any training-survey diagnostic even after the fix; a *small* residual gap is the signal of structural learning, not parity.

The confidence failure has its own precedent. Zaffar et al. measured that *learned* aleatoric uncertainty generalises worst to a new reference map: on Nordland's seasonal shift the learned estimators BTL and STUN reach AUC-PR of **0.07 and 0.05**, a plain L2 descriptor distance 0.18, the pose-spread estimator SUE 0.26, and only geometric verification (DELF-RANSAC) 0.84 ([Zaffar et al., CVPR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00546)). A confidence trained on the same photograph as the features learns "how sure I am on this photograph", which is why the champion stayed ~97 % confident while wrong. Anything the loop does to the trunk must be paired with a confidence that is measured, or at least calibrated, on a survey the features never fitted.

## What transfers from JEPA and world models is a training-only recipe

Strip the family to its mechanisms and it converges on one loop: encode a view; predict, from that encoding plus an optional conditioning vector, the encoding of a *related* view; compare in latent space against a target that receives no gradient; keep the latent from collapsing. I-JEPA predicts target-encoder representations of masked blocks with squared L2, the target encoder being an EMA of the context encoder with momentum ramped **0.996 → 1.0**, through a narrow predictor fed a shared learnable mask token plus a positional embedding ([I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08243v3)). V-JEPA keeps the same structure with an L1 loss "found to be more stable" and argues that with an optimal predictor the encoder gradient equals the gradient of the median absolute deviation of the target, so the encoder is forced to retain target information ([V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08471v1)). The control-side camp reaches the same recipe from the other direction: TD-MPC2 trains its latent with a squared-L2 consistency loss against `sg(h(s'))` weighted **20** against 0.1 for the reward and value heads, uses no EMA at all (stop-gradient on the same encoder), and calls its SimNorm latent (groups of 8 passed through softmax) "essential to the training stability" ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)); EfficientZero's single biggest ablation is removing exactly this consistency term (Atari-100k median **1.090 → 0.340**) ([EfficientZero](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.00210v2)); MuZero states outright that there is "no direct constraint or requirement for the hidden state to capture all information necessary to reconstruct the original observation" ([MuZero](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.08265)).

The anti-collapse evidence is where the small-model relevance lies. SimSiam shows that removing the stop-gradient or the predictor collapses to chance (**67.7 → 0.1 %**), and that a per-channel std of the normalised output near 1/√d is the health check ([SimSiam](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.10566)). SPR — an action-conditioned latent predictor built on a 64×7×7 Atari conv encoder, the closest published analogue to the Berlin trunk's 160×8×8 map — found that an **un-normalised quadratic loss collapses to near-random** while cosine similarity through projection heads trains stably, needs no negatives, and works even with τ = 0 (no EMA) when augmentation is on ([SPR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929)). The capacity caveat comes from Li, Efros and Pathak: SimSiam on ResNet-18 partially collapses on ImageNet (**30.0 %** vs 68.1 % for ResNet-50) because capacity is small relative to data complexity, the collapse is invisible in the loss and must be read from the singular-value spectrum, width matters more than depth, and the same ResNet-18 is fine on CIFAR-10 ([arXiv 2209.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15007)). One city at 1 m/px with three surveys is closer to a small, low-complexity corpus than to ImageNet, and the Berlin trunk additionally carries a dense supervised head (the cell loss) that makes a constant latent useless — MuZero's and TD-MPC2's reason for not needing a decoder. The explicit-regulariser branch is the cheap insurance: VICReg's variance hinge and covariance penalty cost a few lines per branch and no second network ([VICReg](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04906v3)); REJEPA, the one JEPA trained on satellite data, bolted VICReg terms onto its pooled embedding ([REJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03169)).

Masking is the family's one mechanism with no two-view equivalent: it forces the latent to carry *spatial layout*, because the predictor must reconstruct a block's feature from its surroundings. The geometry transplants to a CNN. CNN-JEPA zeroes each conv layer's output under the upscaled mask so masked regions cannot leak through receptive fields, replaces masked locations with one learnable token, uses a 2–3-layer depthwise-separable predictor (deeper did not help), and found that random masking alone drops ImageNet-100 linear from **73.26 to 57.64** — multi-block masking is what makes it work ([CNN-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07514v1)). I-JEPA's own ablation is the same story (multi-block 54.2 vs random 17.6 on 1 % ImageNet). Two caveats apply at Berlin scale. First, CNN-JEPA on the same ResNet-50 ran *below* BYOL (73.3 vs 79.0) and VICReg (74.2): for a CNN the two-view objectives are at least as strong and cheaper, and masking's distinctive value is spatial structure, not raw accuracy. Second, the trunk's stride-16 output is an 8×8 grid, so I-JEPA's 0.15–0.2-scale target blocks are ~3×3 cells — coarse but workable; CNN-JEPA's 7×7-cell regime would need a stride-8 tap.

Action conditioning is the piece the Berlin harness is uniquely placed to use, because every training crop's position, heading and survey are known: translation, rotation and "change of survey" are free actions. Three published encodings all work at small scale. SPR appends the action as one-hot planes to every location of a two-layer 3×3 conv transition model; DINO-WM passes the action through an MLP to a **10-d embedding concatenated to every patch vector** and finds frame-level causal masking decisive (0.92 vs 0.08 success at history 3) ([DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2)); Navigation World Models map each scalar (translation, yaw, time shift) through sine-cosine features and a 2-layer MLP into one vector driving AdaLN ([NWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03572v1)). The evidence that this produces *structure* rather than appearance is indirect but pointed: DINO-WM's patch features beat every global-vector encoder (R3M, ResNet-18, DINO CLS) on every task, and training its pixel decoder jointly *hurt* planning (0.80 vs 0.92). The most relevant number is seq-JEPA's on ResNet-18: conditioning the predictor on the relative 3D rotation between views raised rotation-prediction R² from **0.29 to 0.71** with "no additional loss terms or equivariance-specific predictors", at essentially unchanged classification accuracy (86.05 → 87.41 %) ([seq-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03176v3)). A latent that can predict its neighbour's latent given the offset must encode where things are, which is the property "memorise the ground, not the raster" names.

The table below is the mechanism-by-mechanism transfer verdict, with the scale the evidence was obtained at. Everything in the "training-only" column is discarded at export.

## The pairing is the lever: three surveys are the invariance the fourth survey tests

Photometric invariance in the joint-embedding family comes almost entirely from what is declared a positive pair, not from the objective. BYOL's own ablation is the clean number: removing colour augmentation costs BYOL 72.5 → 63.4 but SimCLR 67.9 → 45.7, and crop-plus-flip alone leaves BYOL at 60.1 against SimCLR's 40.2 ([BYOL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733)); I-JEPA's official config sets `color_jitter_strength: 0.0` and no flips, so it encodes whatever its views share ([ijepa config](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ijepa/blob/main/configs/in1k_vith14_ep300.yaml)). The remote-sensing line turns this into a measured ladder. SSL4EO-S12's three-way ablation on ResNet-50 MoCo-v2 (BigEarthNet-10 % / EuroSAT linear probe) goes **75.1 / 93.1** with one fixed season per location, **76.7 / 94.0** when all four seasons are used but both views come from the same one, and **77.6 / 96.2** when the two views come from two different seasons — the second step isolates "dates as paired views" from "dates as more data" ([SSL4EO-S12 Table IX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07044)). GASSL on fMoW reports MoCo-v2 60.69 → **68.32** frozen top-1 from temporal positives alone, closing to within a point of supervised ([GASSL Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09980)). SeCo's ResNet-18 ladder is MoCo-v2 70.79 → +temporal positives 73.60 → SeCo 77.00 on BigEarthNet, and 83.72 → 89.51 → 93.14 on EuroSAT ([SeCo Tables 1, 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)). The capture-holdout baseline, which looped over the three photographs *sequentially* and fitted the last one, sat on the bottom rung of this ladder; the paired-survey interleave now in `model/train.py` (every position from every capture under one shared heading, shuffled into mixed mini-batches) climbs to the middle rung; an explicit cross-survey consistency term is the top rung and is what the SSL4EO "augment" row measures.

The only small from-scratch CNN in this literature confirms the direction on a localisation task rather than a land-cover probe. Kinnari et al. trained a Siamese CNN from scratch on 18 areas with 3–15 satellite images each at 1 m/px, positives being the *same crop parameters applied to a different-date image of the same area*, with flips, noise, blur, brightness/contrast/colour and small geometric warps on top; real autumn UAV flights against a summer map converged to **26.5–30.6 m** mean error, and training without the winter imagery measurably degraded convergence and error ([Kinnari et al., RA-L 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01967)). The follow-up LSVL localised from an uninformed start to 12.6–18.7 m "under significant winter-summer appearance differences" using 4–15 images per region, triplet loss, and translation 0–35 m, rotation σ = 6°, noise, blur, brightness/contrast and hue/saturation augmentation ([LSVL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03581)). Both are filtered, multi-frame results on a 57M or unstated-size network; the mechanism, not the magnitude, is what carries over. On the augmentation side, LASED's steerable ResNets on a decade of yearly Estonian orthophotos lose R@1 from **64.50 to 22.41** when rotation augmentation is removed — geometric augmentation is cheap and large ([Papapetros et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15089)).

Temporal positives and photometric jitter are substitutes for one part of the gap and complements for the other. SatDINO's no-augmentation rows are the cleanest head-to-head: real cross-date views alone recover fMoW kNN from **63.35 to 67.19** against 68.89 for the full DINO photometric stack, while adding temporal views on top of the full stack was neutral or slightly negative on downstream classification, which is why the authors dropped them ([SatDINO Table 6a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21402)). Burgert et al. re-tested SSL4EO with equalised image counts and found seasonal contrast "can overconstrain the model in some cases" (EuroSAT +4.3 for it, So2Sat-random −3.4 against) ([arXiv 2601.02289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02289)). Both negatives are about semantic transfer from global pretraining; a model whose job is *position* wants exactly the constraint they found too strong — the representation of one place should be constant across surveys. SeCo's motivating sentence states the complement precisely: "no amount of artificial augmentation can show how a snowy mountain summit looks like when the snow melts down" ([SeCo §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)); conversely no real pair spans the +20-brightness re-exposure, which is why every positive aerial result keeps both.

Two refinements guard against over-constraining. SeCo keeps three projection heads on a shared backbone — one invariant to everything, one invariant to season but *variant* to artificial augmentation, one the reverse — so the trunk is not forced to erase the season signal; the single-head temporal-invariant model scores F1 40.12 on change detection against SeCo's 46.94 ([SeCo §3.2, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16607)). For Berlin the analogue is a small side head predicting survey identity from a sub-vector while the cell head reads a survey-invariant sub-vector — SIE's invariant/equivariant split ([SIE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10283)) and TEM's structural-code/sensory-code factorisation, in which a path-integrating code "generalise[s] across different environments" while the sensory code remaps ([TEM](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/770495v2.full)), say the same thing. CACo adds a label-free change gate: the ratio of a location's cross-date feature distance to its within-date augmentation distance, EMA-smoothed and split by a two-component GMM, flags construction sites and switches the invariance term *off* there; ratio-plus-EMA beat raw distance 93.08 vs 91.17 on EuroSAT ([CACo Table 8](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Mall_Change-Aware_Sampling_and_Contrastive_Learning_for_Satellite_Images_CVPR_2023_paper.html)). Berlin's own capture statistics (vegetation fraction 1.3 % → 16.2 %, deep shadow 3 % → 19 % across the surveys, recorded in the project memory) are the kind of per-location swing the ratio normalises for.

The cell loss itself has one measured improvement. PIGEON's cumulative ablation makes distance-aware label smoothing the single most important component — removing it moves mean error **251 → 990 km** and within-1-km accuracy 5.36 → 1.28 % — because every sample also trains coarser granularities and boundary samples get similar targets across neighbouring cells ([PIGEON](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05845v6)). The Berlin trunk already uses a bilinear tent over the four nearest centres; a Gaussian-in-metres target over the 3×3 (or 5×5) block with τ on the order of one cell is the direct analogue, and it moves the belief-pooling prior from decode time into the loss. This is CLIP-ViT-L evidence on web imagery and a modest change, so it sits low in the shortlist, but it is one line.

## A permissively licensed teacher is the largest documented lever, and it fits the hour

The SCR literature's biggest single effect is swapping the feature extractor under the scene-specific head. R-SCoRe's local-encoding ablation on a strong-illumination scene goes ACE backbone **14.1 %** → LoFTR 29.4 % → DeDoDe-PCA **57.7 %** with everything else fixed, and the full system reaches 64.3 / 89.8 / 96.9 % at Aachen night against ACE's 0.0 / 1.0 / 5.1 with photometric augmentation of only ±10 % ([R-SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01421v2)); ACE-G's "DINO-ACE" control lifts Indoor-6 from 70 to 91 % and RIO10's multi-week re-capture median from 358 to 84 cm ([ACE-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11605)). The loop cannot ship DINOv2 (21M parameters for ViT-S), but the project's constraints explicitly allow a permissively licensed teacher at training time, and DINOv2 is the only large SSL teacher that is unambiguously permissive for both code and weights ("released under the Apache License 2.0"), relevant to RGB, and fast enough ([facebookresearch/dinov2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2)). I-JEPA's checkpoints are CC-BY-NC and ViT-H or larger ([ijepa LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/ijepa/main/LICENSE)); V-JEPA 2 is MIT but a video model whose smallest checkpoint is 80M ([facebookresearch/vjepa2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2)); ConvNeXt-V2 weights are CC-BY-NC ([ConvNeXt-V2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ConvNeXt-V2)); Prithvi-EO-2.0 is Apache-2.0 but expects six HLS bands at 30 m ([Prithvi-EO-2.0 card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M)) — the wrong scale and bands, and SEED's warning applies: a MoCo teacher at 60.6 % made a ResNet-18 student *worse* than from-scratch (52.1 vs 52.5) ([SEED](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731)).

The budget arithmetic was measured on this laptop: a ViT-S/14-sized network at 126 px runs ~206 img/s fp32 and ~292 img/s fp16 on the M1, so the ~218k training crops take **12.5 min per survey** once, ~38 min for all three, reusable across every subsequent experiment; a ViT-B pass is ~73 min per survey and only viable as a one-off; running any ViT teacher online per epoch is out of the hour (timing script in the research notes, random weights, PyTorch MPS). CompRess shows that caching teacher embeddings costs only −0.5 linear / −0.4 NN points for ~3× speed ([CompRess](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713)), while Beyer et al. show a *fixed single view* per image starts overfitting after ~600 epochs ([Beyer et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.05237)) — the compromise is to cache per (crop, survey), which gives three distinct teacher views of every ground patch for free, and let the student's own cheap augmentations (rotation, jitter, photometric) ride on top. Cache size is ~0.5 GB for pooled tokens or ~31 GB for full 9×9 patch grids at fp16; pooling the patch grid to 3×3 (~3.5 GB) is the loader-friendly middle.

Which distillation objective matters at this scale, and all the published evidence stops at 4M parameters. On ImageNet with ResNet-18 students, plain L2 feature regression barely beats from-scratch (SEED's ablation 55.3 vs 52.5; CompRess "Reg" 52.2), batch-normalising the targets lifts it to 58.2, relational objectives over a bank of anchors reach 57.9–65.6 (SEED, CompRess, BINGO), and DisCo's feature MSE with a widened student head plus an auxiliary contrastive loss reaches 60.6 — with **+3.5 points from widening the head 512 → 2048 alone**, the "distilling bottleneck" being the student's own projector, not the teacher ([DisCo](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.09124v7); [CompRess](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.14713)). For dense, localisable features the Proteus result is decisive: distilling DINOv2 with three MSE terms (CLS, pre-classifier feature, and iBOT-style masked *patch-token* prediction) gives 85.3 fine-grained average against 71.5–80.5 for logit KD, and the patch-level term is what moved ADE20K from 47.4 to 50.0 mIoU ([Proteus](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10366v2)). The robotics recipe X-Distill is the most directly reusable: DINOv2 teacher, **from-scratch ResNet-18 student**, plain MSE between features with the student's last map resized to the teacher's dimension, DINOv2-S and -L teachers giving "little difference" ([X-Distill](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11269)). TSCM supplies the VPR-specific cross term — match the student on the anchor to the teacher on the *positive* (`d(S(a), T(p)) + d(S(p), T(a))`) — which for Berlin reads: match the student's feature for survey A's crop to the cached teacher feature for survey B's crop of the same ground ([TSCM](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01587)). Under domain shift, distilled students retain cross-domain behaviour far better than pruning to the same size (Office-31 target accuracy 68.6–69.1 % vs 41.8–61.8 %) ([arXiv 2401.12014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.12014)).

Two numbers temper the expectation. From-scratch SSL on 4M-parameter CNNs is weak (EfficientNet-B0 42.2 %, MobileNet-v3-L 36.3 % ImageNet linear with MoCo-v2) and small models "saturate ... bottlenecked by the model capacities" ([SEED](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.04731); [TinyViT](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.10666)); at 0.8M the student will absorb a fraction of DINOv2's geometry, and the measured gain must come from the fourth-survey score, not from any published curve. And the deployed artifact is int8: Grainge et al. found int8 post-training quantisation of VPR backbones lowers R@1 by **6.87 points on average** with GeM pooling dropping sharply, recommending MAC pooling and low-bit quantisation of a larger net over a smaller net ([Grainge et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09028v1)); the corruption literature says int8 hurts high-frequency robustness most and brightness least (~10 % drop) and nudges models toward coarse texture ([arXiv 2304.03968](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03968); [arXiv 2509.21173](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21173v2)). esp-ppq's PC-side quantised evaluation matches the board bit-for-bit, so scoring the int8 export on the fourth survey is a few-minute harness step that would close the open "size gate measures the wrong artifact" finding on its accuracy side as well ([esp-dl quantisation guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_quantize_model.html)).

## What does not transfer, stated explicitly

Anything that needs a sequence or a posterior at inference stays out: the RSSM's deterministic/stochastic split, KL balancing and free bits (they regularise a posterior-vs-prior pair a single-frame encoder does not have), imagination rollouts, MuZero's MCTS, TD-MPC's MPPI planning, and the Neural Map / CMP / Memory Maze family of map states, all of which localise only by integrating known or estimated egomotion over time ([PlaNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04551); [DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04104v2); [Neural Map](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.08360); [Memory Maze](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13383)). Dreamer's reconstruction term is not a necessity to import: its "no image gradients → 0.04" ablation is specific to RL, where reward is the only other signal ([DreamerV2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02193)), and the decoder-free camp plus DreamerPro show it is replaceable whenever a dense target exists — here the cell loss. Genie, Genie 2, Cosmos and NWM are pixel or VAE-latent generators at 1–14 B parameters whose only size-independent piece is the sin-cos action encoding ([Genie](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391v1); [Cosmos](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03575v1)); Genie's latent-action model solves unknown actions, and the harness knows every action exactly. Predictive coding's local Hebbian rules reduce to backprop for a feed-forward CNN ("predictive coding with fixed predictions and identity precisions ... can converge to the exact gradients computed by ... backpropagation") ([Millidge et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.12979v4)), and HTM's bursting-as-anomaly cannot be read from one frame with nothing to compare a prediction against.

Several things *within* the transferable set also do not transfer at this scale or budget. From-scratch SSL on the 0.8M trunk inside an hour is not credible: the CIFAR-scale recipes that work on ResNet-18 need ~1000 epochs, LARS and 4096-wide heads ([solo-learn](https://github.com/vturrisi/solo-learn); [BYOL](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.07733)), and the 4M-parameter from-scratch numbers are 36–47 %. SimSiam specifically is the wrong choice for a tiny model on a large single-domain corpus, since its collapse is worst exactly when "the model is too small relative to the data" ([arXiv 2209.15007](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.15007)). An un-normalised L2 latent-prediction loss on the trunk output is a known collapse path ([SPR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929)). MATTER's receptive-field cap at 49 px is the wrong bias — it keeps texture and discards layout, the opposite of what position memorisation wants ([MATTER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01715)). Decode-time consensus is already refuted in-project (rotation averaging raised rural false fixes 3.75 → 4.25 %), and the world-model literature agrees that structure must be taught during training. Learned aleatoric confidence (BTL/STUN) is the confidence estimator that generalises *worst* across a map change ([Zaffar et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00546)). And ACE-G's 4.4M-iteration, 8-A100 pre-training is not the part that works; only its mapping/query split is.

The evidence gaps are as important as the verdicts. No primary source reports a JEPA-style or latent-prediction result below ~6.5M parameters (SCOTT-3/16) or on a mobile-class CNN; CNN-JEPA has no ResNet-18 or smaller result ([SCOTT/MIM-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18056v1); [CNN-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07514v1)). No distillation study goes below EfficientNet-B0's 4.0M. No paper applies JEPA, a world model, or any latent-prediction method to sub-metre orthophotos, to multi-year surveys of one city, or to absolute localisation; the remote-sensing JEPAs (REJEPA, Sat-JEPA-Diff, HQ-JEPA) are ViT-B on 10 m Sentinel data. No SCR/APR paper reports same-date vs cross-date numbers on aerial imagery, and no source measures int8's effect on cross-survey invariance. The loop's fourth-survey score would be the first such measurement at every one of these intersections, which is a reason to instrument each experiment with the collapse monitors (per-channel std of the normalised pooled feature, effective rank of the feature spectrum) and the +20-brightness probe, since the published failure modes are invisible in the training loss.

## Ranked shortlist for the design agent

Each entry is one focused change to `model/model.py` / `model/train.py`, with the expected direction on the two product rates on the held-out 2024 survey and the evidence level behind it. "Published" means a measured result in a cited source; "scale" is the model the result was obtained at; "extrapolated" means the mechanism is published but at a scale or domain other than a 0.8M CNN on orthophotos. The harness already extracts every lattice position from every training capture under one shared heading into the same chunk, so cross-survey pairs cost nothing extra; nothing below changes the exported graph except where stated.

| Rank | Experiment (one change) | Expected on usable-fix rate | Expected on false-fix rate | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cross-survey latent consistency: cosine loss between projected trunk features of the same position in survey A and `sg(·)` of survey B, VICReg variance/covariance on each branch | Up, largest single effect from the data already in hand | Down (features no longer key on one raster's intensities) | Published at RN-18/50 on satellite (SeCo, GASSL, SSL4EO) and small CNN on 1 m/px localisation (Kinnari); extrapolated to 0.8M |
| 2 | Broad photometric + geometric augmentation (brightness, contrast, gamma, hue/saturation, blur, noise; ±12 px translation jitter) | Up | Down on the re-exposure part of the gap | Published (ACE backbone, Kinnari, LSVL, LASED rotation 22 → 64); project's own +20-brightness probe |
| 3 | Cached DINOv2-S patch-feature distillation with a cross-survey term: MSE from the 8×8 trunk map (via a wide training-only head) to the 9×9 teacher grid of the *other* survey's crop | Up, potentially the largest effect overall | Down | Published at ViT-Ti/RN-18 (Proteus, X-Distill, DisCo); R-SCoRe/ACE-G for the frozen-extractor effect on cross-condition SCR; extrapolated to 0.8M; Apache-2.0 teacher |
| 4 | Translation/rotation as an action: predict the neighbour crop's projected latent from the current latent plus sin-cos (dx, dy, dθ) through a 2-layer transition head, stop-gradient target | Up modestly; mainly forces layout encoding | Down on look-alike confusions, since the latent must carry offset-predictive structure | Published at RN-18 (seq-JEPA R² 0.29 → 0.71) and Atari CNN (SPR); extrapolated to orthophotos |
| 5 | Cross-survey masked latent prediction (CNN-JEPA form): context = survey A with 4 masked 2–3-cell blocks, target = LN'd EMA-teacher features of survey B, L1 on masked cells | Up if it trains; forces spatial layout | Down | Published at RN-50 only; no CNN < 6.5M; highest implementation risk |
| 6 | Mapping/query split for confidence: train the trunk and cell head on all three surveys, but fit the confidence calibration (temperature or a small head) only on one rotated-out training survey | Flat to slightly down | Down, directly | Published at large scale (ACE-G split; Zaffar on learned-confidence failure); extrapolated |
| 7 | Distance-aware soft targets: Gaussian-in-metres over the 3×3 or 5×5 block with τ ≈ one cell, replacing the bilinear tent | Up slightly (sub-cell precision) | Flat | Published on CLIP ViT-L (PIGEON); extrapolated |
| 8 | SeCo-style split: survey-identity side head on a 16–32-d sub-vector, cell head on the remainder | Flat to up | Down if plain invariance over-constrains | Published at RN-18 (+3.4 BigEarthNet by difference, not a controlled ablation) |
| 9 | CACo change gate: EMA ratio of cross-survey to within-survey feature distance per position, GMM split, switch the consistency term off on changed cells | Flat | Down on construction-site cells | Published at RN-18 (93.08 vs 91.17 EuroSAT); extrapolated |

Experiment 1 is the top rung of the ladder the SSL4EO ablation measured, and it is the mechanism every cross-date aerial result that works was trained with; it should be run with SPR's and TD-MPC2's guard rails (cosine through a projector, stop-gradient, a normalised latent) because SPR's quadratic variant collapsed on a trunk of this shape, and with the VICReg variance hinge because the collapse paper shows partial collapse hides in the loss. The weight relative to the cell loss is genuinely unknown — SPR used λ = 2 against its RL loss while TD-MPC2 weighted consistency 200× its cross-entropy heads — so a value of order one against the current cross-entropy is the honest starting point, and the standing project check applies: if the mission score improves while `usable_fix_rate` does not, the term is doing something other than what it claims. Experiment 2 is cheaper and targets the measured symptom directly; it ranks second only because it cannot synthesise leaf-off versus full canopy or a different orthophoto colour pipeline, which is the part of the gap the fourth survey carries most. Nothing in the literature suggests the two interfere, and every positive aerial result combines them, so the design agent can reasonably run 2 then 1, or 1 then 2, as consecutive single changes.

Experiment 3 is the largest documented effect in the SCR literature and the most expensive to set up once (the ~38-minute cache), after which it costs nothing per experiment. Its unknowns are honest: whether a 0.8M trunk absorbs enough of DINOv2's geometry to matter, which no study has measured below 4M, and whether the 14 m teacher patch is the right granularity for a 128 m cell head. The design should follow Proteus and X-Distill (feature MSE, patch-level, from-scratch student), widen the training-only head per DisCo, and add TSCM's cross term so the student's survey-A feature is matched to the teacher's survey-B feature — that is the invariance the fourth survey tests, written into the target. Experiment 4 is the mechanism the harness is uniquely placed to run and the one the world-model lineage actually contributes: a transition head conditioned on a known metric offset makes the latent equivariant to translation without an equivariance loss, and it is a direct training-time replacement for the decode-time consensus already refuted. Experiment 5 is the only item that would make the loop "do JEPA" in the masked sense; it carries the most risk at this scale and its distinctive value (spatial layout) is partly delivered by 4 at lower risk, so it belongs after 4.

Experiments 6–9 are smaller or conditional. The confidence split (6) is the direct answer to the ~97 %-confident-while-wrong failure and to Zaffar's measurement that learned confidence generalises worst; it costs a third of the training surveys for calibration, which is why it is not first. Distance-aware targets (7) and the SeCo split (8) are each one line and worth running when the loop needs a cheap, well-evidenced step; the CACo gate (9) only matters once a consistency term exists for it to gate. One harness step sits outside the ranking but should precede any champion claim: score the esp-ppq int8 export on the fourth survey, because int8 costs VPR backbones ~7 points of recall and can push a model toward exactly the coarse-texture shortcuts this era is trying to unlearn.

## Conclusion

The world-model literature does not hand the Berlin loop an architecture; it hands it a discipline — predict representations of related views, never pixels, against targets that do not move, in a latent that cannot collapse — and the Berlin harness happens to hold the richest version of "related view" any of these papers had: three photographs of the same pixel-aligned ground, with every crop's position and heading known. That turns the family's expensive parts (EMA teachers, masked predictors, action-conditioned dynamics) into training-time scaffolding that never touches the 4 MiB export, and it reframes the capture-holdout era's question from "which SSL method" to "which pairs, which targets, and which signal the confidence is allowed to see".

What changes going forward is the expectation. Every cross-date aerial result that survived a season change was trained on cross-date positives and still lost ground on an unseen acquisition; the fourth survey will score below the training-survey diagnostics after the fix, and a narrowing gap — measured alongside the effective rank of the features and the +20-brightness probe — is the evidence of structural learning. The one lever large enough to change the magnitude rather than the direction is the frozen-extractor effect that R-SCoRe and ACE-G measured, which at this scale can only be borrowed through a cached, permissively licensed teacher; whether a 0.8M CNN can borrow enough of it is the number no paper has, and the loop is the instrument that can produce it.
